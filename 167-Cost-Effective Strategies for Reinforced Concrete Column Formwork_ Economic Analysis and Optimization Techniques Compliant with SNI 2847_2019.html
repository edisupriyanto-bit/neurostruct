Abstract (English Version)

Formwork constitutes 40–60% of concrete work costs and approximately 10% of total building construction expenses. This paper conducts a comprehensive economic analysis and optimization of cost-effective strategies for reinforced concrete column formwork in full compliance with Indonesian National Standard SNI 2847:2019 (Persyaratan Beton Struktural untuk Bangunan Gedung), which modifies ACI 318M-14 provisions. Supporting standards include SNI 1727:2020 for minimum design loads and SNI 1726:2019 for earthquake-resistant design, with emphasis on precise reinforcement placement and confinement detailing.

The study compares conventional timber/plywood formwork with semi-system and full aluminum system methods, evaluating initial costs, reuse cycles, labor requirements, cycle times, and total ownership costs. Key topics include lateral pressure calculations for safe and economical design, material selection, erection procedures, quality control, and stripping practices. Particular focus is given to tropical coastal projects in Bali, where humidity and seismic demands influence material durability and construction efficiency.

Drawing from ACI 347 Guide to Formwork for Concrete and Indonesian case studies, the paper demonstrates potential cost savings of up to 37% for column formwork when adopting semi-system methods, with aluminum systems offering further advantages in high-reuse scenarios.

3. Lateral Pressure Calculations for Economical Design

Safe formwork design prevents over-design (wasteful) or under-design (risky). Lateral pressure from fresh concrete is critical.

Simplified formula (adapted from ACI 347 for columns):

p = C_w × C_c × [150 + 9000 × R / (T + 18)] (kPa)

Where:

- C_w = 1.0 (normal weight concrete)

- C_c = 1.0 (Type I cement)

- R = placement rate (m/h)

- T = concrete temperature (°C)

Limits: p ≤ 150 × h (hydrostatic) or manufacturer maxima; minimum often 600 C_w equivalent.

Copy-paste ready example:

For a 4 m column, R = 2 m/h, T = 30°C (Bali typical):

p ≈ 150 + 9000 × 2 / (30 + 18) = 150 + 18000 / 48 ≈ 150 + 375 = 525 kPa

Apply p_max = 150 × 4 = 600 kPa → design ties/walers using 525 kPa with safety factor 1.5–2.0.

This balanced approach avoids excessive material use while ensuring safety.

4. Cost Comparison of Formwork Methods

# 4.1 Conventional Timber/Plywood

- Low initial cost but limited reuse (20–50 cycles).

- High labor and waste.

- Suitable for irregular or small projects.

# 4.2 Semi-System Formwork (Recommended for Cost Savings)

- Hybrid modular panels with adjustable components.

- Studies show 37% cost reduction for columns and up to 47% for beams compared to conventional.

# 4.3 Aluminum System Formwork

- High initial investment but excellent for repetitive use (200–300+ cycles).

- Lightweight, fast erection, superior finish, and overall economy in multi-story or large projects.

5. Implementation Techniques for Cost Optimization

- Select system based on project scale and reuse potential.

- Standardize column sizes to maximize panel reuse.

- Pre-assemble panels and use quick clamps for faster erection.

- Integrate with seismic detailing early to avoid rework.

- Perform value engineering to balance initial vs lifecycle costs.

Quality control (cover spacers, alignment, curing) prevents expensive repairs. In Bali, moist curing and protective measures extend formwork life.
