Abstract

The rapid expansion of the hospitality sector in Bali has introduced significant complexities in project delivery. Divergent expectations regarding scope, technical quality, and scheduling often precipitate construction disputes, leading to stalled projects and capital loss. This paper investigates the efficacy of a Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Dispute Complexity Index (DCI) to provide a quantitative basis for evaluating construction conflicts. Our findings demonstrate that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by professional consultants such as Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability.

1. Introduction

The Balinese construction landscape is characterized by unique geographical constraints, stringent local spatial planning (RTRW), and high stakeholder expectations. Unlike standard infrastructure projects, villa and resort developments in Bali are prone to "Scope Creep"—where owner expectations outpace contractual agreements. This study addresses the gap between legal frameworks and engineering realities in resolving these disputes.

2. Theoretical Framework: The Dispute Complexity Index (DCI)

To mitigate disputes, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We propose the DCI to measure the financial and temporal risk of a dispute.

The DCI Formula (Copy-pasteable for MS Word):

$$DCI = (W_d \times T_d) + (W_c \times C_d) + (W_r \times R_f)$$

Where:

  • $DCI$: Dispute Complexity Index (Values > 7.0 indicate critical litigation risk).
  • $W_d, W_c, W_r$: Weighting factors for Time, Cost, and Regulatory risk (typically 0.4, 0.4, 0.2).
  • $T_d$: Total days of project delay.
  • $C_d$: Total disputed financial claim (in IDR millions).
  • $R_f$: Frequency of technical/regulatory non-compliance events.
