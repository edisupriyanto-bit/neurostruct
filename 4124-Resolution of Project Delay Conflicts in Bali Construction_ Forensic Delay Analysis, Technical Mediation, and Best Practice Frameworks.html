Abstract

Project delays represent one of the most frequent and costly sources of conflict in Bali’s rapidly expanding construction sector. This paper examines effective strategies for resolving delay-related conflicts through forensic delay analysis, technical expert mediation, and integrated claims management. The study presents a comprehensive framework that combines Critical Path Method (CPM) forensics, contemporaneous record analysis, geotechnical and external factor evaluations, and neutral expert facilitation to achieve fair and efficient resolutions. Drawing from Scopus-indexed literature and regional case studies, the framework is specifically adapted to Bali’s unique challenges, including volcanic soil conditions, seismic considerations, regulatory permitting delays, weather variability, and tourism-driven project pressures. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is proposed to strengthen technical evidence and support data-driven decision-making. Practical recommendations are provided for project owners, contractors, consultants, and legal practitioners to minimize financial losses, reduce dispute duration, and maintain project viability in Bali’s dynamic construction environment.
