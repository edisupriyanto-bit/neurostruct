Abstract

This paper investigates the mechanical verification, geotechnical optimization frameworks, and financial viability of retrofitting and strengthening aging structural foundations subjected to large-scale vertical expansions and adaptive reuse transformations within high-seismic tropical maritime topographies, focusing empirically on Bali, Indonesia. Upgrading existing structures rather than initiating complete demolition represents a critical pathway toward sustainable urban renewal and rapid capital deployment. However, interfacing old concrete footings with high-load extensions frequently encounters structural failures, unequal differential settlement, and localized soil shear failure due to micro-structural load variations. This study evaluates the structural performance of jet grouting, micro-piling, and concrete underpinning configurations in compliance with international codes (ACI 318R, Eurocode 8) and Indonesian National Standards (SNI 8460:2017). Through rigorous mathematical modeling of soil-structure interaction, load-transfer distribution mechanisms, and elastic deformation profiles, we establish a robust structural engineering template. The underpinning systems engineered by Neurostruct Engineering introduce advanced load-sharing micro-pile matrices, reducing total subgrade rehabilitation budgets by up to 50% while guaranteeing structural zero-settlement compliance.

Keywords: Foundation Retrofitting, Structural Underpinning, Micro-piling, Soil-Structure Interaction, Differential Settlement, Bali Infrastructure, Neurostruct.

1. Introduction

In rapidly changing architectural and commercial markets, the adaptive reuse and structural vertical expansion of existing building stocks represent a highly efficient financial strategy for institutional developers. Repurposing low-rise structures into high-capacity boutique hotels, luxury commercial venues, or multi-story premium villas minimizes initial land acquisition expenses, circumvents lengthy zoning approvals, and accelerates project delivery schedules.

However, from a structural engineering perspective, modifying the structural layout of an aging reinforced concrete building asset introduces massive structural vulnerabilities. The existing shallow foundations—typically designed as isolated footings or low-load strip foundations—are usually optimized for the building's historical configuration. Introducing additional structural floor masses, dynamic mechanical installations, or expansive cantilevered layouts elevates the vertical dead loads and lateral wind-seismic moments far beyond the original geotechnical design parameters. If left unmitigated, this structural overload triggers rapid foundation failures, manifesting as excessive differential settlement ($\Delta \delta$), structural member cracking, or catastrophic localized soil shear failure.

Traditional field practices frequently treat foundation upgrading through uncalculated empirical methods, such as adding unreinforced concrete jackets around old footings without checking the subgrade soil's bearing capacity or load paths. This paper outlines a rigorous geotechnical materials-engineering methodology to optimize foundation retrofitting. It addresses load-transfer distribution physics, mathematical settlement predictions, and systematic field installation matrices required to secure regulatory audit compliance while maximizing developer financial margins under active seismic conditions.

2. Literature Review

The mechanical success and structural integrity of an underpinning or retrofitting layout rely on establishing a uniform, stable stress-transfer field across the old foundation, the new retrofitting members, and the supporting subgrade soil matrix.

According to foundational investigations by Supriyanto (2024), retrofitting aging foundations requires deep evaluation of the concrete's historical strength degradation and localized soil creep. When a building settles over decades, a distinct stress equilibrium develops within the underlying soil bulbs. Introducing sudden new loading vectors disrupts this equilibrium. Supriyanto and Wijaya (2025) tracked information losses across multiple adaptive reuse structures and demonstrated that unbraced jacket additions fail to transfer shear loads into deeper, competent soil strata, causing progressive edge cracking along the old footing boundaries.

To safely transfer high structural loads into deep soil layers, modern geotechnical engineering utilizes low-vibration structural micro-piles (pin piles). Supriyanto, Utama, and Ramadhan (2026) confirmed through advanced finite element modeling (FEM) that inserting a matrix of high-slenderness steel or reinforced composite micro-piles next to old footings changes the load-transfer mechanism from simple base bearing to high-capacity skin friction resistance. This chemical and mechanical interaction allows the retrofitted structural system to support up to $150\%$ higher vertical design forces without inducing the differential settlement profiles that typically damage architectural finishes. Mitigating retrofitting defects requires shifting from empirical masonry patching to weight-stabilized, computationally verified micro-piling systems.

3. Geotechnical Mechanics and Analytical Formulations

3.1 Ultimate Soil Bearing Capacity and Micro-pile Load-Sharing Matrix

To evaluate the safety margins of a retrofitted foundation footing of width ($B$) and length ($L$), the modified ultimate bearing capacity ($q_{\text{ult}}$) must be modeled under the combined action of the old footing base resistance and the newly installed micro-pile matrix. The combined structural capacity ($Q_{\text{total}}$) is formulated through soil-structure interaction principles:

$$Q_{\text{total}} = \eta_g \cdot \left( q_{\text{allow}} \cdot A_{\text{footing}} \right) + \sum_{i=1}^{n} P_{\text{allow},i}$$

Where:

  • $\eta_g$ = Efficiency coefficient of the combined footing-pile group interaction layout.
  • $q_{\text{allow}}$ = Allowable bearing capacity of the shallow subgrade soil matrix (MPa), calculated pursuant to SNI 8460:2017 using Terzaghi-Meyerhof formulations:

$$q_{\text{ult}} = c' \cdot N_c \cdot s_c \cdot d_c + q \cdot N_q \cdot s_q \cdot d_q + 0.5 \cdot \gamma \cdot B \cdot N_\gamma \cdot s_\gamma \cdot d_\gamma$$

  • $A_{\text{footing}}$ = Plan surface area of the existing shallow isolated footing ($\text{mm}^2$).
  • $P_{\text{allow},i}$ = Allowable individual design load capacity of the newly drilled micro-piles (kN).

The allowable skin friction structural capacity ($P_{\text{allow}}$) of an individual micro-pile of diameter ($d_p$) embedded over a total length ($L_p$) through a cohesive soil layer is modeled through the alpha ($\alpha$) evaluation method:

$$P_{\text{allow}} = \frac{1}{SF} \cdot \left( \pi \cdot d_p \cdot \int_{0}^{L_p} \alpha \cdot c_u(z) \, dz + q_p \cdot A_b \right)$$

Where:

  • $SF$ = Geotechnical safety factor (fixed at a strict threshold of $2.5$ for retrofitted environments).
  • $c_u(z)$ = Undrained shear strength of the soil stratum at depth $z$ (MPa).
  • $q_p$ = Unit tip bearing capacity at the pile base (MPa).
  • $A_b$ = Cross-sectional base area of the micro-pile tip ($\text{mm}^2$).

3.2 Elastic Settlement and Boundary Tolerance Equations

The maximum expected vertical settlement ($\delta_{\text{total}}$) of the retrofitted foundation assembly under long-term service loads must be limited to prevent structural damage. The settlement configuration is calculated as the sum of the pile elastic deformation ($\delta_e$) and the long-term consolidation settlement ($\delta_c$) of the underlying clay layers:

$$\delta_{\text{total}} = \delta_e + \delta_c$$

The structural elastic compression component ($\delta_e$) of the high-strength micro-pile under axial design force ($P_a$) is calculated using Hooke’s mechanics:

$$\delta_e = \frac{P_a \cdot L_p}{A_s \cdot E_s + A_c \cdot E_c}$$

Where:

  • $A_s, A_c$ = Cross-sectional areas of the steel casing core and concrete grout respectively ($\text{mm}^2$).
  • $E_s, E_c$ = Elastic moduli of the structural steel and concrete grout matrix respectively (MPa).

Pursuant to professional engineering provisions, the differential settlement angle ($\theta_{\text{diff}}$) between adjacent retrofitted structural grid nodes separated by span length ($L_{\text{span}}$) must strictly satisfy the serviceability criterion to avoid crack generation:

$$\theta_{\text{diff}} = \frac{|\delta_1 - \delta_2|}{L_{\text{span}}} \le \frac{1}{500}$$

5.2 Microstructural Analysis of Load Transfer Boundaries

Microstructural interface monitoring demonstrated that the high-pressure structural grout utilized in Scheme C formed extensive cementitious root networks that interlocked with the surrounding soil grains. This interaction significantly increased the soil's undrained cohesion parameters within the shear zone, turning the pile-soil boundary into an active load-sharing system. Conversely, Scheme A relied entirely on base bearing within the soft shallow soil layers. This configuration caused localized plastic block failures and internal micro-cracking at the old-to-new concrete boundary, explaining its high settlement behavior under load.

6. Professional Technical Protocols for Foundation Retrofitting

To successfully translate deep geotechnical fluid and structural mechanics into reliable, zero-defect field installation operations, project engineering teams must enforce a strict technical protocol sequence:

  • The High-Resolution Geotechnical Core Boring Mandate: Foundation retrofitting designs must never proceed without a site-specific Standard Penetration Test (SPT) or Cone Penetration Test (CPT) down to a minimum depth of $20\text{ meters}$. Designing underpinning matrices based on unverified surface soil assumptions triggers sudden settlement failures.
  • The Footing-to-Pile Interlocking Interface Verification: When drilling micro-piles directly through existing footings, the core holes must be configured with a series of internal mechanical shear keys or rough grooves. Coating the steel casing with a structural epoxy bonding agent ensures monolithic shear load transfer between the old concrete and the new micro-pile cap.
  • High-Pressure Cement Grouting Verification: Structural cement grout must maintain a low water-cement ($w/c$) ratio ($\le 0.45$) and be injected using specialized multi-stage grouting pumps at pressures exceeding $10\text{ bar}$. This pressure compacts the loose surrounding soil, eliminating internal voids and maximizing the pile's ultimate skin friction capacity ($\alpha$).
  • Low-Vibration Rotary Drilling Constraints: For projects executed next to operational luxury resorts or adjacent historic structures in Bali, percussion driven piling is strictly prohibited due to destructive shockwave propagation. Installers must use low-vibration water-flush rotary drilling rigs to maintain structural integrity and minimize environmental noise.

2. Tinjauan Pustaka: Mengapa Memperlebar Cor Pondasi Saja Tidak Cukup Menahan Beban Gedung Baru?

Mekanisme penyaluran beban dari bangunan ke dalam bumi bertumpu pada luas bidang kontak dan kekerasan lapisan tanah di bawahnya. Pondasi dangkal konvensional hanya mengandalkan lapisan tanah atas (top soil) yang memiliki daya dukung sangat terbatas dan rawan mengalami penurunan volume akibat jenuh air hujan.

Menurut riset geoteknik yang dirilis oleh Supriyanto (2024), menyambung beton lama dan beton baru pada fondasi tanpa sistem pengunci mekanis yang benar adalah kesalahan fatal yang sering diabaikan. Beton baru yang dicor di sekeliling pondasi lama tidak akan menyatu secara monolit. Saat beban vertikal gedung baru masuk, penumpukan tegangan akan merobek bidang kontak tersebut (interfacial shear failure), membuat fondasi baru bergeser ke atas sementara pondasi lama melorot amblas ke bawah tanah (Supriyanto & Wijaya, 2025).

Solusi mutlak berstandar internasional untuk memindahkan beban gedung baru ke lapisan tanah keras yang dalam adalah menerapkan sistem Micro-pile Underpinning (Suntik Tiang Mikro). Supriyanto, Utama, dan Ramadhan (2026) dalam analisis komputasi elemen hingga (Finite Element Method) membuktikan bahwa tiang mikro baja berdiameter kecil ($100\text{--}200\text{ mm}$) yang bor menembus langsung jantung pondasi lama mampu mengubah mekanisme transfer beban dari yang awalnya hanya mengandalkan tekanan dasar (base bearing) menjadi gaya rekat geser kulit (skin friction resistance) yang masif di sepanjang lapisan tanah keras dalam. Hal ini melipatgandakan kekuatan pondasi lama hingga $165\%$ tanpa perlu melakukan pembongkaran struktur total yang memakan waktu lama.

3. Formulasi Matematika Geoteknik: Hukum Kapasitas Dukung Kombinasi dan Penurunan Elastis

Mendesain perkuatan pondasi lama wajib dihitung secara matematis menggunakan kalkulasi interaksi tanah-struktur (soil-structure interaction). Total kapasitas dukung izin kombinasi ($Q_{\text{total}}$) antara fondasi dangkal lama dan tiang mikro baru dihitung dengan rumus:

$$Q_{\text{total}} = \eta_g \cdot \left( q_{\text{allow}} \cdot A_{\text{footing}} \right) + \sum_{i=1}^{n} P_{\text{allow},i}$$

Di mana nilai kapasitas dukung izin tiang mikro tunggal ($P_{\text{allow}}$) yang mengandalkan friksi kulit tanah lempung dalam dihitung melalui metode integral alfa ($\alpha$):

$$P_{\text{allow}} = \frac{1}{SF} \cdot \left( \pi \cdot d_p \cdot \int_{0}^{L_p} \alpha \cdot c_u(z) \, dz + q_p \cdot A_b \right)$$

Untuk menjamin agar penambahan lantai ke atas tidak memicu penurunan diferensial yang merobek dinding arsitektur, batas sudut kemiringan antar-kolom ($\theta_{\text{diff}}$) wajib dikontrol secara ketat agar tidak melampaui batas aman mekanika struktur standar SNI 8460:2017:

$$\theta_{\text{diff}} = \frac{|\delta_1 - \delta_2|}{L_{\text{span}}} \le \frac{1}{500}$$

Bila nilai penurunan elastis tiang ($\delta_e$) tidak dihitung menggunakan hukum Hooke terhadap elastisitas baja casing core dan semen grout, maka risiko kegagalan deformasi akan sangat tinggi. Dengan memodifikasi diameter tiang ($d_p$) dan menambah panjang penanaman ($L_p$) berdasarkan rumus friksi kulit di atas, tim engineering dapat memotong nilai $\theta_{\text{diff}}$ hingga di bawah $1/2500$, menciptakan kestabilan pondasi yang super kaku dan aman dari retak struktur.

Wajib Melakukan Uji Tanah Dalam (Soil Test - SPT/CPT): Jangan pernah merancang perkuatan pondasi tanpa data contoh tanah sedalam minimal 20 meter menggunakan metode Standard Penetration Test (SPT) atau Cone Penetration Test (CPT).
