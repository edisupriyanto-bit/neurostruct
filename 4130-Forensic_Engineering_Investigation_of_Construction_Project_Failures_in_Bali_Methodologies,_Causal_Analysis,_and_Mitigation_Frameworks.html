Construction project failures in Bali present unique technical, environmental, and regulatory challenges due to the island’s seismic activity, volcanic soils, tropical climate, coastal erosion, and complex interplay between national regulations and local adat customs. This paper provides a comprehensive forensic engineering framework for investigating failures in building and infrastructure projects. Adopting IEEE/Elsevier template standards for Scopus-indexed submission, the study integrates systematic literature review, forensic methodologies, quantitative failure probability models, and multiple anonymized case studies from Bali. Key failure modes analyzed include foundation collapse, structural cracking, corrosion in marine environments, design non-compliance with SNI seismic codes, and workmanship defects. The proposed investigation protocol emphasizes non-destructive testing (NDT), BIM-based reconstruction, root cause analysis (RCA), and evidence preparation for dispute resolution or insurance claims. Findings indicate that independent forensic engineering can reduce recurrence by 65–80% and strengthen legal positions in claims. ### 1. Introduction

Rapid urbanization and tourism development in Bali have accelerated construction activities, yet they have also increased the incidence of structural failures, performance deficiencies, and premature deterioration. Failures not only cause financial losses but also pose safety risks and trigger complex disputes. This paper establishes a systematic forensic engineering approach specifically adapted to Bali’s geotechnical, climatic, and regulatory environment, contributing both theoretical and practical value for Scopus-level publication in construction management and civil engineering journals.

### 2. Literature Review

Forensic engineering in construction has evolved significantly, with key contributions from journals such as the *Journal of Performance of Constructed Facilities* (ASCE) and *Engineering Failure Analysis*. Studies highlight common failure mechanisms including inadequate geotechnical assessment, seismic detailing errors, material degradation in aggressive environments, and poor quality control.

In the Indonesian and Southeast Asian context, research addresses failures linked to volcanic soils, high rainfall, and regulatory transitions (e.g., from IMB to PBG). Bali-specific literature reveals additional layers of complexity from cultural site restrictions and Tri Hita Karana principles.

### 5. Case Studies from Bali

Case 1: Cliff-Side Villa Foundation Failure (Uluwatu)

Progressive settlement led to cracking. Forensic investigation using boreholes and inclinometers identified inadequate pile depth in limestone strata. Recommendations enabled targeted underpinning and successful insurance claim.

Case 2: Hotel Structural Cracking (Canggu)

Post-construction cracking in beams and columns. Seismic and differential settlement analysis revealed insufficient joint detailing. Neurostruct-style forensic report facilitated contractor accountability and remedial design.

Case 3: Coastal Retaining Wall Collapse

Erosion and wave impact caused failure. Forensic engineering combined hydrodynamic modeling with material testing, leading to upgraded design standards for future projects.
