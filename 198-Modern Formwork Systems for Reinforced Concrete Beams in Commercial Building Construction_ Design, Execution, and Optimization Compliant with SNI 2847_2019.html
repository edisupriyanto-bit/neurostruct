Abstract (English Version)

Reinforced concrete beams in commercial buildings must satisfy stringent requirements for load-carrying capacity, serviceability, seismic performance, and construction efficiency. This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of modern formwork systems for reinforced concrete beams in commercial building construction in full compliance with Indonesian National Standard SNI 2847:2019 (Persyaratan Beton Struktural untuk Bangunan Gedung), which modifies ACI 318M-14 provisions. Complementary standards include SNI 1727:2020 for minimum design loads and SNI 1726:2019 for earthquake-resistant design.

Particular emphasis is given to commercial projects in Bali, where mixed-use buildings, hotels, and retail complexes require fast floor cycles, architectural beam finishes, and compliance with seismic demands in a tropical coastal environment.

2. Literature Review and Code Framework

SNI 2847:2019 adopts modified ACI 318M-14 provisions and requires formwork to prevent mortar loss and maintain dimensional accuracy. ACI 347 Guide to Formwork for Concrete serves as the primary international reference for beam formwork design and execution. Studies on commercial projects in Indonesia and Southeast Asia show that aluminum modular beam formwork systems significantly reduce floor cycle times (often 5–7 days per floor) and provide superior reusability and surface quality compared to traditional timber systems. In Bali’s seismic Zone 4 and humid coastal climate, modern systems help maintain tight schedules while ensuring durability of beams that are frequently part of open-plan commercial spaces.

3. Lateral Pressure and Load Calculations for Commercial Beam Formwork

In commercial projects with longer beam spans and heavier loads, accurate lateral pressure prediction is essential for safe and economical formwork design.

Simplified lateral pressure formula for beam side forms (adapted from ACI 347):

p = C_w × C_c × [150 + 9000 × R / (T + 18)] (kPa)

Where:

- C_w = 1.0 (normal weight concrete)

- C_c = 1.0 (Type I cement)

- R = rate of placement (m/h)

- T = concrete temperature (°C)

Copy-paste ready example (Microsoft Word compatible):

For a commercial beam with side height 0.80 m, R = 2.0 m/h (typical commercial pouring rate), T = 30°C (Bali condition):

p ≈ 150 + 9000 × 2.0 / (30 + 18) = 150 + 18000 / 48 ≈ 150 + 375 = 525 kPa

Apply height limit p_max = 150 × 0.80 = 120 kPa or system capacity, then design side ties and walers with appropriate safety factor (1.5–2.0) to maintain form stability.

Soffit formwork must be designed for full wet concrete weight plus construction live loads (typically 2.4–4.0 kPa or higher in commercial settings).

4. Modern Formwork Systems for Commercial Beam Construction

# 4.1 Conventional Timber/Plywood Systems

Still used in some smaller commercial projects due to familiarity and low initial cost, but limited in speed and reusability for repetitive floors.

# 4.2 Semi-System and Aluminum Modular Beam Formwork (Recommended for Commercial Projects)

- Lightweight aluminum panels for soffit and sides with integrated prop heads and quick-clamp mechanisms.

- High reusability (200–300+ cycles), fast erection/dismantling, and excellent surface finish suitable for commercial ceilings.

- Compatible with table form or flying form systems for efficient floor-by-floor construction.

Key Implementation Strategies:

- Standardize beam dimensions across floors to maximize panel reuse.

- Combine beam formwork with slab systems for monolithic pouring where structurally feasible.

- Use adjustable steel props or scaffold shoring for flexible support in commercial layouts.

- Incorporate chamfer strips for clean beam edges in visible areas.

5. Execution Sequence and Quality Control in Commercial Settings

1. Planning: Coordinate beam and slab formwork layout with MEP installations.

2. Shoring Installation: Set primary supports at calculated spacing.

3. Formwork Assembly: Install soffit panels, middle beams, then side panels with quick clamps.

4. Alignment: Use laser levels for precision across large floor plates.

5. Inspection: Verify joint tightness, cover, reinforcement position, and bracing stability.

6. Concreting: Controlled placement with multiple vibrators for large pours.

7. Stripping and Reshoring: Strip beam sides early while maintaining soffit support; apply proper curing.

6. Seismic and Durability Considerations for Bali Commercial Buildings

Beam formwork must allow accurate placement of seismic reinforcement at beam-column joints. In Bali’s coastal commercial projects, dense concrete surfaces from modern sealed systems improve resistance to environmental degradation.
