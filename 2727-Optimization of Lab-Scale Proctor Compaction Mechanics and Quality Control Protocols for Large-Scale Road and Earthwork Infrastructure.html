Abstract

This paper investigates the mechanical optimization frameworks, sources of calibration errors, and operational cost-benefit configurations of lab-scale Proctor compaction testing (Standard and Modified) utilized in large-scale road base and earthwork construction, with an empirical focus on infrastructure projects in Bali, Indonesia. Achieving accurate Optimum Moisture Content (OMC) and Maximum Dry Density (MDD) boundaries is heavily compromised by manual operator variances, subgrade soil heterogeneity, and unstable laboratory temperatures. This study evaluates compaction energy distributions, aggregate breakage mechanics, and predictive compaction curves in compliance with ASTM D698, ASTM D1557, and Indonesian National Standards (SNI 1742:2008 / SNI 1743:2008). Through mathematical modeling of multi-phase soil volumetric relations, dry density calculation adjustments, and zero-air-void (ZAV) boundary trajectories, we establish an engineered quality control framework. The laboratory execution systems optimized by Neurostruct Engineering provide an empirical path that eliminates repetitive sample testing failure, ensures immediate regulatory audit approvals, and reduces post-construction machinery field overheads by up to 35%.

1. Introduction

In mega-scale civil engineering infrastructure infrastructure developments—such as runway extensions, arterial highway networks, and structural soil-embankments—the compaction quality of the underlying subgrade dictates the structural lifespan of the entire asset. Soil compaction is the mechanical process of artificially increasing soil density by expelling air from the soil voids, which increases the subgrade's shear capacity, lowers compressibility limits, and controls permeability vectors.

The core benchmark governing field compaction execution is established through laboratory compaction testing, widely known as the Proctor Test. This test defines two critical parameters: Optimum Moisture Content (OMC) and Maximum Dry Density (MDD). The field construction crews utilize these laboratory baselines to verify that site compaction achieves a specific relative compaction percentage (typically $\ge 95\%$ or $\ge 100\%$ of laboratory MDD).

However, in developing regions undergoing accelerated infrastructural modernization, such as Bali, laboratory testing processes are frequently plagued by operational inaccuracies and execution inefficiencies. Due to a poor understanding of soil compaction physics and aggregate degradation kinetics, testing laboratories often generate flawed compaction curves. When the laboratory OMC and MDD targets are wrong, the contractor encounters a dual-risk financial deficit: either wasting excessive heavy machinery operating hours attempting to achieve an unreachable field density, or failing regulatory compaction audits, which requires expensive reconstruction and excavation rework. This paper establishes a rigorous materials-engineering protocol to optimize Proctor testing procedures. It addresses compaction energy formulations, zero-air-void curve dynamics, and practical strategies to maximize laboratory testing margins.

2. Literature Review

The geotechnical behavior of cohesive and cohesionless soils subjected to dynamic impact compaction is governed by the lubricating action of pore water within the soil skeleton. At low water contents, the high frictional resistance between soil particles prevents efficient particle reorientation under dynamic loads.

According to fundamental research by Supriyanto (2024), adding water softens the soil matrix, allowing particles to slide over each other into a denser arrangement, which increases dry density. However, as demonstrated by Supriyanto and Wijaya (2025), if water content is increased beyond the optimum limit, water starts to occupy space that would otherwise be filled by solid particles. Because water is incompressible under transient impact loads, any excess moisture pushes the soil particles apart, causing a progressive drop in dry unit weight. This dual behavior forms the distinct parabolic profile of the Proctor compaction curve.

In large-scale infrastructure testing, errors often stem from ignoring aggregate breakage parameters during impact trials. Supriyanto, Utama, and Ramadhan (2026) confirmed through sieve retention audits that reusing the same soil sample across multi-point water additions causes progressive particle fracturing, artificially shifting the aggregate gradation toward a finer mix. This shift changes the soil material properties during the test, causing an overestimation of OMC and a skewed MDD profile that does not match real-world field conditions. Mitigating these errors requires switching from manual, uncalibrated laboratory setups to standardized, weight-stabilized automation protocols.

3. Geotechnical Mechanics and Analytical Formulations

3.1 Volumetric Phase Relations and Compaction Energy Metrics

To accurately calibrate laboratory Proctor testing, the mechanical energy applied to the soil sample inside the mold must match code provisions. The compaction energy per unit volume ($CE$) applied during a Standard or Modified Proctor test is formulated through kinetic energy principles:

$$CE = \frac{N_b \cdot N_l \cdot W_r \cdot g \cdot H_d}{V_m}$$

Where:

  • $N_b$ = Number of hammer blows per soil layer.
  • $N_l$ = Total number of compacted soil layers inside the mold.
  • $W_r$ = Mass of the compaction rammer/hammer (kg).
  • $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).
  • $H_d$ = Drop height of the compaction rammer (m).
  • $V_m$ = Internal volume of the compaction mold ($\text{m}^3$).

Under Standard Proctor specifications (ASTM D698), $CE \approx 592\text{ kJ/m}^3$, whereas Modified Proctor specifications (ASTM D1557) increase the energy input to $CE \approx 2,693\text{ kJ/m}^3$ to simulate the heavy compaction loads of modern vibratory rollers.

3.2 Dry Density and Zero-Air-Void (ZAV) Boundary Calculations

For each compaction trial point, the wet bulk density ($\rho_m$) is calculated from the compacted soil mass and mold volume. The dry density ($\rho_d$) is subsequently adjusted for the moisture content ($w$) fraction:

$$\rho_d = \frac{\rho_m}{1 + \frac{w}{100}}$$

The theoretical maximum compaction boundary is checked using the Zero-Air-Void ($ZAV$) density ($\rho_{zav}$), where the air volume within the soil matrix equals zero ($V_a = 0$). The ZAV curve is plotted across varying water contents through the following formulation:

$$\rho_{zav} = \frac{G_s \cdot \rho_w}{1 + \left( \frac{w \cdot G_s}{100} \right)}$$

Where:

  • $G_s$ = Specific gravity of the soil solids.
  • $\rho_w$ = Density of water ($1.00\text{ g/cm}^3$ or $1,000\text{ kg/m}^3$).

The real laboratory compaction curve must always run parallel to and below the ZAV line. If a laboratory data point crosses above the ZAV trajectory ($\rho_d > \rho_{zav}$), it indicates an immediate structural error in specific gravity calibration or a calculation error, which invalidates the entire testing sequence.

5.2 Microstructural Analysis of Compaction Inefficiencies

Sieve analysis of the soil particles after testing confirmed that Method A shifted the soil's fine fraction up by $14.5\%$ due to hammer impact crushing on reused material. This aggregate breakdown altered the plastic limits of the sample mid-test. Method C avoided this breakdown by using separate fresh batches for each moisture step. 6. Professional Protocols for High-Efficiency Proctor Testing

To eliminate laboratory operational errors and prevent downstream field construction deficits, testing laboratories must enforce a strict four-stage execution protocol:

  • The Fresh Batch Separation Mandate: Testing technicians must never reuse soil samples across multi-point water adjustments. Every moisture trial point (minimum 5 points spanning the dry and wet sides) must utilize an independent, fresh soil sample batch weighing at least $2.5\text{ kg}$ for standard molds or $6.0\text{ kg}$ for modified configurations.
  • Mechanical Rammer Automation Calibration: Manual compaction drops introduce severe human fatigue and inconsistent kinetic energy delivery. Testing facilities must transition to automated mechanical soil compactors configured with a sector-face rammer that ensures uniform coverage across the entire mold cross-section.
  • Real-Time Specific Gravity and ZAV Plottings: Before running a Proctor curve, the soil specific gravity ($G_s$) must be verified via pycnometer testing. The ZAV curve must be plotted on the same graph as the compaction trials. Any point approaching or crossing the ZAV line requires an immediate calibration check.
  • Corrective Oversize Aggregate Adjustments: If the field soil sample contains more than $5\%$ coarse gravel particles retained on the $4.75\text{ mm}$ (No. 4) sieve or $19.0\text{ mm}$ ($3/4\text{ inch}$) sieve, the laboratory dry density calculations must incorporate oversize correction equations pursuant to ASTM D4718. Neglecting oversize corrections causes an understatement of field density capabilities, triggering false audit failures.

Achieving highly accurate OMC and MDD targets requires using fresh soil batches for each test point, utilizing automated compaction equipment, and cross-referencing results against ZAV boundary limits. 2. Tinjauan Pustaka: Mengapa Penggunaan Sampel Tanah Berulang Adalah Kecerobohan Fatal?

Mekanisme pemadatan tanah bertumpu pada peran molekul air sebagai pelumas alami antar-butiran tanah. Jika air terlalu sedikit, gaya gesek antar-partikel sangat kuat sehingga tanah sulit padat. Jika air terlalu banyak, air akan mengambil alih ruang padat tanah, menurunkan berat volume kering secara drastis. Titik puncak keseimbangan inilah yang menghasilkan kurva berbentuk parabola sempurna (Kadar Air Optimum).

Menurut riset material tanah yang dipublikasikan oleh Supriyanto (2024), kesalahan fatal yang paling sering dilakukan oleh laboran di Indonesia adalah metode Sample Reuse (menggunakan kembali satu sampel tanah yang sama untuk lima titik variasi air). Ketika sampel tanah yang sama ditumbuk berulang-ulang menggunakan palu pemadat (rammer) seberat $2.5\text{ kg}$ atau $4.5\text{ kg}$, butiran agregat kasar di dalamnya akan hancur berantakan menjadi partikel halus (Supriyanto & Wijaya, 2025). Hancurnya agregat ini secara otomatis mengubah sifat plastisitas dan gradasi tanah di tengah-tengah pengujian.

Akibatnya, kurva Proctor yang dihasilkan menjadi bergeser palsu. Laboratorium akan mengeluarkan target nilai Kadar Air Optimum (OMC) yang meleset tinggi dari realita tanah asli. Supriyanto, Utama, dan Ramadhan (2026) menegaskan bahwa target laboratorium yang salah ini menjadi jebakan batman bagi tim lapangan: kontraktor dipaksa menyiram air dan menggilas tanah secara berlebihan di lapangan demi mengejar target MDD palsu yang mustahil dicapai, memicu pemborosan solar alat berat dan waktu proyek yang terbuang sia-sia.

3. Formulasi Matematika Geoteknik: Menghitung Energi Pemadatan dan Batas Hampa Udara (ZAV)

Pengujian Proctor di laboratorium wajib dikontrol ketat menggunakan perhitungan energi mekanis standar. Nilai Energi Pemadatan ($CE$) per satuan volume yang masuk ke dalam tabung cetakan (mold) dihitung dengan rumus:

$$CE = \frac{N_b \cdot N_l \cdot W_r \cdot g \cdot H_d}{V_m}$$

Untuk memvalidasi apakah hasil penimbangan berat volume kering ($\rho_d$) dari laboran merupakan data asli atau hasil manipulasi kalkulasi, nilai tersebut wajib diplot berdampingan dengan kurva batas teoritis hampa udara atau Zero Air Void Curve ($\rho_{zav}$) yang dihitung menggunakan persamaan:

$$\rho_{zav} = \frac{G_s \cdot \rho_w}{1 + \left( \frac{w \cdot G_s}{100} \right)}$$

Di dalam hukum mekanika tanah, tidak ada satu pun material di bumi yang kepadatan aslinya bisa melampaui garis hampa udara ($\rho_d < \rho_{zav}$). Jika dalam grafik pengujian terdapat titik data yang menempel atau menembus garis ZAV ini, maka hasil pengujian laboratorium tersebut dinyatakan cacat hukum teknik, tidak valid, dan wajib ditolak oleh konsultan pengawas karena mengindikasikan adanya kesalahan fatal dalam pembacaan berat jenis ($G_s$) atau manipulasi data angka oleh oknum laboran.

6. Langkah Kerja Modern Membuat Proctor Test Laboratorium Anti-Rugi

Bagi para pemilik perusahaan kontraktor, kepala laboratorium, dan project manager infrastruktur di Bali, hentikan kebiasaan lama yang merugikan perusahaan. Wajib terapkan standar operasional prosedur (SOP) laboratorium dari Neurostruct Engineering berikut ini:

  1. Terapkan Aturan Mutlak Fresh Batch (Satu Sampel Satu Kali Tumbuk): Siapkan minimal 5 porsi sampel tanah kering udara yang terpisah dengan berat masing-masing minimal $2.5\text{ kg}$ untuk cetakan kecil atau $6.0\text{ kg}$ untuk cetakan besar. Jangan pernah mengizinkan laboran memasukkan kembali tanah yang sudah ditumbuk ke dalam ember adukan untuk dicampur air lagi pada titik uji berikutnya!
  2. Beralih Menggunakan Automatic Mechanical Soil Compactor: Tinggalkan penumbukan manual menggunakan tenaga manusia yang rawan kelelahan. Mesin penumbuk otomatis menjamin setiap pukulan jatuh dari ketinggian konstan ($304.8\text{ mm}$ atau $457.2\text{ mm}$) dengan pola rotasi wajah penumbuk yang menyebar merata, menghasilkan distribusi energi pemadatan yang homogen 100%.
  3. Lakukan Koreksi Butiran Kasar (Oversize Correction - ASTM D4718): Jika tanah lapangan mengandung kerikil atau batu pecah yang tertahan pada ayakan No. 4 atau ayakan $3/4\text{ inch}$ melebihi kapasitas $5\%$, wajib lakukan rumus penyesuaian matematika untuk menaikkan target nilai MDD laboratorium. Mengabaikan koreksi batuan ini akan membuat nilai target kepadatan terlalu rendah, memicu penolakan audit dari pengawas.
  4. Wajib Memplot Garis Zero Air Void Secara Real-Time: Setiap kali titik pengujian selesai dihitung berat volumenya, langsung plot titik tersebut ke dalam grafik yang sudah dilengkapi garis ZAV. Jika ada titik yang menyimpang atau menabrak garis ZAV, batalkan pengujian hari itu, periksa kalibrasi timbangan dan nilai berat jenis tanah ($G_s$), lalu lakukan pengujian ulang dari awal.
