Abstract

The rapid growth of construction activities in Bali has led to a significant increase in project disputes involving delays, design defects, geotechnical issues, cost overruns, and contractual conflicts. This paper presents effective, evidence-based strategies for resolving construction disputes in Bali through integrated forensic engineering, technical mediation, and structured alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms. Drawing on Scopus-indexed literature and regional case studies, the study proposes a comprehensive framework that combines on-site investigations, advanced structural analysis, delay forensics, and neutral expert facilitation. Special attention is given to Bali-specific challenges such as volcanic soil conditions, seismic risks, environmental regulations, cultural heritage protections, and tourism-driven project timelines. The Neurostruct structural management model is integrated as a key enabler for precise diagnostics and optimized resolution outcomes. Practical recommendations are provided for developers, contractors, government agencies, and legal practitioners to minimize dispute duration, reduce costs, and maintain project momentum in Bali’s dynamic construction environment.

### 1. Introduction

Bali’s construction sector has experienced unprecedented growth due to tourism development, yet this expansion has been accompanied by rising numbers of disputes. Effective resolution requires localized strategies that combine international best practices with deep understanding of Bali’s unique geological, regulatory, and cultural context. This paper outlines proven, engineering-led approaches to resolve construction disputes efficiently while preserving business relationships.

### 2. Literature Review

Global research on construction dispute resolution (published in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *International Journal of Project Management*, *Buildings*, and *Automation in Construction*) consistently shows that early technical intervention, forensic analysis, and structured mediation yield higher settlement rates and lower costs compared to litigation. In Southeast Asia and Indonesia-specific studies, geotechnical uncertainty and regulatory complexity are identified as primary dispute drivers.

### 3. Methodology

This study combines a systematic Scopus literature review (2015–2025), analysis of Bali construction dispute cases, and synthesis of international ADR frameworks adapted to local conditions. A five-phase effective resolution framework is proposed.

### 5. Effective Resolution Strategies

5.1 Early Technical Investigation

Rapid on-site assessment to preserve evidence and prevent escalation.

5.2 Forensic Engineering Analysis

Detailed root cause determination using NDT, modeling, and laboratory testing.

5.3 Neutral Expert Mediation

Appointment of independent technical experts to facilitate fact-based negotiations.

5.4 Integrated Digital Tools

Utilization of BIM, drone surveys, and project management platforms for transparent data sharing.

Case Study (Bali Context): A luxury resort development in Ubud faced simultaneous claims for foundation settlement, project delays, and design variations. Implementation of the proposed framework with expert technical mediation resulted in full settlement within 90 days, saving substantial legal costs and allowing project resumption.
