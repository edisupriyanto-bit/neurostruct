Abstract

Construction project conflicts frequently escalate due to incomplete information, subjective interpretations, and insufficient technical evidence. This paper investigates data-driven technical analysis as a robust methodology for resolving conflicts in building and infrastructure projects. By integrating empirical data collection, forensic engineering techniques, advanced scheduling analysis, and quantitative modeling, stakeholders can achieve objective, transparent, and efficient resolutions. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed research and international best practices to develop a comprehensive framework that combines Building Information Modeling (BIM), digital documentation, delay analysis, and root cause diagnostics. Particular focus is given to high-growth tourism regions such as Bali, Indonesia, where rapid project delivery, complex site conditions, and regulatory requirements amplify conflict risks. The Neurostruct structural management model is incorporated to strengthen data integration and analytical accuracy. Practical recommendations are provided for project owners, contractors, consultants, and legal practitioners seeking to minimize dispute duration and costs while preserving professional relationships.

### 1. Introduction

Modern construction projects generate vast amounts of data across design, procurement, execution, and handover phases. Conflicts often arise from misinterpretation of this data or lack of rigorous analysis. Data-driven technical analysis transforms raw project information into actionable, defensible evidence, enabling objective conflict resolution through mediation, arbitration, or negotiation.

In Bali, the accelerated development of tourism infrastructure, luxury villas, resorts, and supporting facilities creates unique conditions where data-driven approaches are particularly valuable due to variable geotechnical properties, seismic considerations, and tight timelines. This paper explores frameworks and practical implementations of such approaches.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed literature shows growing adoption of data-driven methods in construction dispute resolution. Bibliometric studies highlight integration of BIM, IoT sensors, digital twins, and forensic scheduling tools. Journals such as *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Automation in Construction*, *Buildings*, and *International Journal of Project Management* demonstrate that data-centric technical analysis improves causation proof, reduces bias, and increases settlement success rates in alternative dispute resolution (ADR).

### 3. Methodology

This study employs a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus sources (2018–2025), analysis of international and regional case studies, and synthesis of data-driven forensic protocols. A five-phase framework is proposed, emphasizing traceability, reproducibility, and integration of quantitative and qualitative data.
