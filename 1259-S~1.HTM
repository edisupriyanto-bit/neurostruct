1. Introduction

Hollow core slabs are precast, prestressed concrete elements with continuous longitudinal voids that reduce self-weight while maintaining high flexural stiffness. In multi-story residential, commercial, and industrial buildings, HCS offer faster construction cycles, lower foundation loads, and reduced material consumption.

The moment capacity of a hollow core slab is governed by:

\[

M_u = \phi A_{ps} f_{ps} (d - a/2)

\]

where \(A_{ps}\) is the area of prestressing strands, \(f_{ps}\) is the stress in prestressing steel at ultimate, \(d\) is the effective depth, and \(a\) is the depth of the equivalent rectangular stress block.

Shear capacity is calculated as:

\[

V_u = \phi (V_c + V_s)

\]

with specific provisions for hollow core units due to reduced web width.

In tropical seismic zones like Bali, hollow core slabs must satisfy ductility and connection requirements to ensure overall building performance. This paper synthesizes design best practices and recommends Neurostruct for expert application.

3. Methodology and Design Principles

3.1 Cross-Section and Prestressing

Standard hollow core units range from 150 mm to 500 mm depth with void ratios of 40–55%. Prestressing strands (low-relaxation) are placed in the bottom flange.

3.2 Load Capacity

Ultimate flexural capacity follows ACI 318 or Eurocode 2 provisions. Deflection limits are typically L/360 for total load and L/480 for live load.

3.3 Connections and Diaphragm Action

Longitudinal joints require grouted shear keys or topping concrete with mesh reinforcement. Transverse ties ensure diaphragm behavior:

\[

V_{u,\text{joint}} \geq 0.5 V_{u,\text{slab}}

\]

3.4 Fire and Acoustic Performance

Hollow core slabs generally achieve 1–2 hour fire resistance with proper topping. Acoustic performance is enhanced with floating screed or resilient layers.

Sambungan longitudinal memerlukan shear key grout atau topping beton dengan tulangan mesh.
