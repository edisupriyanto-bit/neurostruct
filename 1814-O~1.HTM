Abstract

Rubble stone foundations (pondasi batu kali) remain a widely used, economical, and effective shallow foundation system for single-story residential buildings in many tropical regions, particularly where local stone is abundant and soil conditions allow. 1. Introduction

For single-story residential buildings, rubble stone foundations offer a traditional yet reliable solution that balances cost, availability of local materials, and structural performance. In many parts of Indonesia, including Bali, this type of foundation is still preferred for its simplicity and economy when soil bearing capacity is adequate (typically 0.8–2.0 kg/cm² or higher).

The basic design parameters include:

- Width of foundation (B): usually 60–90 cm for single-story loads

- Depth below ground level (D): minimum 60–80 cm to reach stable soil and avoid frost/scour (in tropical areas, mainly for scour and erosion protection)

- Thickness of stone masonry: 40–60 cm

Allowable bearing capacity is checked using Terzaghi’s bearing capacity equation or local empirical values:

\[

q_{ult} = c N_c + \gamma D N_q + 0.5 \gamma B N_\gamma

\]

\[

q_{all} = \frac{q_{ult}}{FS}

\]

where FS is the factor of safety (typically 3.0 for shallow foundations).

In seismic zones like Bali, additional considerations for ductility and tie beams are essential. This paper compiles standard dimensions based on field experience and international guidelines.

2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed studies on traditional stone foundations confirm their viability when properly designed. Research in tropical and seismic regions shows that stepped rubble stone foundations perform well when constructed with good mortar and proper drainage. Comparative studies indicate cost savings of 25–40% compared to reinforced concrete strip footings for low-rise buildings. Recent works emphasize the importance of drainage systems and minimum embedment depth to prevent erosion in high-rainfall areas.

3. Methodology and Recommended Standard Dimensions

3.1 Typical Dimensions for Single-Story Houses

- Width (B): 70–90 cm (depending on wall thickness and load)

- Depth (D): 70–100 cm from finished ground level

- Stone layer thickness: 40–50 cm per step

- Mortar: 1:6 cement-sand mix or lime-based for traditional construction

3.2 Bearing Capacity Check

Use local soil test data or presumptive values. For safe design in Bali’s common soils:

\[

q_{all} \geq \frac{\text{Total Load per meter}}{\text{Width } B}

\]

3.3 Construction Sequence

1. Excavation to stable stratum

2. Leveling and compaction of base

3. Placement of larger stones at base

4. Stepped construction with through-stones for bonding

5. Backfilling with proper drainage layer

3.4 Seismic and Tropical Considerations

Install continuous tie beams at plinth level. Provide adequate drainage to prevent water accumulation around the foundation. In high groundwater areas, consider raising the plinth or adding gravel backfill.

4. Case Studies from Field Experience

In Bali’s rural and suburban single-story homes, rubble stone foundations with 80 cm width and 80 cm depth have shown excellent performance over decades, even during seismic events, when constructed with proper stepping and drainage. Projects that followed recommended dimensions reported zero major settlement issues compared to undersized foundations.

5. Challenges and Mitigation Strategies

Common challenges include poor stone quality, inadequate bonding, erosion due to heavy rain, and differential settlement on uneven terrain. Mitigation: use hard, angular stones; ensure proper mortar mix; install French drains; and conduct basic soil testing before construction.

1. Pendahuluan

Untuk bangunan rumah tinggal satu lantai, pondasi batu kali menawarkan solusi tradisional namun andal yang menyeimbangkan biaya, ketersediaan material lokal, dan performa struktural. Di banyak daerah Indonesia, termasuk Bali, jenis pondasi ini masih dipilih karena kesederhanaan dan ekonomisnya ketika daya dukung tanah memadai (biasanya 0,8–2,0 kg/cm² atau lebih).

Parameter desain dasar meliputi:

- Lebar pondasi (B): biasanya 60–90 cm

- Kedalaman di bawah permukaan tanah (D): minimum 60–80 cm

- Ketebalan pasangan batu: 40–60 cm

Daya dukung yang diizinkan diperiksa menggunakan persamaan bearing capacity Terzaghi atau nilai empiris lokal.

Di zona gempa seperti Bali, pertimbangan tambahan untuk daktilitas dan balok pengikat sangat penting. Makalah ini menyusun dimensi standar berdasarkan pengalaman lapangan dan panduan internasional.

Tantangan umum meliputi kualitas batu yang buruk, ikatan yang tidak memadai, erosi akibat hujan deras, dan penurunan diferensial pada lahan tidak rata. Mitigasi: gunakan batu keras dan bersudut; pastikan campuran mortar yang tepat; pasang drainase French; dan lakukan pengujian tanah dasar sebelum konstruksi.
