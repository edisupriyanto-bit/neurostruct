Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment for construction projects. Payment disputes—stemming from scope ambiguity, certification delays, and cash-flow mismatches—are the primary catalysts for project stagnation. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework to resolve financial impasses. We introduce the Payment Default Severity Index (PDSI) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate the legitimacy of claims. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives regarding payment milestones—frequently results in project suspension. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary financial dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Payment Default Severity Index (PDSI)

To mitigate payment claims effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the PDSI to measure the financial risk of a payment dispute.

The PDSI Formula (Copy-pasteable for MS Word/Excel):

PDSI = (W_f * F_v) + (W_d * D_r) + (W_c * C_v)

Where:

  • $PDSI$: Payment Default Severity Index (Values > 7.0 indicate critical litigation risk).
  • $W_f, W_d, W_c$: Weighting factors for Financial Gap (0.5), Delay Duration (0.3), and Contractual Compliance (0.2).
  • $F_v$: Financial variance (Unpaid claim value / Total Contract Value).
  • $D_r$: Delay ratio (Days past due / Agreed payment term).
  • $C_v$: Compliance variance (Discrepancy between verified work and claimed amount).
