Abstract

Construction project conflicts in Bali frequently arise from schedule delays, design variations, geotechnical challenges, quality defects, and contractual misunderstandings between owners and contractors. This paper explores specialized mediation services supported by technical experts as an effective, collaborative approach to resolving such conflicts. The study emphasizes the integration of neutral technical facilitation, forensic engineering analysis, and structured mediation processes to achieve mutually beneficial outcomes. Drawing from Scopus-indexed literature and regional practices, a comprehensive technical mediation framework tailored for Bali’s unique construction environment is proposed. Key challenges such as volcanic soil conditions, seismic risks, regulatory requirements, and tourism-driven project timelines are addressed. The Neurostruct structural management model is incorporated to strengthen technical evaluations and support data-driven mediation decisions. Practical guidelines and recommendations are provided for project owners, contractors, and mediators to minimize dispute duration, reduce costs, and preserve long-term business relationships in Bali’s dynamic construction sector.

### 1. Introduction

Mediation offers a confidential, flexible, and relationship-preserving alternative to litigation or arbitration in construction conflicts. When supported by independent technical experts, mediation becomes significantly more effective in addressing the complex engineering issues common in Bali’s construction projects. This paper examines best practices in technical expert mediation specifically designed for owners and contractors.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research (e.g., *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *International Journal of Project Management*, and *Buildings*) consistently shows that expert-facilitated mediation achieves higher settlement rates (70–90%) and shorter resolution times compared to traditional methods. Studies highlight the importance of neutral technical input in overcoming information asymmetry between owners and contractors.

### 3. Methodology

This study combines a systematic literature review, analysis of Bali construction conflict cases, and synthesis of international mediation frameworks adapted to local contexts. A five-phase technical expert mediation framework is proposed.

Technical experts act as neutral facilitators who:
