Abstract

Construction failures in buildings and infrastructure projects often lead to significant economic losses, safety concerns, and complex disputes among stakeholders. This paper presents a comprehensive examination of technical evaluation services for construction failures and their critical role in dispute resolution. Through systematic forensic engineering investigations, root cause analysis, failure mode identification, and liability assessment, professional technical evaluations provide objective evidence essential for mediation, arbitration, and litigation. The study synthesizes findings from Scopus-indexed literature and international case studies to propose an integrated evaluation framework. Emphasis is placed on challenges in seismically active and rapidly developing regions such as Bali, Indonesia, where volcanic soil conditions, environmental factors, and accelerated construction timelines increase failure risks. The Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to enhance diagnostic precision and evidential robustness. Practical guidelines and recommendations are offered to help project stakeholders achieve efficient, fair, and technically sound resolutions while minimizing future risks.

### 1. Introduction

Construction failures—ranging from minor defects to major structural collapses—frequently trigger disputes concerning responsibility, repair costs, and project delays. Technical evaluation by qualified engineers transforms these incidents from adversarial conflicts into solvable engineering problems through objective, evidence-based analysis.

In Bali, the combination of challenging geotechnical conditions, high seismic activity, and rapid tourism infrastructure development heightens the likelihood of construction failures and associated disputes. This paper details methodologies, frameworks, and best practices for technical failure evaluation in dispute resolution.

### 2. Literature Review

Forensic evaluation of construction failures is well-documented in high-impact Scopus-indexed journals. Studies emphasize systematic approaches to failure investigation, including the integration of non-destructive testing, computational modeling, and delay analysis. Research in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Forensic Engineering* (ICE), and *Engineering Failure Analysis* highlights that rigorous technical evaluations significantly improve dispute settlement rates and reduce resolution costs by providing clear causation pathways.

### 3. Methodology

This study adopts a systematic literature review of peer-reviewed sources (2015–2025), analysis of failure case studies, and synthesis of international forensic standards (ASCE, RILEM, and Indonesian SNI). ### 4. Key Methods in Technical Failure Evaluation

4.1 Structural Failure Assessment

Detailed inspection using visual surveys, non-destructive testing (NDT), and finite element modeling.

4.2 Delay and Productivity Failure Analysis

Forensic schedule review to separate failure-induced delays from other causes.

Equation 1 (Clean copy-paste for Word):

Failure-Induced Delay (days) = Actual Completion − Planned Completion (adjusted for failure event)

Causation Ratio = (Failure-Related Critical Days / Total Delay Days)

Liability Allocation (%) = f(Contract Clauses, Technical Causation Proof)

4.3 Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

Application of techniques such as 5-Why, Fishbone Diagram, and Fault Tree Analysis to identify primary and contributing factors.

4.4 Geotechnical Failure Evaluation

Critical in Bali for assessing settlement, slope stability, and foundation performance on volcanic soils.
