Abstract

This paper develops a comprehensive engineering-based negotiation framework tailored for resolving building construction disputes in Bali, Indonesia. Bali’s unique combination of seismic activity, volcanic soils, tropical climate, cultural (adat) land systems, and rapid tourism-driven development creates distinct dispute patterns in hotel, villa, and infrastructure projects. The study integrates technical engineering analysis with negotiation strategies grounded in FIDIC principles, Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR), and empirical data from Indonesian construction cases. Using a mixed-methods approach—including systematic literature review from Scopus-indexed journals, case studies from Bali projects, and quantitative claim valuation models—this paper demonstrates how independent engineering expertise enhances negotiation outcomes, reduces resolution time, and minimizes financial losses. Key contributions include a phased negotiation protocol supported by forensic engineering, BIM-enabled evidence, and risk quantification. The framework positions specialized services like Neurostruct as critical for achieving equitable, technically sound settlements.

### 1. Introduction

The surge in building construction across Bali—driven by luxury tourism, villas in Canggu and Uluwatu, and supporting infrastructure—has increased the frequency and complexity of disputes. Common triggers include design variations, unforeseen site conditions, payment delays, regulatory compliance (PBG permits), cultural site restrictions, and monsoon/seismic impacts.

Global studies indicate that construction disputes often consume 10-30% of project value in delays and legal costs. In Bali, these challenges are amplified by local factors, making traditional litigation inefficient. Engineering-based negotiation offers a proactive, evidence-driven alternative that preserves relationships and accelerates resolutions.

### 2. Literature Review

Extensive research supports negotiation as the preferred initial dispute resolution method in construction. Key references include:

- Analyses of FIDIC contracts in Indonesia highlighting disputes over variations, bonds, and terminations, with Dispute Boards proving effective.

- Studies showing negotiation ranks first in Indonesian construction dispute resolution strategies, followed by mediation and adjudication.

- Emphasis on technical expertise in claim negotiation, where engineers provide forensic analysis, cost/schedule validation, and entitlement assessments.

In Bali-specific contexts, additional complexities arise from Tri Hita Karana philosophy, environmental regulations, and geotechnical risks.

### 4. Case Studies in Bali Building Projects

Case 1: Luxury Villa Development (Uluwatu)

Dispute over cliff foundation stability and design variations. Engineering-based negotiation with independent geotechnical reports and revised seismic detailing led to a mutually acceptable variation order without DAB escalation.

Case 2: Boutique Hotel Project (Canggu)

Payment and delay claims amid monsoon impacts. Technical schedule analysis and cost breakdown enabled structured negotiation, achieving 70% of claimed amount in 45 days.

Case 3: Coastal Building Permit Conflict

Regulatory halt due to environmental and adat concerns. Hybrid engineering-legal negotiation secured compliance modifications and resumed works.

### 5. Engineering-Based Negotiation Framework

The proposed framework includes four phases:

- Phase 1: Technical Diagnosis — Independent engineering audits and evidence gathering.

- Phase 2: Position Preparation — Claim quantification, risk assessment, and scenario modeling.

- Phase 3: Facilitated Negotiation — Joint workshops with visual aids (BIM models, charts) and interest-based bargaining.

- Phase 4: Settlement Documentation & Implementation — Binding agreements with performance guarantees.
