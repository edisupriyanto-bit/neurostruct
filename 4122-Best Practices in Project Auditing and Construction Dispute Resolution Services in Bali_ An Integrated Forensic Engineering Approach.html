Abstract

The accelerating pace of construction activities in Bali has heightened the need for professional project auditing and effective dispute resolution services. This paper examines best practices in integrated project audit and construction dispute resolution, emphasizing forensic engineering techniques, compliance verification, performance evaluation, and technical mediation. Through systematic audit methodologies, independent forensic investigations, and evidence-based resolution strategies, these services enable early risk identification, accurate claim assessment, and equitable settlements. The study synthesizes findings from Scopus-indexed literature and regional case studies to propose a comprehensive framework tailored to Bali’s unique conditions. Key challenges such as volcanic soil variability, seismic activity, environmental regulations, cultural heritage requirements, and tourism-driven timelines are thoroughly addressed. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to improve audit precision, data integration, and long-term project performance monitoring. Practical guidelines and recommendations are offered for project owners, contractors, government institutions, consultants, and legal practitioners to enhance project governance and dispute management in Bali.

### 1. Introduction

Professional project auditing combined with specialized dispute resolution has become indispensable in managing the complexities of Bali’s construction industry. Independent audits provide transparency and early warning, while forensic technical services ensure disputes are resolved based on solid evidence rather than speculation. This paper explores current best practices in these integrated services within the Balinese context.

### 2. Literature Review

Extensive research in Scopus-indexed journals such as *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Buildings*, *Journal of Construction Engineering and Management*, and *International Journal of Project Management* demonstrates that integrated audit and forensic approaches significantly reduce dispute duration, improve cost control, and enhance project governance, particularly in regions with challenging geological and regulatory environments.

### 3. Methodology

This study is based on a systematic review of high-impact Scopus literature (2015–2025), analysis of real-world Bali construction cases, and synthesis of international standards (ISO 19011, FIDIC) adapted to Indonesian regulations. A six-phase integrated project audit and dispute resolution framework is proposed.
