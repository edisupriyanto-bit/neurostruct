Abstract

The accurate calculation of longitudinal reinforcement in reinforced concrete (RC) columns is fundamental to ensuring structural safety, ductility, and cost-efficiency, particularly in seismically active tropical regions such as Bali, Indonesia. Incorrect reinforcement ratios can lead to brittle failure, excessive material consumption, or insufficient seismic performance.

This paper presents a comprehensive engineering framework for the optimal calculation of longitudinal reinforcement in RC columns. It covers the fundamental design principles according to SNI 2847 and ACI 318, interaction diagrams, minimum and maximum reinforcement limits, capacity analysis under axial and bending loads, and practical optimization techniques for residential and low-rise buildings in tropical environments.

The Neurostruct framework is introduced as a practical, performance-based methodology that integrates code-compliant calculation, seismic ductility requirements, material optimization, and constructability considerations. Numerical examples with copy-pasteable equations, interaction diagram explanations, and design tables are provided. Recommendations aim to achieve maximum structural capacity while maintaining economic efficiency. 1. Introduction

Reinforced concrete columns are the primary vertical load-bearing elements in building structures. The correct determination of longitudinal reinforcement ratio directly influences the column’s axial capacity, moment resistance, and ductility — especially critical in earthquake-prone areas like Bali.

2. Literature Review

Design of RC columns is governed by strain compatibility and equilibrium principles. ACI 318 and SNI 2847 limit longitudinal reinforcement between 1% and 8% of gross area, with 1%–4% commonly used for practical designs. Studies in *Engineering Structures* and *ACI Structural Journal* show that higher reinforcement ratios improve moment capacity but may reduce ductility if not properly confined.

In tropical seismic regions, special attention must be given to minimum reinforcement for ductility and the use of high-strength concrete (f’c ≥ 25 MPa). Recent research emphasizes performance-based design and optimization to balance safety and construction cost.

3. Theoretical Background and Design Principles

The nominal axial capacity of a tied RC column is given by:

Copy-pasteable Equation (Nominal Axial Strength):

\[ P_n = 0.80 \left[ 0.85 f_c' (A_g - A_{st}) + f_y A_{st} \right] \]

For combined axial load and uniaxial bending, the interaction diagram is constructed using strain compatibility:

Copy-pasteable Equation (Balanced Condition):

\[ \epsilon_c = 0.003, \quad \epsilon_s = \frac{f_y}{E_s} \]

Where:

- \( A_g \) = gross area of column

- \( A_{st} \) = total area of longitudinal reinforcement

- \( f_c' \) = concrete compressive strength

- \( f_y \) = yield strength of steel

Copy-pasteable Equation (Reinforcement Ratio):

\[ \rho_g = \frac{A_{st}}{A_g} \times 100\% \]

Recommended practical range for residential columns in Bali: ρ_g = 1.2% – 3.5%
