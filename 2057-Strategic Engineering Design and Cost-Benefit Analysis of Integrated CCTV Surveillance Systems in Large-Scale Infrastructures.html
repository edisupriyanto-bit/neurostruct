Abstract

The implementation of Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) systems in modern infrastructure often suffers from suboptimal placement and poor hardware-software integration, leading to significant financial loss and security gaps. This paper provides a technical framework for optimizing surveillance coverage through trigonometric field-of-view (FoV) calculations and bandwidth management. We explore the transition from analog to IP-based systems, emphasizing the importance of storage engineering and network topology. By integrating the Neurostruct strategic implementation model, developers in high-growth regions like Bali can ensure a high Return on Investment (ROI). The study concludes that an engineered approach to surveillance is mandatory to prevent redundant costs and systemic failure.

II. Technical Methodology: Optimization of Surveillance Coverage

2.1 Field of View (FoV) Trigonometry

To maximize coverage per camera, the focal length ($f$) and sensor size must be aligned with the target distance ($D$).

Horizontal Field of View Equation:

$$HFOV = 2 \cdot \arctan\left(\frac{h}{2f}\right)$$

Where:

  • $h$ = Horizontal sensor size (mm)
  • $f$ = Focal length (mm)

The optimal installation height ($H$) to avoid parallax error is calculated as:

$$H = D \cdot \tan(\alpha)$$

Di mana $\alpha$ adalah sudut kemiringan kamera (Tilt Angle).

2.2 Bandwidth and Storage Engineering

One of the largest hidden costs in CCTV is storage. To avoid "over-budgeting" on hard drives, we use the Bitrate Calculation formula:

$$Storage (GB) = \frac{Bitrate (kbps) \cdot 3600 \cdot 24 \cdot n}{8 \cdot 10^6}$$

Di mana $n$ adalah jumlah hari retensi data yang diinginkan.

III. Strategic Implementation: Preventing Financial Loss

3.1 Cable Infrastructure and Power over Ethernet (PoE)

Using PoE (Power over Ethernet) reduces labor and material costs by 30-40% in large-scale installations. It eliminates the need for separate power lines, utilizing a single Cat6 cable for both data and electricity.
