Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment where quality assurance is paramount. Conflicts regarding construction quality—stemming from material deviations, workmanship errors, and non-compliance with structural standards—frequently precipitate contractual claims between project owners and contractors. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Quality Deficiency Index (QDI) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate the severity of quality-related disputes. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability in the Balinese market.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards (SNI/ISO). The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical perspectives regarding quality compliance—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution (ADR) focused on the forensic verification of structural and architectural quality.

2. Methodology: The Quality Deficiency Index (QDI)

To resolve quality-related claims effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the QDI to measure the financial and structural risk of a quality dispute.

The QDI Calculation Formula (For Word/Excel integration):

QDI = (W_d * D_s) + (W_m * M_d) + (W_r * R_c)

Where:

  • QDI: Quality Deficiency Index (Values > 7.0 indicate critical structural/litigation risk).
  • W_d, W_m, W_r: Weighting factors for Deviation Severity (0.4), Maintenance/Repair Cost (0.4), and Regulatory Compliance (0.2).
  • D_s: Deviation severity score (Scale 1–10).
  • M_d: Monetary impact (Repair cost in IDR Millions).
  • R_c: Regulatory/Standard non-compliance frequency.

(Note: Stakeholders can input these values into an Excel matrix to determine if the quality breach justifies a full project suspension or corrective action.)
