1. Introduction

Interior painting (pengecatan interior) is the final visible layer that protects substrates (plaster, gypsum board, concrete, or wood), enhances aesthetics, and contributes to healthy indoor environments. In tropical regions like Bali, high relative humidity (often 70–90%), frequent rainfall, temperature swings, and airborne salts in coastal areas accelerate coating degradation through moisture ingress, fungal growth, and thermal expansion mismatches.

High-quality interior painting requires rigorous surface preparation, appropriate material selection, controlled environmental conditions during application, and systematic quality inspection. This paper synthesizes theoretical principles, practical techniques, defect analysis, and performance evaluation aligned with international research and relevant SNI standards for paints and varnishes.

2. Literature Review

Significant research exists on coating performance in tropical climates. Studies in *Progress in Organic Coatings* and *Building and Environment* highlight the impact of humidity on adhesion loss and mold proliferation. Research on low-VOC acrylic emulsions demonstrates superior breathability and reduced blistering compared to older solvent-based systems.

Common defects documented include:

- Blistering and peeling due to trapped moisture

- Mold and mildew growth on porous surfaces

- Cracking and flaking from substrate movement or poor film flexibility

- Yellowing or fading under UV exposure (even indoors through windows)

Indonesian references cover SNI specifications for emulsion paints (e.g., SNI 7669:2012 or updated equivalents) emphasizing washability, hiding power, and VOC limits. Bali-specific challenges include rapid drying in dry seasons causing poor leveling and prolonged dampness promoting microbial attack. Recent Scopus papers advocate the use of anti-fungal additives, silica-based breathable coatings, and proper ventilation strategies for tropical buildings.

3. Theoretical Background and Quality Principles

# 3.1 Surface Preparation and Adhesion

Proper preparation is fundamental. The substrate must achieve a surface roughness that promotes mechanical interlocking. Adhesion strength can be evaluated using pull-off tests:

\[ f_b = \frac{P}{A} \]

where \( f_b \) is bond strength (MPa), \( P \) is the maximum tensile force at failure (N), and \( A \) is the dolly area (mm²). Target minimum adhesion for interior coatings is typically 1.0–1.5 MPa on sound plaster.

Moisture content of the substrate should be below 8–12% before painting (measured with moisture meters).

# 3.2 Coating Film Formation and Thickness

Dry film thickness (DFT) is critical for durability. Recommended total DFT for high-quality interior systems:

- Primer: 30–50 μm

- Undercoat: 40–60 μm

- Topcoat (2 layers): 50–80 μm each

The hiding power and opacity are related to pigment volume concentration (PVC). Optimal PVC for interior latex paints is usually 40–60%.

# 3.3 Environmental Factors in Tropical Application

Application should occur when ambient temperature is 20–32°C and relative humidity <85%. The dew point differential must be considered to avoid condensation:

Dew point temperature \( T_d \) can be approximated using Magnus formula (simplified):

\[ T_d \approx \frac{b \cdot \gamma}{a - \gamma} \]

where \( \gamma = \ln(RH/100) + \frac{a \cdot T}{b + T} \), with constants \( a = 17.27 \), \( b = 237.7^\circ \)C, \( T \) in °C, and RH in % (for rough estimation only; use calibrated instruments in practice).

4. High-Quality Interior Painting Techniques

# 4.1 Material Selection

- Primer: Alkali-resistant primer for new plaster or concrete to block efflorescence; acrylic primer for gypsum board.

- Topcoat: Premium low-VOC acrylic latex with anti-fungal and washable properties (Class 1 scrub resistance per SNI).

- Specialty coatings: Mold-resistant, low-sheen for high-humidity areas (bathrooms), or eggshell/matte for living spaces.

Avoid cheap high-VOC paints that compromise indoor air quality.

# 4.2 Application Process

1. Substrate inspection and repair: Fill cracks, level surfaces, sand smooth.

2. Cleaning: Remove dust, grease, and loose particles.

3. Priming: Apply uniformly; allow full drying (4–6 hours).

4. Undercoat/Topcoat: Apply 2–3 thin coats using roller or spray for even coverage. Sand lightly between coats for smoothness.

5. Environmental control: Use dehumidifiers and fans in Bali’s humid conditions; ensure good ventilation.

# 4.3 Quality Inspection and Defect Prevention

Inspection stages: after priming, between coats, and at final acceptance (7–14 days after completion).

Checklist criteria:

- Uniform color and sheen (no holidays or lap marks)

- No visible defects (blisters, runs, sags)

- Adhesion test on sample areas

- Washability and scrub resistance verification

Common tropical defects and prevention:

- Mold: Use anti-fungal paint + ensure <60% indoor RH long-term

- Blistering: Proper drying time between coats + moisture-free substrate

- Cracking: Flexible coatings + control substrate movement through proper plaster quality

5. Performance Evaluation in Bali Construction Context

In Bali villas and hotels, high-quality interior painting must withstand daily cleaning, high occupancy humidity, and occasional salt-laden air. Case patterns show that premium acrylic systems with proper preparation last 8–12 years before recoating, compared to 3–5 years for economy paints.

Integration with overall finishing works (after high-quality plastering) is essential. BIM-based defect mapping can further enhance quality documentation.
