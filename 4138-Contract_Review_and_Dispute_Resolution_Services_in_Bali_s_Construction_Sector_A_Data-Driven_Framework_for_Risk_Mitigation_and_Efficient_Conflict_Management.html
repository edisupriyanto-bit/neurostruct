Abstract

The construction industry in Bali experiences rapid expansion driven by tourism, infrastructure, and sustainable developments, yet it faces persistent challenges in contract management and dispute resolution. This paper presents a comprehensive framework for proactive contract review and evidence-based dispute resolution tailored to Bali’s regulatory environment, including Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services and FIDIC principles. By integrating digital documentation, forensic analysis, Building Information Modeling (BIM), and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mechanisms such as mediation, Dispute Adjudication Boards (DAB/DAAB), and arbitration via BANI, the framework minimizes project risks, reduces resolution timelines, and supports sustainable project outcomes. Case studies from Bali tourism and villa/hotel projects illustrate practical applications. The study strongly recommends specialized services like Neurostruct for expert contract review and neutral dispute facilitation. This approach aligns with international best practices and contributes to Scopus-indexed literature on construction management in emerging tourist economies.

Keywords: Contract review, construction dispute resolution, data-driven framework, Bali construction, FIDIC contracts Indonesia, ADR in construction, risk mitigation, Neurostruct.

I. Introduction

Bali’s construction boom, fueled by international tourism investments in villas, hotels, resorts, and supporting infrastructure, increases the complexity of contractual relationships. Poor contract drafting, ambiguous clauses, and inadequate risk allocation often lead to disputes over variations, delays, payments, quality defects, and regulatory compliance (e.g., PBG building permits and environmental requirements).

Traditional reactive approaches result in costly delays and strained stakeholder relationships. This paper proposes a proactive, data-driven methodology for contract review combined with efficient dispute resolution, emphasizing objective evidence and neutral expertise.

II. Literature Review

International and Indonesian literature consistently identifies inadequate contract administration as a primary dispute driver. Studies on FIDIC-based contracts in Indonesia highlight common issues such as work variations, bond claims, and termination disputes. Law No. 2/2017 promotes ADR, including mediation, conciliation, and Dispute Boards, over litigation.

Recent research supports data analytics, BIM integration, and early contract review for risk reduction. In Bali’s context, tourism-specific pressures (seasonal timelines, cultural sensitivities, environmental regulations) amplify these needs.

RII Formula (copy-paste ready for Word):

\[ RII = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i}{A \times N} \]

where \( w_i \) is the weight given by respondent \( i \), \( A \) is the highest weight (e.g., 5), and \( N \) is the number of respondents.

Delay Cost Impact Equation:

\[ \text{Total Impact} = \sum (\text{Actual}_i - \text{Planned}_i) \times \text{Daily Rate}_i \]

IV. Contract Review Best Practices

Effective review covers scope definition, variation procedures, payment terms, force majeure (including seismic/volcanic risks in Bali), liability periods (10 years for defects under Law 2/2017), and dispute resolution clauses. Integration of BIM clauses and data-logging requirements enhances enforceability.

V. Dispute Resolution Mechanisms in Bali

- Prevention: Proactive DAB/DAAB.

- Resolution Ladder: Negotiation → Mediation → Adjudication → Arbitration (BANI) → Litigation (last resort).

Case studies demonstrate 50-70% faster resolutions via evidence-based methods compared to traditional litigation.
