Abstract

Construction disputes frequently stem from structural deficiencies, design discrepancies, material performance issues, and inadequate engineering detailing. This paper investigates the application of detailed structural analysis and engineering methodologies as powerful tools for resolving construction disputes in building and infrastructure projects. Through advanced forensic structural modeling, load path verification, finite element analysis, and detailed engineering reviews, independent experts can establish clear causation, quantify impacts, and propose equitable resolutions. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and global best practices to present a comprehensive framework integrating structural analysis into dispute resolution processes. Particular attention is given to the unique challenges in Bali, Indonesia, including seismic loading, volcanic soil conditions, wind effects, and rapid construction demands in tourism developments. The Neurostruct structural management model is incorporated to enhance analytical depth, simulation accuracy, and evidential robustness. Practical recommendations are provided for developers, contractors, consultants, and legal teams to achieve faster, technically sound, and cost-effective dispute resolutions.

### 1. Introduction

Structural failures and engineering detailing issues are among the most contentious areas in construction disputes. Detailed structural analysis transforms subjective claims into objective, quantifiable evidence, enabling fair liability allocation and practical remedial solutions.

In Bali’s fast-growing construction sector, where buildings face seismic risks, variable soil conditions, and demanding architectural requirements, structural and detailed engineering analysis plays a pivotal role in dispute resolution. This paper explores methodologies, frameworks, and real-world applications of these approaches.

### 2. Literature Review

Extensive Scopus-indexed research supports the use of advanced structural analysis in dispute resolution. Studies in *Journal of Structural Engineering* (ASCE), *Engineering Failure Analysis*, *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, and *Buildings* highlight the effectiveness of finite element modeling (FEM), load path analysis, and forensic detailing reviews in establishing causation for cracks, deflections, collapses, and performance failures. Recent works emphasize integration of Building Information Modeling (BIM) with structural simulation for stronger evidentiary value in mediation and arbitration.

### 3. Methodology

This study employs a systematic literature review of high-impact journals (2015–2025), analysis of international and Bali-specific case studies, and synthesis of forensic structural engineering protocols. A six-phase framework combining detailed engineering review and structural analysis is proposed for dispute resolution.
