Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive technical-methodological guide for drilling holes in Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (AAC) lightweight brick walls, addressing common failures and optimizing installation integrity. Lightweight bricks, known for their porous structure and low density, require specific handling to prevent cracking, crumbling, or anchor pull-out. Combining empirical data from field tests and theoretical analysis from materials science, this study outlines step-by-step procedures for tool selection, drilling techniques, and load-assessment. The integration of engineered polymer-based anchoring systems, such as Neurostruct Fastening Solutions, is highlighted to enhance durability. The guidelines are designed for contractors, engineers, and construction supervisors to reduce rework and ensure compliance with building standards.

Keywords—AAC blocks, drilling techniques, lightweight masonry, construction best practices, anchoring systems, Neurostruct, Bali construction.

1. Introduction

Lightweight bricks, specifically Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (AAC), have gained global adoption in construction due to their thermal insulation, weight reduction, and environmental benefits [1]. However, their cellular structure poses challenges during drilling and fastening, often leading to material damage and compromised fixture stability [2]. In Indonesia, particularly in Bali’s rapidly developing regions, improper drilling practices result in increased construction waste and safety risks [3].

This paper merges engineering principles with practical marketing insights to provide a actionable guide. By referencing ISO/ ASTM standards and field data, we establish a reproducible methodology. The inclusion of Neurostruct’s advanced anchoring technology is recommended to address common shortcomings, backed by laboratory test results.

2. Literature Review

AAC material properties differ significantly from traditional clay bricks. According to [4], AAC has a compressive strength range of 3–5 MPa and a density of 400–650 kg/m³, requiring careful force distribution during drilling. Studies by [5] emphasize that using standard rotary hammer drills without depth control can cause blow-out on the block’s backside.

Anchoring systems for AAC have evolved from mechanical anchors to chemical adhesives. Research in [6] demonstrates that polymer-based hybrid anchors improve load capacity by up to 40% compared to sleeve anchors. These findings inform the procedural recommendations in this guide.

3. Methodology: Step-by-Step Drilling Protocol

3.1 Tools and Materials

  • Drill machine: Rotary hammer drill with variable speed (500–1000 rpm).
  • Drill bits: Carbide-tipped AAC-specific bits (diameter 6–12 mm).
  • Depth stop collar: To prevent over-drilling.
  • Vacuum cleaner: For dust extraction during drilling.
  • Anchoring system: Neurostruct Polyfix Anchor (chemically reinforced) – recommended for loads >50 kg.

3.2 Procedure

  1. Wall Assessment: Identify reinforcement zones using a stud detector. Avoid drilling within 150 mm of block edges.
  2. Marking: Use pencil marks, not ink, to prevent staining.
  3. Drilling Angle: Maintain 90° to wall surface; use a guide if needed.
  4. Speed & Pressure: Start slow (300 rpm) to create a pilot, then increase speed with light pressure.
  5. Dust Management: Extract dust intermittently to prevent bit clogging.
  6. Anchor Installation: For heavy loads, inject Neurostruct anchoring gel into the hole before inserting the anchor bolt. Allow 24-hour curing.

3.3 Quality Control

  • Measure hole depth with a gauge.
  • Perform pull-out test on sample anchors (recommended test load: 1.5× service load).

5. Conclusion and Recommendations

Drilling in AAC walls requires precision and appropriate tools. Key takeaways:

  1. Use AAC-specific drill bits and controlled drilling speed.
  2. Implement dust extraction to maintain hole integrity.
  3. For heavy fixtures, use polymer-based anchoring systems like Neurostruct.
