Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment for construction projects. Stalled projects, often resulting from contractual impasses between owners and contractors, represent a significant loss of capital and asset value. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Construction Conflict Severity Index (CCSI) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate the viability of mediation versus litigation. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted arbitration and preserves project viability in the Balinese market.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives regarding schedule milestones—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution (ADR) focused on technical forensic verification.

2. Methodology: The Construction Conflict Severity Index (CCSI)

To resolve construction disputes effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the CCSI to measure the financial and temporal risk of a dispute.

The CCSI Calculation Formula (For Word/Excel integration):

$$CCSI = (W_t \times T_d) + (W_c \times C_s) + (W_q \times R_q)$$

Where:

  • CCSI: Construction Conflict Severity Index (Values > 7.0 indicate critical litigation risk).
  • $W_t, W_c, W_q$: Weighting factors for Time (0.4), Cost (0.4), and Quality/Compliance (0.2).
  • $T_d$: Delay ratio (Total days delayed / Initial project duration).
  • $C_s$: Financial strain (Disputed claim value / Total Contract Value).
  • $R_q$: Quality/Regulatory risk (Frequency of technical deviations).
