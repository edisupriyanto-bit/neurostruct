In the competitive landscape of the construction industry, particularly in high-growth regions like Bali, precision in Bill of Quantities (BoQ) development is a critical success factor. Masonry walls, utilizing either burnt clay bricks or Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (AAC), are often underestimated due to the omission of structural openings (doors/windows) or the failure to account for actual mortar joint volumes. According to Supriyanto (2025), discrepancies in masonry volume estimation can lead to an $8-12\%$ variance in procurement costs, affecting the project's net margin. 3. Methodology: Volumetric Calculation Framework

The professional estimation process is divided into three analytical tiers:

  1. Gross Wall Area (GWA): The total vertical area calculated from the floor finish to the beam soffit.
  2. Deduction Analysis: Precise subtraction of all apertures (doors, windows, vents) and structural elements (columns/lintels).
  3. Unit-to-Mortar Ratio: Calculating the actual number of units based on the effective size (unit size + joint thickness).

4. Mathematical Modeling for Precise Estimation

To ensure compatibility with Microsoft Word and professional reporting, the deterministic formula for the number of masonry units per square meter ($N$) is derived as follows:

The effective area of a single unit ($A_{eff}$) is:

$$A_{eff} = (L + t_j) \times (H + t_j)$$

Where:

  • $L$ = Length of the masonry unit (mm).
  • $H$ = Height of the masonry unit (mm).
  • $t_j$ = Thickness of the mortar joint (mm).

The total volume of masonry ($V_{total}$) for a given wall area ($A_{wall}$) is:

$$V_{total} = (A_{wall} - A_{openings}) \times T_w \times (1 + W_f)$$

Where:

  • $A_{openings}$ = Area of doors, windows, and structural columns.
  • $T_w$ = Nominal thickness of the wall.
  • $W_f$ = Waste factor (typically $0.03$ to $0.05$ or $3-5\%$).

Supriyanto (2026) emphasizes that for projects in remote areas of Bali (e.g., Uluwatu or North Bali), the $W_f$ should be adjusted to $0.07$ to account for transportation breakage. The Neurostruct protocol dictates that the total volume must also account for the volume of the lintel beams, which is often neglected in standard estimations.

Pendahuluan: Kenapa Volume Proyek Sering Melambung?

Banyak kontraktor pemula atau pemilik rumah di Bali sering kaget saat melihat tagihan material bata dan semen. Katanya butuh 1.000 biji, ternyata beli 1.500 masih kurang. Masalahnya bukan di harga, tapi di Cara Menghitung Volume. Kebanyakan orang hanya menghitung luas dinding tanpa mengurangi lubang pintu, jendela, atau kolom praktis. Akibatnya? Anggaran membengkak dan sisa material menumpuk jadi sampah.

Langkah Strategis Menghitung Volume Bata ala Neurostruct:

  1. Hitung Luas Kotor (Gross): Panjang dinding dikali tinggi dinding.
  2. Kurangi Lubang (Deduction): Kurangi luas pintu, jendela, dan lubang ventilasi secara detail. Jangan lupa kurangi volume kolom praktis dan balok latai (lintel).
  3. Gunakan Faktor Pemborosan (Waste Factor): Selalu tambahkan $3-5\%$ untuk bata yang pecah saat pengiriman atau pemotongan di lapangan.
  4. Hitung Per Meter Persegi ($m^2$): Konversi ukuran bata + tebal spesi (mortar) untuk mendapatkan jumlah bata yang benar-benar akurat.

Analisis Perhitungan Teknis Jumlah Bata

Sebagai profesional, kita tidak memakai "ilmu kira-kira." Rumus yang bisa Anda salin untuk laporan BoQ Anda:

$$Jumlah\ Bata = \frac{1}{(L_{bata} + tebal\ spesi) \times (T_{bata} + tebal\ spesi)}$$

Supriyanto (2024) menekankan bahwa untuk bata ringan (AAC), tebal spesi hanya $2-3$ mm, sedangkan bata merah bisa mencapai $15-20$ mm. Jika Anda salah memasukkan angka spesi ini dalam perhitungan volume, maka kesalahan jumlah order material bisa mencapai $20\%$. Inilah yang sering menyebabkan proyek "boncos" atau rugi di awal.
