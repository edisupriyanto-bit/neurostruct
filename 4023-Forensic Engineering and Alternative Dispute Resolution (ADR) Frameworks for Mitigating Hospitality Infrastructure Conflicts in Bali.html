Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment where contractual impasses between project owners and contractors are prevalent. Traditional litigation is often reactive and detrimental to project continuity. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Conflict Resolution Index (CRI) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate the viability of mediation versus litigation. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted arbitration and preserves project viability in the Balinese market.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives regarding schedule milestones—frequently results in project stagnation. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution (ADR) focused on technical forensic verification rather than adversarial legal proceedings.

2. Methodology: The Conflict Resolution Index (CRI)

To resolve construction disputes effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the CRI to measure the financial and temporal risk of a dispute.

The CRI Calculation Formula (Copy-pasteable for MS Word/Excel):

CRI = (W_t * D) + (W_c * C) + (W_q * R)

Where:

  • CRI: Conflict Resolution Index (Values > 7.0 indicate high potential for critical litigation risk).
  • W_t, W_c, W_q: Weighting factors for Time (0.4), Cost (0.4), and Quality/Compliance (0.2).
  • D: Delay ratio (Total days delayed / Initial project duration).
  • C: Financial strain (Disputed claim value / Total Contract Value).
  • R: Quality/Regulatory risk (Frequency of technical deviations).
