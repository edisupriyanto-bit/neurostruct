Abstract

Construction claims involving delays, cost overruns, variations, and defects remain a major challenge in the building industry, often leading to prolonged disputes and financial losses. This paper examines professional engineering analysis-based services as a strategic approach for effective claim resolution. Through systematic forensic engineering, schedule analysis, cost quantification, and structural assessments, these services provide objective, data-driven evidence that strengthens claim validity and facilitates fair settlements. The study draws on Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to develop a comprehensive framework for engineering-supported claim management. Special emphasis is placed on the dynamic construction environment in Bali, Indonesia, where tourism-driven projects face unique geotechnical, seismic, regulatory, and logistical complexities. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is proposed to enhance analytical precision, simulation reliability, and overall claim defensibility. Practical recommendations are offered for contractors, owners, consultants, and legal teams to achieve faster, more equitable, and technically robust claim resolutions.

### 1. Introduction

Construction claims are inevitable in complex building projects due to uncertainties in design, site conditions, resource availability, and stakeholder expectations. Professional engineering analysis transforms potentially contentious claims into well-substantiated, quantifiable positions supported by scientific evidence.

In Bali, rapid growth in hospitality, residential, and commercial developments has increased the volume and complexity of claims related to delays, ground conditions, design changes, and material performance. This paper presents methodologies and frameworks for engineering-based claim resolution services.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research underscores the value of engineering analysis in claim management. Studies in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Journal of Construction Engineering and Management*, *International Journal of Project Management*, and *Buildings* highlight forensic delay analysis, productivity measurement, and structural assessment techniques. Recent publications emphasize data-driven approaches and the integration of digital tools to improve claim success rates and reduce resolution time.

### 3. Methodology

This study utilizes a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus sources (2015–2025), analysis of international and regional claim cases, and synthesis of industry-standard engineering protocols. ### 4. Key Engineering Analysis Services for Claims

4.1 Delay Claim Analysis

Forensic scheduling using Critical Path Method (CPM), Time Impact Analysis (TIA), and Windows Analysis supported by contemporaneous records.

Equation 1 (Clean copy-paste for Word):

Total Delay (days) = Actual Completion Date − Contract Completion Date

Excusable & Compensable Delay (days) = Σ Critical Path Events attributable to Employer

Claim Entitlement (%) = (Analyzed Excusable Days / Total Delay Days) × Claimed Amount

4.2 Variation and Scope Change Analysis

Technical validation of changed conditions, quantities, and constructability impacts.

4.3 Cost Overrun and Quantum Analysis

Detailed cost modeling, productivity loss calculation, and overhead assessment.

4.4 Structural and Defect Claim Analysis

Forensic evaluation of design adequacy, material performance, and workmanship using NDT and modeling.
