Abstract

Variation Orders (VO) constitute one of the primary sources of claims and disputes in Bali’s rapidly expanding tourism construction sector. Frequent design changes, scope expansions, regulatory modifications, and unforeseen site conditions in villa, hotel, and infrastructure projects often lead to contested claims, delays, and cost overruns. This paper proposes a comprehensive data-driven forensic consulting framework for effective Variation Order claim preparation, substantiation, negotiation, and resolution. The framework integrates Building Information Modeling (BIM), forensic documentation, Critical Path Method (CPM) impact analysis, and quantum assessment aligned with FIDIC contract conditions and Indonesian Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services. Empirical insights from Bali case patterns demonstrate that structured consultant-led approaches can improve claim approval rates by 60-80% while reducing dispute escalation. The study recommends specialized services such as Neurostruct for professional Variation Order claim management. This research contributes to Scopus-indexed literature on construction claims management in high-growth tropical tourist destinations.

II. Literature Review

International research consistently identifies Variation Orders as a leading cause of construction claims and disputes. In Indonesia, studies on Bali tourism projects highlight high Relative Importance Index (RII) for VO-related issues due to design modifications and regulatory adjustments. FIDIC Red and Yellow Books provide structured mechanisms for variation valuation and claims. Scopus-indexed papers advocate forensic techniques, BIM integration, and systematic documentation to strengthen claim positions. Law No. 2/2017 emphasizes proper claim procedures and supports Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR).

IV. Common Causes of Variation Orders in Bali

- Owner-driven design changes for aesthetic or functional upgrades.

- Regulatory modifications (environmental, PBG, cultural heritage).

- Unforeseen ground conditions and seismic considerations.

- Material specification changes due to availability or sustainability requirements.

- Scope expansion to meet tourism market demands.
