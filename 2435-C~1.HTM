1. Introduction

Falls from heights are a leading cause of fatalities in construction, accounting for approximately 30–40% of all construction deaths globally. In Indonesia, including Bali’s rapidly growing infrastructure sector, similar trends are observed, particularly during roofing, scaffolding, and formwork activities.

Effective prevention follows the hierarchy of controls:

1. Elimination (avoid working at height where possible)

2. Substitution (use safer alternatives)

3. Engineering controls (guardrails, safety nets, platforms)

4. Administrative controls (training, work permits, supervision)

5. Personal Protective Equipment (full-body harness with lanyard)

Key regulatory references include SNI 7569:2011 (Safety Nets), SNI 8870:2019 (Personal Fall Arrest Systems), and alignment with OSHA 1926 Subpart M and ANSI Z359 standards.

The arresting force in a fall protection system must be limited:

\[

F_{max} \leq 6 \text{ kN (for full-body harness)}

\]

This paper synthesizes technical guidelines and field best practices for safe work at heights.

2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research consistently shows that proper use of collective protection (guardrails, nets) is more effective than personal systems. Studies in tropical regions highlight increased slip risks due to rain and humidity, necessitating non-slip surfaces and frequent equipment inspection. Recent papers on fall arrest systems emphasize energy absorbers and proper anchorage points to limit impact forces. In seismic zones, flexible systems that accommodate structural movement are recommended.

3. Methodology and Safety Protocols

3.1 Risk Assessment

Conduct Job Safety Analysis (JSA) for every height-related task. Calculate fall clearance:

\[

\text{Required Clearance} = \text{Free Fall Distance} + \text{Deceleration Distance} + \text{Harness Stretch} + \text{Safety Margin}

\]

3.2 Engineering Controls

- Guardrails: Minimum 1.0 m height with midrail and toeboard.

- Safety Nets: Installed as close as possible below the work level (max 6 m).

- Scaffolding: Designed per SNI and inspected daily.

3.3 Personal Fall Arrest Systems (PFAS)

- Full-body harness with shock-absorbing lanyard.

- Anchorage points must withstand 22.2 kN (5000 lbf).

- Rescue plan must be in place before work begins.

3.4 Training and Supervision

All workers at heights must receive competent person training. Daily toolbox meetings and continuous supervision are mandatory.

4. Case Studies in Tropical Construction

In Bali’s hotel and infrastructure projects, implementation of comprehensive fall protection programs (guardrails + PFAS + safety nets) resulted in zero height-related incidents over multi-year periods. Projects that neglected daily scaffolding inspections experienced near-misses, while those following SNI-aligned protocols achieved excellent safety records.

5. Challenges and Solutions in Tropical Environments

Challenges include frequent rain making surfaces slippery, high humidity causing corrosion of metal components, and dense sites limiting safe anchorage. Solutions:

- Use weather-resistant, non-slip scaffolding planks.

- Schedule height work during drier periods when possible.

- Implement frequent equipment inspection and replacement programs.

- Provide shaded rest areas and hydration protocols.

3. Metodologi dan Protokol Keselamatan

3.1 Penilaian Risiko

Lakukan Job Safety Analysis (JSA) untuk setiap tugas di ketinggian. Hitung clearance jatuh:

\[

\text{Required Clearance} = \text{Jarak Jatuh Bebas} + \text{Jarak Decelerasi} + \text{Peregangan Harness} + \text{Margin Keselamatan}

\]

3.2 Kontrol Rekayasa

- Guardrail: Tinggi minimal 1,0 m dengan midrail dan toeboard.

- Safety Net: Dipasang sedekat mungkin di bawah level kerja (maks 6 m).

- Scaffolding: Dirancang sesuai SNI dan diperiksa setiap hari.

3.3 Sistem Penangkap Jatuh Pribadi (PFAS)

- Full-body harness dengan lanyard penyerap energi.

- Titik jangkar harus menahan 22,2 kN.

- Rencana penyelamatan harus ada sebelum pekerjaan dimulai.
