1. Introduction

Tropical landscape design in residential and villa projects demands careful plant selection to create functional, beautiful, and resilient outdoor spaces. In Bali, where lush gardens are central to both traditional and modern living, contractors and developers frequently face challenges in choosing species that thrive in high humidity, intense sunlight, variable rainfall, and volcanic or coastal soils.

Incorrect plant selection leads to high mortality rates, excessive water and fertilizer use, frequent replacement, and failure to meet aesthetic or ecological goals. This paper addresses the often-underestimated professional approach to plant selection for tropical landscapes, providing engineering-oriented criteria and practical methods that contractors “wajib diketahui” (must know) for successful project delivery.

The content is structured to bridge horticultural knowledge with construction practice, ensuring selections support structural stability, drainage, erosion control, and long-term sustainability.

2. Literature Review

Recent studies emphasize multi-criteria frameworks for tropical plant selection, incorporating climatic tolerance, soil adaptability, water use efficiency, growth habits, and ecosystem services. Research in *Landscape and Urban Planning* and *Urban Forestry & Urban Greening* highlights the advantages of native or well-adapted species for reduced maintenance and higher biodiversity value.

In Southeast Asia and Bali specifically, papers discuss the use of layered planting (canopy trees, understory shrubs, ground covers) to mimic natural forest structures, improving microclimate and stormwater management. Challenges noted include salinity tolerance in coastal areas, wind resistance, and compatibility with construction timelines. Sustainable landscaping research stresses low-water, low-maintenance species to address water scarcity and climate resilience.

Indonesian and Balinese landscape practices increasingly integrate traditional elements (such as frangipani, heliconia, and bamboo) with modern performance criteria, supported by local guidelines for green open spaces.

3. Theoretical Background and Selection Criteria

#3.1 Multi-Criteria Evaluation Framework

Effective plant selection uses a weighted scoring system based on:

- Climatic suitability (temperature, humidity, rainfall)

- Soil adaptability (pH, drainage, nutrient requirements)

- Water efficiency (drought tolerance vs. water-loving)

- Growth rate and mature size

- Pest and disease resistance

- Aesthetic and functional value (color, texture, shade, fragrance)

- Maintenance requirements and lifecycle cost

A simple scoring matrix can be expressed as:

Total Score = Σ (Criterion Weight × Performance Score)

where weights sum to 1.0 and performance is rated 1–10.

#3.2 Layering Concept in Tropical Design

Effective tropical landscapes use vertical layering:

- Canopy layer: Tall trees for shade and structure (e.g., palms, frangipani)

- Mid-layer: Shrubs and small trees for color and screening

- Ground cover: Low plants for soil protection and weed suppression

This approach reduces soil erosion, improves water infiltration, and creates microclimates.

#3.3 Sustainability and Construction Integration

Plants should support construction goals: erosion control on slopes, stabilization around foundations, and compatibility with drainage systems. Avoid aggressive root systems near structures.

4. Practical Plant Selection and Implementation Techniques

#4.1 Recommended Plant Categories for Bali

- Canopy Trees: Coconut palm (Cocos nucifera), Frangipani (Plumeria spp.), Rain Tree (Samanea saman)

- Mid-Layer Shrubs: Hibiscus, Bougainvillea, Heliconia, Croton, Ixora

- Ground Covers: Philodendron, ferns, mondo grass, or native grasses

- Climbers and Screening: Jasmine, Thunbergia, Bougainvillea on trellises

Selection should prioritize native or well-adapted species for lower maintenance and ecological benefits.

#4.2 Site-Specific Assessment

1. Analyze soil type, pH, drainage, and sunlight exposure.

2. Consider wind patterns, especially in coastal or elevated sites.

3. Evaluate water availability and irrigation feasibility.

4. Account for construction phasing to protect young plants.

#4.3 Installation Best Practices

- Prepare planting pits with improved soil mix (compost + sand for drainage).

- Install at correct depth and spacing to allow mature growth.

- Provide staking for trees and initial watering regime.

- Use mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

#4.4 Maintenance Planning

Develop a simple maintenance schedule focusing on pruning, fertilizing, pest monitoring, and irrigation adjustment. Integrated pest management (IPM) is preferred over chemical reliance.

5. Applications and Case Considerations in Bali Construction

In Bali villa and residential projects, thoughtful plant selection creates signature tropical gardens that enhance property value while supporting environmental goals. Layered designs with palms, flowering shrubs, and ground covers are common, but success depends on matching species to micro-sites (sunny vs. shaded, dry vs. moist areas). Contractors who integrate landscape planning early avoid costly redesigns and plant replacement.

Examples from local projects show that using drought-tolerant yet lush species reduces water bills and maintenance labor significantly.

3. Latar Belakang Teori dan Kriteria Pemilihan

#3.1 Kerangka Evaluasi Multi-Kriteria

Pemilihan tanaman efektif menggunakan sistem penilaian berbobot berdasarkan:

- Kesesuaian iklim (suhu, kelembaban, curah hujan)

- Adaptasi tanah (pH, drainase, kebutuhan nutrisi)

- Efisiensi air

- Laju pertumbuhan dan ukuran dewasa

- Ketahanan hama dan penyakit

- Nilai estetika dan fungsional

Skor total dapat dihitung sebagai jumlah tertimbang dari skor performa setiap kriteria.

#3.2 Konsep Layering dalam Desain Tropis

Lanskap tropis efektif menggunakan layering vertikal:

- Lapisan kanopi: pohon tinggi untuk naungan dan struktur

- Lapisan tengah: semak dan pohon kecil untuk warna dan penyaringan

- Ground cover: tanaman rendah untuk perlindungan tanah dan penekanan gulma

Pendekatan ini mengurangi erosi tanah, meningkatkan infiltrasi air, dan menciptakan mikroklimat.

#3.3 Keberlanjutan dan Integrasi Konstruksi

Tanaman harus mendukung tujuan konstruksi: kontrol erosi pada lereng, stabilisasi sekitar pondasi, dan kesesuaian dengan sistem drainase. Hindari sistem akar agresif di dekat struktur.

4. Teknik Pemilihan dan Implementasi Praktis

#4.1 Kategori Tanaman yang Direkomendasikan untuk Bali

- Pohon Kanopi: Kelapa (Cocos nucifera), Kamboja (Plumeria spp.), Pohon Hujan (Samanea saman)

- Semak Lapisan Tengah: Kembang Sepatu, Bougainvillea, Heliconia, Croton, Ixora

- Ground Cover: Philodendron, pakis, rumput mondo, atau rumput asli

- Climber dan Penyaring: Melati, Thunbergia, Bougainvillea pada teralis

Pemilihan harus memprioritaskan spesies asli atau yang sudah beradaptasi untuk pemeliharaan rendah dan manfaat ekologis.

#4.2 Penilaian Spesifik Situs

1. Analisis jenis tanah, pH, drainase, dan paparan sinar matahari.

2. Pertimbangkan pola angin, terutama di area pesisir atau elevated.

3. Evaluasi ketersediaan air dan kelayakan irigasi.

4. Sesuaikan dengan tahapan konstruksi untuk melindungi tanaman muda.

#4.3 Praktik Pemasangan Terbaik

- Siapkan lubang tanam dengan campuran tanah yang diperbaiki.

- Tanam pada kedalaman yang benar dan jarak yang sesuai untuk pertumbuhan dewasa.

- Berikan penyangga untuk pohon dan regime penyiraman awal.

- Gunakan mulsa untuk menahan kelembaban dan menekan gulma.

#4.4 Perencanaan Pemeliharaan

Buat jadwal pemeliharaan sederhana yang fokus pada pemangkasan, pemupukan, pemantauan hama, dan penyesuaian irigasi. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) lebih disukai daripada ketergantungan kimia.

Contoh dari proyek lokal menunjukkan bahwa penggunaan spesies tahan kekeringan namun tetap lush mengurangi tagihan air dan tenaga pemeliharaan secara signifikan.
