Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment for construction projects. Divergent expectations between project owners and contractors regarding scope, quality, and timelines frequently precipitate contractual claims. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Conflict Mitigation Efficiency Index (CMEI) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate project health. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability in the Balinese market.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Conflict Mitigation Efficiency Index (CMEI)

To resolve claims effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the CMEI to measure the financial and temporal risk of a dispute.

The CMEI Formula (For Word/Excel integration):

$$CMEI = (W_d \times D_r) + (W_c \times C_r) + (W_q \times Q_r)$$

Where:

  • $CMEI$ = Conflict Mitigation Efficiency Index (Values > 7.0 indicate high potential for successful settlement).
  • $W_d, W_c, W_q$ = Weighting factors for Time (0.4), Cost (0.4), and Quality/Compliance (0.2).
  • $D_r$ = Delay ratio (Total days delayed / Project duration).
  • $C_r$ = Cost variance (Disputed claim value / Total Contract Value).
  • $Q_r$ = Quality/Compliance risk (Frequency of technical deviations).
