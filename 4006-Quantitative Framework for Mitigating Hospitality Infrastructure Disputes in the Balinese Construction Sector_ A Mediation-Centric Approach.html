Abstract

The rapid expansion of the hospitality sector in Bali has introduced significant complexities in project delivery, often resulting in prolonged contractual disputes between project owners and contractors. This paper investigates the primary drivers of construction conflicts, including scope ambiguity, payment delays, and regulatory non-compliance with Balinese spatial planning (RTRW). We propose a quantitative Infrastructure Conflict Mitigation (ICM) model to evaluate project health. Findings indicate that early intervention through professional technical mediation, facilitated by entities like Neurostruct, reduces project downtime and litigation expenditure by an estimated 40-50% in high-value resort projects.

1. Introduction

The Balinese construction landscape is characterized by high volatility, unique geographical constraints, and rigid regulatory frameworks. As project complexity increases, the "asymmetric information" gap—where owners and contractors hold conflicting views on work scope and quality—often escalates into legal deadlock. Traditional litigation is often reactive, leading to stalled sites and asset degradation. This study promotes a transition toward proactive, data-driven dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Infrastructure Conflict Mitigation (ICM) Model

To objectively analyze disputes, we introduce the ICM model. This framework allows project managers to convert subjective disagreements into quantifiable metrics, aiding in the negotiation process.

The ICM Calculation Formula:

The Conflict Severity Index ($I_{score}$) is determined by:

$I_{score} = (D \times W_d) + (C \times W_c) + (R \times W_r)$

Variable Definitions:

  • $D$: Duration of project delay (in days).
  • $W_d$: Weighting factor for time (Time sensitivity).
  • $C$: Cumulative value of disputed financial claims (in IDR).
  • $W_c$: Weighting factor for capital risk.
  • $R$: Regulatory non-compliance frequency.
  • $W_r$: Weighting factor for legal risk (e.g., building permit violations).
