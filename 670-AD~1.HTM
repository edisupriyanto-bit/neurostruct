1. Introduction

Building drainage systems (pekerjaan drainase bangunan) collect and convey rainwater and wastewater away from structures to prevent flooding, ponding, and water-induced damage such as leaks, mold growth, and structural deterioration. In tropical regions like Bali, Indonesia, where annual rainfall can exceed 2000 mm with intense short-duration events, inadequate drainage frequently leads to leaks through roofs, walls, and foundations.

Leak-proof performance requires integrated design of hydraulic capacity, slope, materials, and waterproofing details. This paper examines principles for residential applications, including roof gutters, downpipes, surface channels, and basement/perimeter drainage, with emphasis on anti-leak strategies at critical junctions. It aligns with international best practices and SNI standards for plumbing and stormwater systems.

2. Literature Review

Research on roof and building drainage highlights the importance of primary and secondary (overflow) systems to handle extreme events. Studies in *Building and Environment* and practical guides from ACO and similar manufacturers emphasize gravity and siphonic drainage for flat and pitched roofs. In tropical climates, steep slopes (minimum 1–3% for flat roofs) and adequate gutter sizing prevent ponding and leaks.

Indonesian standards such as SNI 8153:2015 provide guidelines for plumbing and drainage in buildings, while SNI 03-3424-1994 and related documents address surface drainage. Scopus-indexed papers on urban drainage in humid tropics stress climate change impacts, recommending oversized systems or secondary overflows. Bali-specific challenges include high-intensity rains, volcanic soils affecting subsurface drainage, and seismic considerations for pipe supports.

Common failure modes—clogged gutters, insufficient slope, poor joint sealing, and material corrosion—are mitigated through proper detailing and maintenance.

3. Theoretical Background and Hydraulic Design

# 3.1 Flow in Open Channels and Pipes

Manning’s equation is widely used for gravity drainage design:

\[ V = \frac{1}{n} R^{2/3} S^{1/2} \]

where \( V \) is velocity (m/s), \( n \) is Manning’s roughness coefficient (e.g., 0.013 for smooth concrete), \( R \) is hydraulic radius (m), and \( S \) is slope (m/m).

Flow rate \( Q = V \cdot A \), where \( A \) is cross-sectional area.

For roof gutters and downpipes, capacity must exceed design rainfall intensity (often based on 10–100 year return periods per local data).

# 3.2 Roof Drainage Sizing

Primary drainage handles normal rainfall; secondary (overflow) systems activate during blockages or extreme events. Minimum slope for flat roofs is typically 1% (optimal 2–3%) to prevent ponding.

# 3.3 Waterproofing Integration

Leak prevention at roof drains, scuppers, and pipe penetrations requires compatible waterproof membranes (e.g., bitumen or polyurethane) with proper flashing and seals. Vapor barriers and breathable layers manage humidity in tropics.

4. Best Practices for Leak-Proof Drainage Systems

# 4.1 Roof Drainage Components

- Gutters and Eaves: Sized per roof area and rainfall intensity; use corrosion-resistant materials (PVC, stainless steel, or coated aluminum) for Bali’s coastal humidity.

- Downpipes: Vertical leaders with adequate diameter; include cleanouts and connections to surface drains.

- Flat Roof Systems: Internal drains with sumps or siphonic systems for large areas; secondary scuppers or overflow drains mandatory.

- Pitched Roofs: Valleys and hips require reinforced waterproofing; steep slopes (minimum 30–45°) promote rapid runoff.

# 4.2 Surface and Perimeter Drainage

- Grading to slope away from foundations (minimum 2% for 3–5 m).

- French drains or perimeter channels with gravel backfill for subsurface water.

- Integration with rainwater harvesting where feasible.

# 4.3 Anti-Leak Detailing and Materials

- Waterproof membranes at all penetrations and junctions.

- Expansion joints and flexible seals to accommodate thermal/seismic movement.

- Regular maintenance access points to prevent clogging by leaves or debris common in tropical vegetation.

Installation sequence: substrate preparation, waterproofing application, drain installation with proper flashing, testing for leaks under simulated rainfall.

# 4.4 Quality Control and Testing

- Hydraulic testing of assembled systems.

- Visual and flood testing for waterproof integrity.

- Compliance checks with SNI 8153:2015 and local building codes.

5. Applications in Bali Residential Construction

Bali’s villas and homes often feature pitched tile/metal roofs or flat concrete elements amid lush gardens and variable terrain. Effective drainage prevents leaks during monsoons, protects foundations from saturation, and reduces mold risks in humid conditions. Hybrid systems combining reflective roofs, proper slopes, and robust gutters/downpipes have shown significant reductions in water-related defects. Integration with site grading is critical on sloping lots to avoid erosion or foundation undermining.
