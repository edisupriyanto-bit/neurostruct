1. Introduction

In residential and villa construction, particularly in cost-sensitive markets like Bali, it is common to build reinforced concrete columns directly between masonry walls (either brick or lightweight block). This hybrid system combines the speed and economy of masonry with the strength and ductility of RC columns. However, improper installation often leads to honeycombing, poor bonding, vertical cracks, and reduced structural performance, especially under seismic loading.

This paper provides clear, practical guidance for contractors on the correct way to install RC columns between masonry walls. It covers preparation, reinforcement placement, formwork, concreting, curing, and quality control, with special attention to challenges faced by small contractors and beginners.

The techniques presented are fully compliant with SNI 2847:2019 and SNI 1726:2019, while remaining simple and cost-effective for field application in tropical conditions.

2. Literature Review

Research on hybrid masonry-RC systems shows that proper interface detailing significantly affects load transfer and crack control. Studies in *Engineering Structures* emphasize the importance of mechanical anchorage (starter bars, dowels, or mesh) and good bond between new concrete and existing masonry.

In tropical climates, high humidity accelerates shrinkage and increases the risk of debonding. Indonesian standards require minimum concrete cover, proper vibration in confined spaces, and adequate curing. Field studies from Southeast Asia highlight that poor construction sequencing is a leading cause of defects in confined columns.

3. Theoretical Background and Key Principles

# 3.1 Load Transfer and Bonding

The column must achieve monolithic behavior with the masonry walls. This requires:

- Mechanical connection via starter bars or dowels

- Roughened masonry surface for better bond

- High-slump concrete with good workability for narrow spaces

# 3.2 Formwork Pressure and Design

Concrete pressure on formwork in narrow columns is calculated similarly to walls:

\[ p_{max} = \rho \cdot g \cdot h \]

where \( \rho \) ≈ 2400 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², h = pour height.

For columns cast between walls, external bracing against the masonry is often used.

# 3.3 Seismic Considerations

Columns between walls must be properly confined and anchored to beams or slabs above to avoid soft-story mechanisms. Minimum transverse reinforcement follows SNI 2847 Chapter 18 for special moment frames or ordinary frames.

4. Practical Step-by-Step Techniques for Installing Columns Between Masonry Walls

# 4.1 Preparation Stage

1. Build masonry walls on both sides first (or leave a gap of column width + 20–30 mm tolerance).

2. Roughen the inner faces of the masonry with a chisel or wire brush for mechanical bond.

3. Install vertical starter bars or dowels from the foundation/lower beam into the column position (minimum 40d embedment).

4. Place horizontal ties or mesh across the column location every 400–600 mm height.

# 4.2 Reinforcement Placement

- Use full-height vertical bars with proper lap splices outside potential plastic hinge zones.

- Install ties (hoops) at spacing per SNI (typically 100–150 mm in critical zones).

- Ensure minimum concrete cover of 40 mm (or 50 mm in aggressive environments).

# 4.3 Formwork System for Confined Spaces

- Use timber planks or steel panels braced against the existing masonry walls.

- Install strongbacks and walers on the outer side.

- Apply form release agent generously.

- Leave the top open or provide a small pour window for vibration access.

# 4.4 Concreting Procedure

- Use concrete with slump 150–200 mm for easy flow in narrow spaces.

- Place concrete in layers of maximum 300–400 mm.

- Use internal poker vibrator carefully, inserting from the top and moving slowly to avoid honeycombing.

- For very narrow columns (<300 mm), consider self-compacting concrete (SCC) if budget allows.

# 4.5 Curing and Stripping

- Keep forms in place for at least 24–48 hours.

- Apply moist curing immediately after stripping (wet burlap or curing compound).

- Protect the fresh column from direct sun and rain for the first 7 days.

# 4.6 Quality Control Checks

- Verify verticality (plumbness) before and after concreting.

- Check cover and bar spacing.

- Perform slump test and make cube specimens for strength verification.

- Visual inspection for honeycombing after stripping.

4. Teknik Langkah demi Langkah Memasang Kolom di Antara Dinding Bata

# 4.1 Tahap Persiapan

1. Bangun dinding masonry di kedua sisi terlebih dahulu (atau sisakan celah sesuai lebar kolom + toleransi).

2. Kasarkan permukaan dalam dinding dengan pahat atau sikat kawat.

3. Pasang starter bar vertikal dari pondasi ke posisi kolom.

4. Pasang tie horizontal atau mesh melintang setiap 400–600 mm tinggi.

# 4.2 Penempatan Tulangan

- Gunakan tulangan vertikal full height dengan lap splice yang tepat.

- Pasang tie (hoop) dengan jarak sesuai SNI.

- Pastikan selimut beton minimal 40 mm.

# 4.3 Sistem Bekisting di Ruang Sempit

- Gunakan papan kayu atau panel baja yang disangga ke dinding masonry yang ada.

- Pasang strongback dan waler di sisi luar.

# 4.4 Prosedur Pengecoran

- Gunakan beton dengan slump 150–200 mm.

- Cor secara bertahap maksimal 300–400 mm per lapis.

- Gunakan vibrator poker internal dengan hati-hati.

# 4.5 Curing dan Pembongkaran

- Biarkan bekisting minimal 24–48 jam.

- Lakukan curing basah segera setelah pembongkaran.

# 4.6 Pengendalian Kualitas

- Periksa keteplakan sebelum dan sesudah pengecoran.

- Lakukan slump test dan buat benda uji kubus.

- Inspeksi visual setelah stripping.
