Abstract

Construction disputes often arise from technical discrepancies, structural failures, or deviations from specified material standards, leading to significant financial and temporal losses. Traditional legal mediation frequently lacks the technical depth required to address the root causes of engineering failures, resulting in superficial settlements. This paper proposes a robust methodology for construction dispute resolution based on rigorous technical engineering analysis. By utilizing forensic engineering, non-destructive testing (NDT), and advanced structural modeling, we demonstrate how objective data can mitigate conflict and provide equitable solutions. The integration of Indonesian National Standards (SNI) and international benchmarks (Eurocode/ACI) serves as the primary framework for arbitration. Particular focus is given to the Bali region, where specific geotechnical conditions and seismic risks necessitate a specialized forensic approach. The study concludes that data-driven engineering audits significantly increase the probability of out-of-court settlements.

1. Introduction

The construction industry is inherently prone to disputes due to its complexity, multi-stakeholder nature, and environmental variables. In regions with high seismic activity and specific climatic conditions, such as Indonesia, technical failures in masonry, foundations, and reinforced concrete often lead to prolonged legal battles.

Historically, disputes over "cracked walls" or "tilting floors" were argued through subjective testimonies. However, technical engineering analysis offers a paradigm shift from adversarial litigation to data-driven resolution. By treating a disputed building as a "forensic site," engineers can determine with mathematical certainty whether a failure was caused by design negligence, poor execution, or Force Majeure.

2. Methodology: The Forensic Engineering Framework

To resolve a construction dispute effectively, a systematic engineering audit must be conducted to establish a "Technical Baseline."

  1. Visual Forensic Audit: Systematic mapping of cracks (shear vs. shrinkage), settlement patterns, and concrete spalling.
  2. Material Verification & NDT: Comparing as-built material strength ($f'c$) against the original Bill of Quantities (BoQ) using Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV) and Schmidt Hammer tests.
  3. Geotechnical Review: Assessing soil-structure interaction, especially in Bali’s varied terrain (limestone cliffs vs. alluvial plains).
  4. Structural Simulation: Re-modeling the building using Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to determine if the design was flawed ($R_n < U$) or if the execution failed to meet design specifications.

3. Mathematical Analysis of Structural Integrity

In cases involving load-bearing disputes, the safety factor ($SF$) must be recalculated to provide an objective basis for the claim.

Equation 1: Reliability Quantification

$$SF = \frac{R_n}{U}$$

Where:

$R_n$ = Nominal resistance (actual measured strength of the element).

$U$ = Required strength (factored loads per SNI 1727).

For concrete beam disputes involving reinforcement deficiencies, the nominal moment capacity ($M_n$) is calculated as:

$$M_n = A_s \cdot f_y \cdot (d - \frac{a}{2})$$

Where:

$$a = \frac{A_s \cdot f_y}{0.85 \cdot f'_c \cdot b}$$

By plugging in as-built data (measured $A_s$ from GPR scanning and $f'_c$ from core drills), the engineer provides the "empirical truth" required to settle claims regarding structural under-performance.
