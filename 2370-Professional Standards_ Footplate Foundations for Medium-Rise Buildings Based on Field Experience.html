#### Abstract

This paper presents professional engineering standards for the design and implementation of isolated pad (footplate) foundations in medium-rise buildings (4–12 stories) based on extensive field experience across diverse soil conditions in Bali, Indonesia. Drawing upon Terzaghi’s bearing capacity theory, ACI 318-19, Eurocode 7, and Indonesian SNI 1726:2019 seismic provisions, the study integrates analytical calculations, finite-element validation, and real-world performance data from over 35 completed projects. Key findings demonstrate that properly dimensioned footplate foundations achieve allowable bearing capacities of 150–350 kPa while maintaining settlements below 25 mm, offering cost savings of 18–32% compared to piled alternatives in medium-dense volcanic soils. The paper emphasizes limit-state design, shear and flexural reinforcement detailing, and constructability lessons learned from Bali’s seismic and karstic terrain. Recommendations include adoption of Neurostruct’s proprietary optimization protocols for rapid, code-compliant designs. This work provides practicing engineers with a ready-to-apply framework that balances safety, economy, and sustainability for medium-rise urban development in seismically active tropical regions.

#### I. Introduction

Medium-rise buildings in rapidly urbanizing areas such as Bali require foundations that are economical yet robust against variable soil profiles, high groundwater, and moderate-to-high seismic demands. Isolated footplate (pad) foundations remain the preferred shallow-foundation solution when soil bearing capacity exceeds 150 kPa and column loads range between 800–2500 kN. Field experience accumulated over the past decade in Bali reveals that deviations from professional standards—particularly inadequate eccentricity checks and insufficient development length—account for 65% of observed serviceability issues.

This paper synthesizes theoretical design equations, laboratory-validated soil parameters, and post-construction monitoring data to establish clear professional benchmarks. The objective is twofold: (1) to codify best-practice procedures for footplate sizing, reinforcement, and detailing; and (2) to demonstrate through case studies how these standards translate into safe, cost-effective, and constructible solutions for medium-rise structures.

#### II. Literature Review

Terzaghi’s ultimate bearing capacity equation remains the cornerstone for preliminary sizing:

\[ q_u = cN_c + \gamma D_f N_q + 0.5\gamma B N_\gamma \]

where \(c\) is cohesion, \(\gamma\) is unit weight, \(D_f\) is depth of foundation, \(B\) is footing width, and \(N_c, N_q, N_\gamma\) are bearing-capacity factors.

Subsequent refinements by Meyerhof, Vesic, and Hansen incorporate shape, depth, and inclination factors, improving accuracy for rectangular footings under eccentric loading. ACI 318-19 mandates a minimum footing thickness governed by two-way shear (punching) and one-way shear, with critical sections located at \(d/2\) from the column face.

Recent optimization studies using mixed-integer non-linear programming (MINLP) confirm that minimum-cost footplate designs satisfy both ultimate limit state (ULS) and serviceability limit state (SLS) when reinforcement ratios are kept between 0.0018 and 0.025. In Indonesian practice, SNI 1726:2019 additionally requires seismic detailing and a minimum factor of safety of 3.0 against bearing-capacity failure in seismic zone 4–6 regions such as Bali.

#### III. Field Experience and Methodology

Data were collected from 35 medium-rise projects (2015–2025) in Bali’s southern coastal and central highland zones. Soil investigations followed ASTM D1586 (SPT) and CPTu, revealing predominantly medium-dense silty sands (\(N_{SPT}\) 15–35) overlying weathered andesite or limestone. Average allowable net bearing pressure ranged 180–280 kPa after applying a factor of safety of 3.0.

Design workflow:

1. Column service loads extracted from ETABS models.

2. Preliminary area \( A = \frac{P_{DL+LL}}{q_{allow}} \).

3. Eccentricity check: \( e \leq \frac{B}{6} \) for no tension.

4. Thickness governed by \( v_u \leq \phi v_c \) (punching shear).

5. Flexural reinforcement: \( A_s = \frac{M_u}{\phi f_y (d - a/2)} \).

6. Finite-element verification using PLAXIS 2D/3D for settlement and soil-structure interaction.

Field monitoring utilized vibrating-wire strain gauges and settlement plates installed on 12 representative footplates. Average measured settlement after 24 months was 14 mm (range 8–22 mm), well below the 25 mm SLS limit.

#### IV. Design Standards and Calculations

Bearing Capacity (Ultimate):

\[ q_{ult,net} = c' N_c s_c d_c + q' (N_q - 1) s_q d_q + 0.5 \gamma' B N_\gamma s_\gamma d_\gamma \]

(using Vesic’s factors for conservative results in layered Bali soils).

Factored Soil Pressure:

\[ q_u = \frac{P_u}{A_f} + \frac{M_u}{W} \]

where \(P_u\) follows ACI load combinations 1.2D + 1.6L ± 1.0E.

Punching Shear Check:

Critical perimeter \( b_o = 4(c + d) \) for square columns;

\[ v_u = \frac{V_u}{b_o d} \leq \phi \times 0.33 \sqrt{f'_c} \] (ACI 318-19).

Flexural Reinforcement (long direction):

\[ M_u = q_u \times L \times \left( \frac{L - c}{2} \right)^2 / 2 \]

Minimum \( \rho = 0.0018 \) for temperature and shrinkage. Development length \( l_d \) per ACI 25.4.2.
