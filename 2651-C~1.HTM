1. Introduction

Waterproofing is a critical protective layer in buildings that prevents water ingress, protects structural elements, and extends service life. Incorrect material estimation leads to shortages (causing delays and rework) or excess (increasing costs and waste). Professional quantity takeoff integrates surface area measurement, coverage rates from manufacturers, overlap/wastage factors, and site-specific conditions.

General formula for material quantity:

\[

Q = A \times N \times C \times (1 + W)

\]

where:

- \(Q\) = total material quantity (kg, liters, m² rolls)

- \(A\) = surface area (m²)

- \(N\) = number of coats/layers

- \(C\) = coverage rate (kg/m² or liters/m² per coat)

- \(W\) = wastage/overlap factor (typically 0.05–0.15 or 5–15%)

For vertical surfaces and coving (upturns), add perimeter-based calculations. In tropical climates like Bali, high humidity and heavy rainfall require robust systems with higher consumption rates and extra buffers.

2. Literature Review

Industry guidelines and Scopus-indexed studies emphasize accurate takeoff for waterproofing. Coverage rates vary: liquid membranes 0.5–1.5 kg/m² per coat, cementitious 1.5–2.5 kg/m² per mm thickness, bituminous 1–2 kg/m². Research highlights the impact of surface porosity, temperature, and application method on actual consumption. In tropical conditions, additional factors such as evaporation rates and substrate moisture content affect material performance and required quantities.

3. Methodology and Calculation Guidelines

3.1 Surface Area Measurement

Measure gross area + coving (typically 150–300 mm upturn on walls). Add overlaps (50–100 mm for sheets) and wastage (5–15%).

3.2 Coverage Rates (Typical Values)

- Liquid-applied membrane: 0.8–1.2 liters/m² per coat (2 coats common).

- Cementitious slurry: 1.5–2.5 kg/m² per coat (2–3 coats).

- Sheet membrane: 1.1 × area (including overlaps).

3.3 Practical Formulas

For multi-coat systems:

\[

Q_{\text{total}} = A \times \left( \sum C_i \right) \times (1 + O + W)

\]

where \(C_i\) is consumption per coat, \(O\) overlap factor, \(W\) wastage.

3.4 Adjustments for Tropical Conditions

Increase wastage buffer to 10–20% due to humidity and irregular surfaces. Account for primer consumption (0.2–0.4 liters/m²).
