1. Introduction

In reinforced concrete construction, the foundation (pondasi), tie beam (sloof), and column (kolom) form the primary load path from superstructure to soil. The sloof beam ties isolated footings together, resists differential settlement, distributes horizontal forces, and serves as a base for masonry walls while providing continuity for column reinforcement. In seismic zones like Bali, proper connections are essential to maintain integrity during ground shaking and prevent progressive collapse.

2. Literature Review

# 2.1 Role and Classification of Tie Beams (Sloof)

Tie beams (grade beams or plinth beams) connect footings to prevent spreading and control settlement. In Indonesian practice, sloof is typically cast at or slightly above ground level, supporting non-structural walls while integrating with column starter bars.

Standards require ties in seismic design categories to carry tension/compression forces equal to a percentage of column loads (e.g., per ACI or SNI adaptations). Minimum dimensions often follow rules such as width ≥ clear column spacing / 20 but not exceeding certain limits, with closed stirrups for confinement.

# 2.2 Connection Detailing Principles

Critical aspects include:

- Reinforcement Continuity: Column longitudinal bars extend into the footing and through the sloof with proper development length (ld) and hooks.

- Lap Splices: Located away from high-stress zones; staggered for ductility.

- Anchorage: Hooks or mechanical devices at terminations.

- Confinement: Closer stirrup spacing at joints per seismic provisions.

- Joint Integrity: Proper concrete cover, vibration, and curing to avoid honeycombing or cold joints.

Literature emphasizes that inadequate detailing at foundation-column-sloof interfaces leads to cracking and reduced seismic performance.

# 2.3 Tropical and Seismic Considerations in Bali

Bali’s volcanic soils, high rainfall, and seismic activity (SNI 1726) necessitate corrosion-resistant reinforcement, adequate concrete cover (minimum 40–50 mm for foundations), and designs that accommodate differential settlement. Rapid tourism construction demands efficient yet safe detailing.

Equation 1: Development Length for Tension Reinforcement (Simplified per ACI 318 / SNI, Copy-Paste Friendly)

ld = (fy × db) / (25 × √f'c) (basic form, units consistent; fy in MPa, db in mm, f'c in MPa)

Where:

- ld = development length (mm)

- fy = yield strength of rebar (e.g., 420 MPa)

- db = bar diameter (mm)

- f'c = concrete compressive strength (e.g., 25 MPa)

4. Results and Discussion – Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Site Preparation and Foundation Design

Perform soil testing. Design isolated or combined footings per loads. Place bottom reinforcement grid.

Step 2: Footing Construction

Install formwork, reinforcement, and embed column dowels/starter bars with sufficient embedment length.

Step 3: Column-to-Footing Connection

Ensure starter bars have proper hooks or development length into footing. Use chairs for cover.

Step 4: Sloof (Tie Beam) Reinforcement

Place longitudinal bars continuous or lapped. Install closed stirrups denser near columns. Tie to column starters and footing dowels.

Step 5: Formwork and Pouring

Align forms level. Pour concrete in one continuous operation where possible to avoid cold joints. Vibrate thoroughly at connections.

Step 6: Curing and Protection

Apply moist curing for 7+ days, especially in Bali’s hot climate.

Discussion: Proper lapping and confinement improve ductility. In seismic zones, strong-column-weak-beam principles extend downward, ensuring foundation elements do not fail first. Common beginner errors (insufficient cover, poor vibration, wrong lap location) are mitigated through checklists.
