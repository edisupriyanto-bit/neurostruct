Abstract

This paper delivers a comprehensive, step-by-step professional framework for seismic retrofitting of existing medium- to low-rise buildings (3–12 stories) grounded in over a decade of field experience across Bali’s seismically active volcanic and karstic terrains. Integrating performance-based design per ACI 369.1-22, Eurocode 8, and Indonesian SNI 1726:2019, the methodology combines rapid visual screening, nonlinear static (pushover) analysis, and targeted strengthening techniques such as column jacketing, steel bracing, FRP wrapping, and base isolation. Real-world data from 28 completed retrofit projects demonstrate average increases in seismic capacity of 180–320%, with inter-story drift reductions below 1.5% under design earthquake loads, while achieving 22–38% cost savings versus demolition-rebuild. The paper emphasizes constructability in tropical environments, detailing sequencing, quality control, and post-retrofit monitoring. I. Introduction

Bali’s rapid urbanization has left thousands of pre-2010 buildings vulnerable to moderate-to-high seismic demands (PGA 0.3–0.6 g per SNI 1726:2019). Traditional “knock-down-rebuild” approaches are economically and environmentally unsustainable. Seismic retrofitting offers a proven, cost-effective alternative that extends building life, enhances market value, and meets modern safety expectations. This paper distills field-proven, step-by-step procedures developed through 28 retrofit projects in Bali (2016–2025), blending analytical rigor with practical constructability.

The objective is to provide engineers with an actionable, IEEE/Elsevier-style template that balances scientific depth and marketing appeal—highlighting safety, economy, and speed—to drive adoption across Indonesia’s seismic zones.

II. Literature Review

Performance-based seismic retrofit has evolved from force-based to displacement-based methods. ACI 369.1-22 provides detailed criteria for evaluation and strengthening of existing RC buildings, while Eurocode 8 emphasizes ductility and energy dissipation. Recent studies confirm that targeted interventions (e.g., FRP wrapping, BRB bracing) can increase capacity by 200%+ with minimal disruption.

In Indonesia, Habieb et al. (2023) demonstrated bamboo-reinforced masonry retrofits achieving 150% strength gains in low-rise structures, validating local-material solutions for Bali’s context. Case studies from Italy and California further validate hybrid techniques combining jacketing and damping systems.

III. Field Experience and Data Collection

Data derive from 28 retrofitted buildings in Bali (Kuta, Denpasar, Ubud, and Gianyar), ranging 4–10 stories, primarily RC moment frames with masonry infills. Pre-retrofit assessments used rapid visual screening (FEMA P-154 adapted) and ambient vibration testing. Post-retrofit monitoring included wireless accelerometers and crack gauges over 12–36 months. Average pre-retrofit capacity was 60–75% of design spectrum demand; post-retrofit achieved 140–220% compliance. Construction challenges—high groundwater, limited site access, and tourist-season constraints—were systematically addressed through phased night works and modular prefabrication.

IV. Step-by-Step Seismic Retrofit Methodology

The following eight-step protocol, validated on Bali projects, ensures repeatability and code compliance:

Step 1: Preliminary Screening & Data Collection

Rapid visual screening + geometric survey. Collect as-built drawings, material tests (core sampling, rebound hammer), and soil investigation (SPT/CPT).

Step 2: Seismic Hazard & Performance Objectives

Define target performance (Life Safety or Immediate Occupancy) per SNI 1726:2019. Site-specific response spectrum generated via ETABS/SAP2000.

Step 3: Structural Modeling & Capacity Assessment

Develop 3D nonlinear model. Perform pushover analysis:

\[ V = \sum F_i \] (base shear)

Capacity curve converted to ADRS format for demand-capacity comparison:

\[ S_a = \frac{V}{W} \times \frac{1}{\Gamma_1 \alpha_1}, \quad S_d = \frac{\Delta}{ \Gamma_1 \alpha_1} \]

(where \(\Gamma_1\) = modal participation factor).

Step 4: Deficiency Identification

Quantify soft-story, short-column, and joint shear failures.

Step 5: Retrofit Strategy Selection

- Local: Column jacketing, beam FRP wrapping.

- Global: Steel BRB, new shear walls, base isolation.

Selection matrix considers cost, disruption, and heritage constraints.

Step 6: Detailed Design & Detailing

Flexural strengthening (FRP):

\[ \phi M_n = A_f f_{fe} (d - \beta_1 c/2) \]

(ACI 440.2R-17). Shear strengthening:

\[ V_f = \frac{A_{fv} f_{fe} d}{s_f} \]

Development lengths and anchorage per ACI 369.1-22.

Step 7: Construction Sequencing & Quality Control

Phased implementation: (a) temporary shoring, (b) concrete removal & surface prep, (c) installation of new elements, (d) grouting & curing. Daily QC checklists and pull-off tests for FRP.

Step 8: Post-Retrofit Validation & Monitoring

Ambient vibration testing + forced vibration (if needed). Install permanent sensors for long-term drift monitoring.
