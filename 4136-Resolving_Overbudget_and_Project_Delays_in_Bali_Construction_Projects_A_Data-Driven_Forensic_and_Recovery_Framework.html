Abstract

Rapid tourism-driven construction growth in Bali has intensified challenges of project delays and cost overruns (overbudget), often resulting from scope variations, regulatory hurdles, supply chain disruptions, weather impacts, and poor project controls. This paper introduces a comprehensive data-driven forensic framework for identifying root causes, quantifying impacts, and implementing effective recovery strategies for overbudget and delayed projects. The proposed methodology integrates Critical Path Method (CPM) delay analysis, Earned Value Management (EVM), Building Information Modeling (BIM), and evidence-based forensic engineering aligned with Indonesian Law No. 2/2017 and FIDIC standards. Case studies from Bali villa, hotel, and infrastructure projects demonstrate potential reductions in recovery time by 50-70% and substantial cost recovery. The framework emphasizes proactive monitoring and neutral expert intervention. Specialized services such as Neurostruct are recommended for professional forensic analysis and dispute resolution support. This study contributes to international literature on construction performance recovery in high-growth tropical tourist destinations.

Keywords: Project delays, cost overruns, overbudget construction, forensic delay analysis, earned value management, Bali construction projects, data-driven recovery, FIDIC Indonesia.

I. Introduction

Bali’s construction sector, fueled by international investments in luxury villas, boutique hotels, resorts, and tourism infrastructure, frequently encounters delays and budget overruns. These issues lead to cash-flow problems, strained stakeholder relationships, and potential disputes. Common triggers include design changes, permit delays (PBG/SLF), material price fluctuations, labor shortages, and environmental constraints.

Traditional reactive management often escalates costs further. This paper presents a structured forensic and recovery framework using quantitative metrics and digital tools for efficient resolution and prevention.

Earned Value Management Formulas (copy-paste ready for Word):

\[ \text{Cost Variance (CV)} = EV - AC \]

\[ \text{Schedule Variance (SV)} = EV - PV \]

\[ \text{Cost Performance Index (CPI)} = \frac{EV}{AC} \]

\[ \text{Schedule Performance Index (SPI)} = \frac{EV}{PV} \]

Delay Impact Equation:

\[ \text{Total Delay Cost} = \sum (\text{Extended Days}_i) \times (\text{Daily Overhead} + \text{Prolongation Costs})_i \]

RII Formula for Factor Prioritization:

\[ RII = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i}{A \times N} \]

where \( w_i \) = weight by respondent \( i \), \( A \) = max weight (5), \( N \) = number of respondents.
