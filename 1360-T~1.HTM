Abstract

The final surface quality of cement-based finishing coat (acian or skim coat) is heavily dependent on the application and leveling technique, particularly the use of steel trowel (roskam besi). Improper troweling techniques in tropical climates often result in surface defects such as trowel marks, uneven thickness, blistering, map cracking, and poor long-term durability. This paper presents a comprehensive engineering review and practical framework for achieving uniform, high-quality leveling of acian using steel trowels on concrete and plastered walls.

Key aspects examined include tool selection, timing of application stages (floating, leveling, and final polishing), pressure control, trowel angle, environmental factors (temperature and humidity), and operator technique. The influence of these parameters on surface flatness, adhesion, shrinkage cracking risk, and aesthetic finish is analyzed based on international research and field observations in Bali, Indonesia.

2. Literature Review

Studies on thin-layer cementitious finishes highlight that surface quality is significantly affected by the timing of troweling operations relative to the mortar’s setting time. Early troweling while the mix is too wet can cause water bleeding and surface weakness, while late troweling on partially hardened mortar leads to tearing and uneven surfaces. Research published in *Construction and Building Materials* and *Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering* emphasizes the importance of staged troweling (initial floating, intermediate leveling, and final burnishing) under controlled environmental conditions.

In tropical climates, high temperature and low humidity accelerate surface drying, increasing the risk of plastic shrinkage cracking during troweling. Proper tool angle (typically 15–30°), consistent pressure, and frequent cleaning of the trowel are critical factors often overlooked in field practice.

3. Challenges in Leveling Acian with Steel Trowel in Tropical Climates

Common challenges include:

- Rapid surface drying due to high temperature and wind, causing differential setting.

- Formation of trowel marks and waves if pressure is inconsistent.

- Blistering and delamination from trapped air or excessive troweling on wet surfaces.

- Map cracking due to overworking the surface.

- Difficulty achieving uniform thickness (ideal 2–3 mm) on large wall areas.

These issues are particularly pronounced in Bali’s coastal and high-humidity microclimates.

4. Best Practices for Acian Leveling Using Steel Trowel (Roskam Besi)

# 4.1 Tool Selection and Preparation

Use high-quality stainless steel or polished carbon steel trowels (300–400 mm length) with rounded edges. Clean trowels frequently to prevent drag marks.

# 4.2 Staged Troweling Technique

1. Initial Floating — Use wooden or plastic float to level the acian roughly within 10–20 minutes after application.

2. Intermediate Leveling — Apply steel trowel at 20–30° angle with moderate pressure once the surface has stiffened slightly.

3. Final Polishing/Burnishing — Use light pressure with a clean trowel at a flatter angle (5–15°) when the surface reaches “thumb-print hard” consistency.

# 4.3 Environmental Control

Apply acian during cooler hours (early morning or late afternoon). Protect the surface from direct sunlight and wind using shading or misting.
