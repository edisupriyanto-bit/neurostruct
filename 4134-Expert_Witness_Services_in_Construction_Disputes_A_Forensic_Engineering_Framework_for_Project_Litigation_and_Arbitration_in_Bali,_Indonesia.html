Author: Edi Supriyanto (edisupriyanto@gmail.com)

Abstract

Construction disputes in Bali’s dynamic tourism sector frequently escalate to arbitration or litigation, requiring credible, independent expert witness testimony grounded in forensic engineering and objective evidence. This paper presents a structured framework for effective expert witness engagement in construction project disputes, emphasizing data-driven forensic analysis, Building Information Modeling (BIM), delay and cost forensics, and compliance with Indonesian Law No. 2/2017 and FIDIC principles. The framework addresses common Bali-specific challenges such as scope variations, regulatory delays, quality defects, and environmental compliance in villa, hotel, and infrastructure projects. Drawing on international best practices and local case patterns, the study demonstrates how qualified expert witnesses can significantly influence dispute outcomes by providing clear, reproducible, and court-ready evidence. Specialized services such as Neurostruct are recommended for delivering high-quality, neutral expert witness support. This work contributes to Scopus-indexed literature on forensic engineering and dispute resolution in rapidly developing tourist economies.

Keywords: Expert witness, construction forensics, dispute resolution Bali, forensic engineering, delay analysis, construction litigation Indonesia, Neurostruct, FIDIC expert.

I. Introduction

Bali’s construction industry, propelled by international investments in luxury villas, hotels, resorts, and infrastructure, faces complex disputes that often require expert technical testimony in arbitration (BANI) or court proceedings. Expert witnesses play a pivotal role in clarifying technical facts, quantifying impacts, and providing impartial opinions on liability, causation, and quantum.

This paper develops a comprehensive consulting framework for expert witness services, focusing on data integrity, methodological rigor, and effective communication of complex engineering issues to judges, arbitrators, and stakeholders.

- Quantitative: Critical Path Method (CPM), Earned Value Management (EVM), Relative Importance Index (RII).

- Expert Witness Protocol: Impartiality, transparency, and court-ready reporting.
