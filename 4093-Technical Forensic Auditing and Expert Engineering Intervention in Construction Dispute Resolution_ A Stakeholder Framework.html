Abstract— Construction disputes are increasingly complex, often involving intricate structural failures, material non-compliance, and schedule delays. Traditional legal arbitration often lacks the technical depth required for equitable resolution. This paper explores the "Engineer-as-Expert" model, utilizing forensic structural analysis and Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) to provide objective data for conflict settlement. Through a comparative study of international standards (ASTM, ACI, and SNI), we propose a framework where technical truth precedes legal interpretation.

I. Introduction

Conflict in construction projects is not merely a legal friction but often a symptom of underlying engineering discrepancies. In rapid development zones like Bali, where seismic requirements and environmental conditions are stringent, technical disputes regarding structural integrity are common. This research posits that technical engineering analysis is the ultimate objective tool for dispute resolution.

II. Technical Forensic Framework

The resolution process begins with a structural audit. When a dispute arises regarding concrete strength, the Safety Factor ($SF$) must be reassessed using empirical data from core drills or Schmidt hammer tests. To evaluate the validity of a claim regarding structural failure, the Nominal Strength ($R_n$) must be compared against the Required Strength ($U$). The governing inequality is:

$$\Phi R_n \ge U$$

For a disputed reinforced concrete member under flexure, the nominal moment capacity ($M_n$) is calculated as follows:

$$M_n = A_s \cdot f_y \cdot (d - a / 2)$$

Where $a$ (depth of the equivalent stress block) is defined by:

$$a = (A_s \cdot f_y) / (0.85 \cdot f'_c \cdot b)$$

2. Metodologi Resolusi Konflik

Pendekatan kami melibatkan audit teknis independen. Kami menggunakan pengujian Non-Destructive Test (NDT) untuk mendapatkan data objektif. Dalam sengketa mengenai keamanan bangunan, kami menghitung ulang indeks reliabilitas menggunakan rumus beban terfaktor mengacu pada SNI 1726 dan SNI 2847:

$$1.2D + 1.6L + 1.0E$$

Dimana $D$ adalah beban mati, $L$ adalah beban hidup, dan $E$ adalah beban gempa. Perbandingan antara beban ini dengan kapasitas aktual bangunan menentukan apakah klaim kerusakan valid atau tidak secara engineering.
