The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to develop an integrated forensic framework. Special focus is given to the challenges in seismically active and rapidly developing regions such as Bali, Indonesia, where volcanic soil conditions, high rainfall, seismic activity, and accelerated construction increase damage risks. The Neurostruct structural management model is incorporated to enhance diagnostic accuracy, simulation reliability, and evidential strength. Practical recommendations are offered for developers, contractors, insurers, and legal practitioners seeking technically sound and efficient dispute resolutions.

### 1. Introduction

Building damage, ranging from minor cracks to major structural failures, frequently becomes the focal point of construction disputes. Accurate damage analysis provides the scientific foundation needed to determine causation, allocate liability, and recommend appropriate remedies.

In Bali, rapid tourism-driven construction combined with challenging site conditions has led to increased cases of building damage and associated disputes. This paper details forensic methodologies and frameworks for building damage analysis in dispute resolution contexts.

### 2. Literature Review

Forensic investigation of building damage is extensively covered in Scopus-indexed journals. Studies in *Engineering Failure Analysis*, *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Forensic Engineering* (ICE), and *Buildings* emphasize systematic approaches including non-destructive testing (NDT), finite element modeling, and root cause analysis. Research shows that professional damage analysis significantly improves settlement rates by providing clear, quantifiable evidence.

This study employs a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus sources (2015–2025), analysis of international and regional case studies, and synthesis of established forensic protocols. A six-phase building damage analysis framework is proposed for dispute resolution.

### 4. Key Methods in Building Damage Analysis

4.1 Visual and Non-Destructive Testing

Use of crack mapping, ultrasonic pulse velocity, rebound hammer, and ground-penetrating radar (GPR).

4.2 Structural Modeling and Simulation

Finite element analysis to evaluate stress distribution and failure mechanisms.

Equation 1 (Clean copy-paste for Word):

Stress (σ) = Force (F) / Cross-sectional Area (A)

Deflection (δ) = (P L³) / (3 E I)

Safety Factor = Material Strength / Calculated Stress

Damage Contribution (%) = (Analyzed Load/Event Impact / Total Observed Damage) × 100

4.3 Root Cause Analysis

Application of 5-Why technique, Fishbone diagrams, and fault tree analysis.

4.4 Geotechnical Damage Assessment

Critical in Bali for evaluating settlement, soil-structure interaction, and foundation performance.
