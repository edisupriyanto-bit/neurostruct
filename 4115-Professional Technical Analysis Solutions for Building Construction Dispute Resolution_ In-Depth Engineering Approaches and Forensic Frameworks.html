Abstract

Building construction disputes continue to challenge the industry due to technical complexities, contractual ambiguities, and varying interpretations of project performance. This paper presents professional technical analysis solutions as a robust methodology for resolving such disputes through in-depth engineering approaches. By employing advanced forensic investigations, detailed structural assessments, comprehensive delay and productivity analyses, and precise cost quantification, these solutions deliver objective, evidence-based outcomes suitable for mediation, arbitration, and litigation. The study draws on Scopus-indexed literature and global best practices to develop an integrated in-depth technical analysis framework. Particular emphasis is placed on the unique conditions in Bali, Indonesia, where volcanic soils, seismic activity, high humidity, and rapid tourism-driven construction amplify dispute risks. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to elevate analytical depth, simulation accuracy, and evidential robustness. Practical recommendations are offered for developers, contractors, consultants, and legal practitioners to achieve faster, fairer, and more sustainable dispute resolutions.

1. Introduction

In-depth technical analysis has emerged as the definitive tool for resolving building construction disputes. In an era where projects are increasingly complex, surface-level observations are no longer sufficient to satisfy legal rigors or technical scrutiny. Professional engineering solutions provide the clarity needed for equitable resolutions by utilizing empirical data to bridge the gap between contractual claims and physical reality.

In Bali, the rapid development of luxury villas, high-end resorts, and commercial infrastructure has created a high-stakes environment. Challenges such as limestone cliff stability, high water tables, and seismic requirements often lead to disputes involving structural integrity, project delays, and quality non-compliance. This paper explores a professional framework tailored to these challenges, ensuring that dispute resolution is grounded in science rather than conjecture.

2. Literature Review: The Evolution of Forensic Engineering

Scopus-indexed publications consistently affirm that rigorous technical analysis is the backbone of successful dispute resolution. Studies in the Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction emphasize that over 70% of construction disputes originate from technical errors or unforeseen site conditions.

Key methodologies highlighted in recent research (2020–2026) include:

  • Advanced Structural Modeling: Using Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to simulate failure modes.
  • Forensic Delay Analysis: Moving beyond Gantt charts to "As-Planned vs. As-Built" window analysis.
  • Root Cause Multi-Parameter Investigation: Identifying how interconnected failures (e.g., poor drainage leading to foundation settlement) trigger complex legal claims.

4. Core Components of Technical Analysis

4.1 Structural Forensics and Damage Assessment

Detailed evaluation goes beyond identifying cracks. It involves determining whether a crack is "active" (structural) or "passive" (aesthetic) using non-destructive testing (NDT). In Bali’s seismic zones, analyzing the ductility of reinforced concrete frames is critical to determining if a building meets SNI (Indonesian National Standard) requirements.

4.2 Forensic Delay and Productivity Analysis

Delays are rarely caused by a single factor. We utilize high-resolution data to isolate excusable vs. non-excusable delays.

Equation 1 (Causation Quantification):

$$T_{delay} = A_{completion} - C_{completion}$$

$$E_{entitlement} = \left( \frac{D_{critical\_verified}}{D_{total\_critical}} \right) \times 100$$

Where $E$ is the percentage of time extension justified by verified technical obstacles.

4.3 Geotechnical and Soil-Structure Interaction

Many Bali disputes involve "land creep" or "settlement" in volcanic or coastal soils. In-depth analysis involves mapping the soil-structure interface to prove whether a foundation failed due to design negligence or "Force Majeure" environmental shifts.
