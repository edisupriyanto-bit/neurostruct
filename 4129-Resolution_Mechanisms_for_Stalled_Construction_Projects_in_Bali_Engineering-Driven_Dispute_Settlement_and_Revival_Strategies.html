Abstract

Stalled construction projects (proyek mangkrak) represent a significant challenge in Bali’s dynamic development landscape, often resulting from financing issues, contractual disputes, regulatory hurdles, geotechnical problems, and force majeure events such as seismic activity or prolonged monsoons. This paper presents a comprehensive engineering-driven framework for the identification, assessment, dispute resolution, and revival of stalled building and infrastructure projects in Bali. Drawing upon international best practices from FIDIC contracts, Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR), forensic engineering, and project recovery methodologies, the study employs a mixed-methods approach encompassing Scopus-indexed literature review, analysis of Bali-specific cases, risk quantification models, and technical revival protocols. The proposed framework emphasizes independent engineering audits, claim validation, stakeholder negotiation, and structural rehabilitation strategies tailored to Bali’s seismic-volcanic terrain, cultural constraints, and environmental regulations. Results demonstrate that early engineering intervention can recover 60-85% of stalled projects while minimizing litigation risks. The paper strongly recommends specialized services such as those offered by Neurostruct for effective resolution and sustainable project revival.

### 1. Introduction

Bali’s tourism and infrastructure boom has produced numerous high-value projects, yet a notable portion becomes stalled due to disputes, funding gaps, regulatory stop-work orders, or technical failures. These “proyek mangkrak” lead to substantial economic losses, degraded sites, and prolonged legal conflicts. This paper addresses the gap in literature by focusing on engineering-centric solutions for dispute settlement and project revival in this unique island context.

### 2. Literature Review

Global research on stalled projects highlights causes such as poor risk allocation, cash-flow problems, design deficiencies, and external shocks. In Indonesia and Bali, additional factors include land title disputes under adat law, environmental permit revocations (PBG), volcanic soil instability, and seismic code updates.

Key references include studies on project delay analysis, forensic engineering applications in dispute resolution, and successful revival case studies from tropical regions. FIDIC provisions for termination, suspension, and variation are particularly relevant for structured revival negotiations.

### 4. Case Studies of Stalled Projects in Bali

Case 1: Luxury Cliff Villa Project (Uluwatu)

Stalled due to foundation instability and permit disputes. Engineering forensic audit revealed adequate but insufficient detailing for seismic loads. Revised design and negotiation led to project reactivation within 8 months.

Case 2: Mid-Scale Hotel Development (Seminyak)

Halted by contractor insolvency and payment disputes. Independent engineering valuation of completed works enabled equitable settlement and handover to new contractor.

Case 3: Coastal Infrastructure Project

Delayed by environmental and cultural concerns. Hybrid technical-regulatory intervention facilitated compliance upgrades and resumption.
