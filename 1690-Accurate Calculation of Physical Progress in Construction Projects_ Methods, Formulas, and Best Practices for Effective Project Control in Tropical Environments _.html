1. Introduction

Physical progress represents the actual quantifiable work completed relative to the total planned scope. Unlike financial or schedule progress, it focuses on tangible deliverables and serves as the foundation for earned value management (EVM). In construction projects, inaccurate progress measurement often leads to delayed payments, disputes, cost overruns, and schedule slippage.

Common objective methods include:

- Units Completed: Progress = (Actual units installed / Total planned units) × 100%

- Incremental Milestones: Assign weighted percentages to discrete steps.

- Weighted Equivalent Units: Combine quantities with labor or cost weights.

The overall physical progress for a project or work package can be calculated as a weighted average:

\[

\text{Physical Progress (\%)} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (w_i \times p_i)

\]

where \(w_i\) is the weight of activity \(i\) (based on budgeted cost, man-hours, or quantity), and \(p_i\) is the percent complete of activity \(i\).

In tropical regions like Bali, factors such as rainfall interruptions and material supply variability necessitate frequent, accurate updates. This paper provides a comprehensive framework aligned with international best practices and local standards.

2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research emphasizes objective methods over subjective “expert judgment.” Choi et al. (2020) proposed real-time physical progress measurement using sensors and quantity tracking. Studies on EVM in construction confirm that integrating physical quantities with budgeted costs yields reliable performance indices:

- Schedule Performance Index (SPI) = EV / PV

- Cost Performance Index (CPI) = EV / AC

Incremental milestones and weighted units are widely recommended for simplicity and accuracy in reporting. Indonesian construction guidelines (e.g., Permen PU) stress monthly physical progress reporting based on realized versus planned volumes.

3. Methodology and Calculation Methods

3.1 Units Completed Method

Suitable for repetitive tasks (e.g., piling, painting).

\[

\% \text{ Complete} = \frac{\text{Actual Quantity}}{\text{Total Quantity}} \times 100

\]

3.2 Incremental Milestones (Weighted Milestone)

Divide work into measurable steps with predefined weights summing to 100%. Progress is credited only upon milestone completion.

3.3 Earned Value Management Integration

Earned Value (EV) = % Physical Complete × Budget at Completion (BAC)

Planned Value (PV) = % Scheduled Complete × BAC

3.4 Quantity Surveyor Approach

Measure installed volumes (m³ concrete, m² plaster, etc.) and multiply by unit rates for weighted contribution.

3.5 Practical Formulas for Overall Progress

For multi-activity projects:

\[

\text{Overall Physical Progress} = \frac{\sum (\text{Actual Quantity}_i \times \text{Unit Weight}_i)}{\sum (\text{Total Quantity}_i \times \text{Unit Weight}_i)} \times 100\%

\]

4. Case Studies in Construction Projects

In Bali’s infrastructure and residential developments, projects using weighted quantity methods achieved more accurate monthly reporting, enabling timely corrective actions and reducing payment disputes. Hybrid EVM-physical tracking improved forecast reliability during monsoon seasons.

5. Challenges and Best Practices in Tropical Projects

Challenges include weather-induced delays, supply chain variability, and subjective reporting. Best practices: daily quantity logs, digital tools for real-time tracking, independent verification by quantity surveyors, and integration with BIM or project management software. Regular reconciliation between physical and financial progress prevents discrepancies.
