2. Literature Review

# 2.1 Definitions and Classifications

A building drainage system is a network of components that collects, conveys, and disposes of stormwater and wastewater from a structure and its immediate surroundings. It is typically divided into:

- Roof Drainage: Gutters, downspouts, and roof drains.

- Surface Drainage: Catch basins, channels, and swales for paved/impervious areas.

- Subsurface Drainage: French drains or perforated pipes for groundwater control.

- Sustainable Drainage Systems (SuDS): Nature-based solutions like rain gardens, permeable pavements, and bioretention cells.

In Indonesian context, systems follow Permen PU No. 12/2014 and SNI standards for urban drainage, emphasizing primary (main channels), secondary, and tertiary networks integrated with retention and infiltration.

# 2.2 Key Components and Functions

- Collection Components: Gutters, scuppers, roof drains, street inlets, catch basins.

- Conveyance Components: Downspouts, pipes (PVC, concrete), open channels, manholes.

- Disposal/Storage Components: Outlets to municipal systems, soakaways, retention ponds, infiltration trenches.

- Ancillary Elements: Gratings, traps (to prevent debris), valves, and overflow (secondary) systems.

Standards such as IPC and ASCE 7 guide sizing based on rainfall intensity, catchment area, and roof geometry.

# 2.3 Challenges in Tropical and Seismic Environments (Bali Context)

High rainfall intensity, rapid runoff from impervious surfaces (roofs, parking areas), and soil variability require oversized capacities and erosion control. Seismic movements demand flexible joints and durable materials. Literature on SuDS in subtropical/tropical cities highlights benefits of green roofs, bioretention, and permeable pavements in reducing peak flows by 30–70% and improving water quality.

3. Methodology

This study synthesizes standards, hydraulic equations, and case applications through literature review and procedural framework development. The Neurostruct protocol structures drainage design and construction into sequential phases with quality checkpoints.

Equation 1: Rational Method for Peak Runoff (Copy-Paste Friendly for Word)

Q = C × I × A

Where:

- Q = peak runoff (m³/s or L/s)

- C = runoff coefficient (0.7–0.95 for impervious roofs/pavements)

- I = rainfall intensity (mm/hr or m/s, based on 100-year, 1-hour or shorter duration)

- A = catchment area (ha or m²)

Equation 2: Manning’s Equation for Open Channel Flow

V = (1/n) × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2)

Where:

- V = velocity (m/s)

- n = Manning’s roughness coefficient

- R = hydraulic radius (m)

- S = slope (m/m)

4. Results and Discussion

A well-designed building drainage system sized per Rational Method and IPC/ASCE guidelines prevents ponding on roofs (secondary drainage critical to avoid structural overload) and surface flooding. In tropical settings, incorporating SuDS reduces runoff volume by promoting infiltration and evapotranspiration.

In Bali, roof drainage must handle high-intensity short-duration rains, while surface systems address impervious tourist-area developments. Components like catch basins with sediment traps minimize clogging. Neurostruct enhances reliability through phased planning (hydrologic analysis → component sizing → installation sequencing → testing), material selection (corrosion-resistant PVC/HDPE), and regular maintenance protocols.

Lifecycle benefits include 25–50% reduction in flood-related damages and improved groundwater recharge via infiltration features.
