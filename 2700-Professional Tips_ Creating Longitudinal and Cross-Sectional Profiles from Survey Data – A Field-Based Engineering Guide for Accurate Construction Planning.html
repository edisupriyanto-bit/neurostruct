II. Literature Review

Longitudinal profiles plot station (chainage) versus reduced level along a centerline, while cross-sections display offset versus elevation perpendicular to the alignment. Standard reduction formulas include:

\[ \text{Reduced Level (RL)} = \text{Height of Instrument (HI)} - \text{Foresight (FS)} \]

or for trigonometric leveling:

\[ \text{RL}_2 = \text{RL}_1 + \Delta H \]

where \(\Delta H = D \cdot \sin \theta\) (D = slope distance, \(\theta\) = vertical angle).

Coordinate transformation from local survey coordinates to profile station-offset format uses:

\[ \text{Station} = \sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2} \]

\[ \text{Offset} = \frac{(x - x_0)(y_1 - y_0) - (y - y_0)(x_1 - x_0)}{\sqrt{(x_1 - x_0)^2 + (y_1 - y_0)^2}} \]

Recent studies confirm that digital profile generation reduces human error by 85–95% and improves earthwork accuracy. Indonesian practice follows SNI standards for topographic surveys, emphasizing minimum 1 m contour intervals and profile scales of 1:100 to 1:500 for construction.

IV. Professional Step-by-Step Protocol for Creating Profiles

Step 1: Establish Control and Collect Raw Data

Set temporary benchmarks; record backsight, intermediate sight, foresight readings.

Step 2: Reduce Levels and Compute Coordinates

Apply HI-FS formula for all points.

Step 3: Define Alignment Centerline

Input stationing (chainage) along design centerline.

Step 4: Generate Longitudinal Profile

Plot station (x-axis) vs. existing ground RL (y-axis) at 5–20 m intervals.

Step 5: Generate Cross-Sectional Profiles

At each station, extract offsets (left/right) and corresponding RLs.

Step 6: Plot and Annotate

Use scales (horizontal 1:500, vertical 1:100); add proposed design line, cut/fill hatching.

Step 7: Quality Verification

Field-check 10% of points; recalculate if discrepancy >5 mm.

Step 8: Export and Integrate

Output to CAD for earthwork quantities and construction drawings.
