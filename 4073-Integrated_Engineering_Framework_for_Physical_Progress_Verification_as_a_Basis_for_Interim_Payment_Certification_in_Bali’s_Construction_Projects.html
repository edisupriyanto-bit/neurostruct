Abstract

The accuracy of physical progress verification is a fundamental pillar in construction financial governance, particularly in Bali’s high-growth hospitality and residential sectors. Discrepancies between reported progress and actual site completion often lead to overpayment, cash flow imbalances, and contractual disputes. This paper proposes a standardized Engineering Verification Framework (EVF) that utilizes multi-point measurement protocols and Earned Value Management (EVM). By integrating on-site physical audits with Bill of Quantities (BoQ) reconciliation, this study provides a mathematical model to ensure that Interim Payment Certificates (IPC) reflect true structural and architectural milestones. The implementation of this framework in Bali demonstrates a significant reduction in financial risk and project stagnation.

1. Introduction

In the context of Bali’s construction industry, where projects are often decentralized and subject to varied topographical challenges, "Over-claiming" by contractors remains a persistent risk. Traditional verification methods based on visual estimation are prone to subjectivity. This paper argues that an independent engineering verification is essential to validate that the "Value of Work Done" (VOWD) aligns with the contracted payment schedule.

2. Methodology: Systematic Progress Verification

The verification process follows a rigorous technical workflow:

  1. Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) Alignment: Ensuring every activity on-site corresponds to a specific line item in the BoQ.
  2. Physical Volumetric Measurement: Utilizing laser distance meters and drone photogrammetry for earthworks and structural volume verification.
  3. Qualitative Compliance Check: Progress is only "verified" if it meets the quality standards defined in the Technical Specifications (SNI).
  4. Certification of Payment: Issuing a technical recommendation that acts as the basis for the owner’s fund disbursement.

3. Mathematical Models for Progress Calculation

To determine the Percent Complete ($PC$) of a project, we utilize the weighted value method based on the Budgeted Cost of Work Performed (BCWP):

$$PC = \frac{\sum (V_{a,i} \times P_{u,i})}{\text{Total Contract Value}} \times 100\%$$

Where:

  • $V_{a,i}$ = Actual verified volume of item $i$
  • $P_{u,i}$ = Unit price of item $i$ as per contract

The variance between reported progress ($PC_r$) and verified progress ($PC_v$) is defined as the Overpayment Risk ($R_o$):

$$R_o = (PC_r - PC_v) \times \text{Total Contract Value}$$

If $R_o > 0$, the certification must be adjusted to prevent the contractor from being "ahead of the curve" financially compared to their physical output.

4. Regional Challenges in Bali

  1. Topographical Variables: Verifying progress in sloped areas (Ubud/Uluwatu) requires precise elevation checks to avoid volume inflation in retaining walls.
  2. Cultural Constraints: Accounting for "Hari Raya" and local ceremonies that affect the duration but not necessarily the physical volume in a specific billing cycle.
  3. Material Procurement: Verifying "Materials on Site" (MOS) as part of progress, which requires strict warehousing audits to prevent double-claiming.

Ketidaksesuaian antara laporan kontraktor dengan realita lapangan seringkali menyebabkan kelebihan bayar (overpayment) yang membahayakan arus kas pemilik proyek. Artikel ini membahas penerapan kerangka kerja verifikasi teknis menggunakan metode Earned Value Management (EVM). 2. Metode Verifikasi Progres yang Akurat

Layanan verifikasi profesional mencakup:

  • Opname Lapangan: Pengukuran ulang volume beton, pasangan dinding, dan luasan finishing secara manual maupun digital.
  • Checklist Kualitas: Progres fisik hanya diakui jika kualitasnya memenuhi standar (misal: beton mencapai umur yang cukup atau pasangan keramik tidak kopong).
  • Rekonsiliasi RAB: Menghitung bobot persentase berdasarkan harga satuan yang telah disepakati dalam kontrak awal.

3. Rumus Teknis Perhitungan Progres dan Pembayaran

Dalam menentukan nilai progres untuk dasar pembayaran, digunakan rumus bobot item pekerjaan ($W_i$):

$$W_i = \frac{Q_{aktual,i} \times H_{satuan,i}}{\text{Nilai Kontrak Total}} \times 100\%$$

Total progres proyek ($P_{total}$) didapatkan dari penjumlahan seluruh bobot item yang telah selesai:

$$P_{total} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i$$

Nilai pembayaran yang layak dibayarkan ($NP$) setelah dikurangi uang muka (Down Payment) dan retensi adalah:

$$NP = (P_{total} \times \text{Nilai Kontrak}) - \text{Potongan DP} - \text{Retensi} - \text{Pembayaran Sebelumnya}$$

Rumus ini memastikan bahwa dana yang keluar dari dompet investor benar-benar sebanding dengan "benda" yang sudah terbangun di lokasi.

4. Keunikan Verifikasi Proyek di Bali

Audit progres di Bali memiliki tantangan khusus:

  • Pekerjaan Persiapan: Verifikasi biaya mobilisasi dan pembersihan lahan yang seringkali tidak terlihat namun menyerap anggaran besar.
  • Material di Lokasi (MOS): Penilaian apakah material yang sudah dibeli namun belum terpasang boleh dibayar atau tidak, untuk menjaga likuiditas proyek.
  • Kondisi Geografis: Memastikan volume galian dan timbunan di daerah tebing (seperti Uluwatu) dihitung secara akurat menggunakan alat ukur koordinat.
