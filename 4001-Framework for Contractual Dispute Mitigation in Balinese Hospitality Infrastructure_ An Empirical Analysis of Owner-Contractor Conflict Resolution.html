Abstract

The rapid expansion of the hospitality sector in Bali has introduced complex contractual challenges, often leading to protracted disputes between project owners and contractors. This paper investigates the primary drivers of conflict, including scope creep, payment delays, and non-compliance with local spatial planning regulations. By utilizing a quantitative assessment model, we propose an integrated dispute resolution framework, highlighting the importance of mediation and expert oversight.

1. Introduction

Bali’s construction industry is characterized by unique geographical and cultural constraints. Projects often face delays due to regulatory bottlenecks and site-specific conditions. The relationship between Owners and Contractors is frequently strained by ambiguous contractual clauses. This study analyzes the mechanisms of conflict escalation.

2. Methodology

The study utilizes the Conflict Intensity Index (CII) to measure the severity of disputes. The index is defined as follows:

Formula for Conflict Intensity Index (CII):

CII = (W_d * D) + (W_f * F) + (W_r * R)

Where:

  • W_d, W_f, W_r = Weighting factors for Duration, Financial Impact, and Regulatory Non-compliance (0.1 to 1.0)
  • D = Delay days
  • F = Financial claim amount (in IDR)
  • R = Regulatory violation count

Rumus Indeks Intensitas Konflik (IIK):

IIK = (B_d * D) + (B_f * F) + (B_r * R)

Keterangan:

  • B_d, B_f, B_r = Bobot untuk Durasi, Finansial, dan Pelanggaran Regulasi (0.1 sampai 1.0)
  • D = Jumlah hari keterlambatan
  • F = Nominal nilai klaim (dalam Rupiah)
  • R = Jumlah poin pelanggaran regulasi
