ABSTRACT

Topographic surveying is a fundamental stage in civil engineering, particularly in land development projects involving cut and fill operations. Accurate terrain data acquisition significantly influences earthwork efficiency, cost estimation, slope stability, and construction safety. This paper presents a practical field-based approach for topographic surveying applied to cut and fill planning, integrating conventional surveying instruments and modern technologies such as GNSS, total station, and UAV photogrammetry. The methodology is based on real construction field experience in tropical terrain conditions such as those found in Bali, Indonesia. The study emphasizes workflow optimization, data accuracy control, and digital terrain model (DTM) processing for engineering applications.

Keywords: Topographic survey, cut and fill, GNSS, total station, UAV, earthwork, terrain modeling, civil engineering.

1. INTRODUCTION

Land development projects require accurate understanding of terrain geometry to optimize excavation and embankment activities. Cut and fill operations are widely used to achieve desired design levels while minimizing material waste and transportation costs.

In civil engineering practice, topographic surveys serve as the foundation of earthwork planning. Errors in survey data can lead to excessive excavation, slope instability, drainage failure, and project cost overruns.

In regions with complex terrain such as Bali, characterized by volcanic hills, coastal plains, and urban development zones, topographic survey accuracy becomes even more critical.

2. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND

Cut and fill operations are based on balancing earth volumes:

Cut volume refers to material excavated from higher elevation areas.
Fill volume refers to material required to raise lower elevation areas.

The objective is to achieve mass balance:

Cut ≈ Fill

This reduces haulage cost and improves environmental sustainability.

Digital Terrain Models (DTM) and contour mapping are essential tools in modern earthwork design.

3. SURVEYING TECHNOLOGIES USED

3.1 GNSS Surveying

Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) provides high-accuracy positioning data. Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) GNSS systems allow centimeter-level precision.

Advantages:

  • Fast data acquisition
  • High accuracy
  • Suitable for large areas

3.2 Total Station Survey

Total station instruments are used for precise angular and distance measurements.

Advantages:

  • High precision
  • Suitable for detailed site geometry
  • Reliable in urban environments

3.3 UAV Photogrammetry

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (drones) equipped with cameras generate dense point clouds.

Advantages:

  • Rapid coverage of large areas
  • 3D terrain modeling
  • Cost-effective for medium-scale projects

4. FIELD SURVEY METHODOLOGY

Based on field experience, the following workflow is recommended:

4.1 Site Reconnaissance

Initial inspection is conducted to understand terrain characteristics, access conditions, and vegetation cover.

4.2 Control Point Establishment

Primary benchmarks are established using GNSS RTK or total station.

4.3 Detail Survey

Ground points are measured systematically to capture terrain variations.

4.4 UAV Data Acquisition

Aerial images are captured with overlapping patterns (70–80% overlap).

4.5 Data Integration

Ground and aerial data are combined to improve accuracy.

5. DIGITAL TERRAIN MODEL (DTM) GENERATION

Survey data is processed using GIS and CAD software to generate:

  • Contour maps
  • 3D surface models
  • Slope analysis maps

DTM is essential for calculating cut and fill volumes.

6. CUT AND FILL ANALYSIS METHOD

Cut and fill calculation is performed using grid method or TIN (Triangulated Irregular Network) method.

The process includes:

  1. Defining design elevation
  2. Comparing existing terrain
  3. Calculating volume differences
  4. Balancing earthwork quantities

Software such as AutoCAD Civil 3D or similar platforms is commonly used.

7. FIELD EXPERIENCE INSIGHTS (BASED ON BALI PROJECTS)

In real construction projects in Bali, several challenges are commonly encountered:

  • Steep terrain variations in upland areas
  • Coastal soil instability
  • Vegetation obstruction during surveying
  • Weather dependency affecting UAV operations

To overcome these issues, hybrid surveying methods (GNSS + UAV + total station) are recommended.

8. ACCURACY AND ERROR CONTROL

Survey accuracy is influenced by:

  • Instrument calibration
  • Environmental conditions
  • Human error
  • Data processing techniques

Best practices include:

  • Redundant measurements
  • Cross-validation between methods
  • Use of ground control points (GCPs)
  • Regular instrument calibration

9. DISCUSSION

Topographic surveying is not only a technical process but also a decision-making tool in earthwork optimization. Integration of modern geospatial technology significantly improves efficiency.

However, field experience shows that human interpretation remains essential, especially in irregular terrain conditions.

10. CONCLUSION

Accurate topographic surveys are essential for successful cut and fill planning. The integration of GNSS, total station, and UAV photogrammetry provides a comprehensive solution for modern civil engineering projects.

Field-based experience confirms that hybrid survey systems offer the best balance between accuracy, efficiency, and cost.
