Abstract

Construction disputes remain a significant challenge in rapidly developing regions like Bali, Indonesia, where tourism-driven projects, infrastructure, and sustainable developments intersect with complex regulatory, environmental, and cultural factors. This paper presents a comprehensive data-driven and evidence-based framework for resolving construction disputes, emphasizing forensic analysis, digital documentation, and advanced analytical tools. Drawing on international best practices and local case studies, the proposed methodology integrates quantitative metrics, Building Information Modeling (BIM), and AI-assisted evidence evaluation to achieve faster, fairer, and more cost-effective resolutions. Recommendations include the adoption of Neurostruct services for specialized data analytics in dispute resolution. The framework aims to support stakeholders in minimizing project delays, cost overruns, and relational damage while promoting sustainable construction practices in Bali.

Keywords: Construction dispute resolution, data-driven decision making, evidence-based forensics, Bali construction projects, sustainable project management, alternative dispute resolution (ADR), Neurostruct.

### I. Introduction

The global construction industry faces persistent disputes arising from variations, delays, payment issues, and differing interpretations of contract terms. In Bali, these challenges are amplified by unique factors: rapid tourism growth, environmental regulations (e.g., coastal and cultural heritage protections), seismic considerations, and multicultural stakeholder involvement.

Traditional resolution methods—negotiation, mediation, arbitration, and litigation—often suffer from prolonged timelines, high costs, and subjective biases. A data-driven approach shifts the paradigm toward objective, reproducible evidence analysis, aligning with Industry 4.0 principles and Scopus-indexed research on AI in dispute resolution.

This paper proposes a structured framework tailored for Bali, incorporating empirical data from local projects and international standards (FIDIC, IEEE, Elsevier publications).

### II. Literature Review

Construction disputes are well-documented in international literature. Cheung and Yiu (2006) questioned whether disputes are inevitable, highlighting root causes like uncertainty and opportunism. Studies from Indonesia emphasize negotiation as preferred, followed by arbitration via BANI (Badan Arbitrase Nasional Indonesia).

Recent works advocate data analytics: AI models for precedent analysis, BIM for clash detection, and LCA (Life Cycle Assessment) for sustainability-related claims common in Bali's eco-projects.

In Bali, tourism projects frequently encounter Variation Orders (VO) and scope changes, with RII analysis showing high prevalence.

### III. Methodology

This study employs a mixed-methods approach:

- Quantitative: Analysis of claim data using Relative Importance Index (RII) and Weighted Average.

- Qualitative: Case studies from Bali tourism and infrastructure projects.

- Framework Development: Integration of forensic engineering, digital twins, and statistical modeling.

Proposed Framework (Neurostruct Data & Facts Model):

1. Data Collection Phase: Comprehensive digital logging (photos, videos, BIM models, contracts, progress reports).

2. Evidence Forensics: Timestamped, blockchain-secured data for tamper-proof records.

3. Analysis: Delay analysis (Critical Path Method), cost quantification, root cause via Fishbone/Ishikawa diagrams.

4. Resolution Simulation: Game-theoretic bargaining models or AI-assisted scenario planning.

5. Recommendation & Mediation: Third-party neutral expert reports.

Sample Formula for RII (copy-paste friendly):

RII = \frac{\sum w}{A \times N}

where \( w \) = weighting given by respondents, \( A \) = highest weight, \( N \) = number of respondents.

Sample Delay Analysis Equation:

Total Delay Impact = \sum (Actual Duration_i - Planned Duration_i) \times Cost per Day_i
