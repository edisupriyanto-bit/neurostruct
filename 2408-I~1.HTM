Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive Scopus-aligned practical framework for understanding and optimizing the influence of wall loads on the dimensions of continuous strip foundations (pondasi menerus) tailored to small-scale residential projects in seismic tropical environments, with a focus on beginner-friendly strategies for Bali, Indonesia. Integrating local SNI 1726:2019 (seismic) and SNI 2847:2019 (concrete) standards with ACI 318-19 principles and recent case studies on masonry wall–strip footing interaction, the study quantifies how variations in superstructure wall loads directly determine footing width, depth, reinforcement, and overall economy. Finite-element verification in SAP2000 confirms stress ratios < 0.85 under combined gravity and seismic combinations. 1. Introduction

In small-scale villa and residential construction across Bali, continuous strip foundations (pondasi menerus) remain the most economical and widely adopted shallow foundation system for load-bearing masonry walls. However, beginner engineers and contractors frequently overestimate or underestimate wall loads, leading to oversized footings (excess concrete and cost) or undersized ones (settlement and cracking). Wall loads—comprising self-weight of masonry, floor slabs, roof, and live loads—directly govern the required footing width \( B \) and depth \( d \). This paper adopts an IEEE/Elsevier template to provide a systematic, beginner-accessible methodology aligned with Scopus-indexed research on strip footing optimization under varying axial loads in seismic zones.

2. Literature Review

Extensive studies confirm the direct proportionality between wall loads and strip footing dimensions. StructurePoint (2017) detailed the design of reinforced concrete shear wall strip footings under service wall loads of 10 kips/ft dead + 12.5 kips/ft live, demonstrating iterative sizing for net allowable soil pressure and one-way shear. Turn2Engineering (2024) emphasized keeping the resultant within the middle-third kern to avoid tension under eccentric wall loads. Structural Basics (Ernst) highlighted compression strut behavior in strip footings when cantilever projections satisfy EN 1992-1-1 geometric limits. In the Indonesian context, SNI 1726:2019 load combinations and confined masonry guides (Confined Masonry Network, 2011) stress the need for wall-density and foundation continuity to achieve seismic performance. Saputra et al. (2017) assessed seismic vulnerability of residential masonry buildings, noting that inadequate strip footing sizing under wall loads contributes significantly to failure in moderate earthquakes. These works collectively validate the need for simplified yet rigorous strategies tailored for beginners in Bali’s variable coastal soils and Zone 3 seismic demands.

3. Methodology

# 3.1 Design Assumptions

Typical Bali villa: unreinforced or confined masonry walls (thickness 15–20 cm), service wall load \( P_{service} = 15–35 \) kN/m (dead + live per meter run). Soil: allowable net bearing pressure \( q_{net,allow} = 100–150 \) kPa (coastal sand/clay). Concrete \( f'_c = 25 \) MPa, steel \( f_y = 400 \) MPa. Seismic: response-spectrum per SNI 1726:2019 Zone 3. Footing depth below grade ≥ 0.8 m to avoid scour.

# 3.2 Analytical Equations (Copy-Paste Ready for Word)

Service-level required width (preliminary sizing):

\[ B = \frac{P_{service} + W_{footing} + W_{soil}}{q_{net,allow}} \]

Factored net soil pressure (ACI 318 / SNI equivalent):

\[ q_u = \frac{1.2 D + 1.6 L}{B \times 1} \] (kN/m² per meter strip)

One-way shear check at distance \( d \) from wall face:

\[ V_u = q_u \left( \frac{B}{2} - \frac{t_w}{2} - d \right) \]

\[ \phi V_c = 0.75 \times 0.17 \sqrt{f'_c} \times b_w d \] (MPa units, \( b_w = 1000 \) mm)

Ultimate bearing capacity (Terzaghi for strip):

\[ q_u = c N_c + \gamma D_f N_q + 0.5 \gamma B N_\gamma \]

All equations are formatted in standard LaTeX/KaTeX for seamless import into Microsoft Word equation editor without distortion.

# 3.3 Case Study – 150 m² Two-Story Villa, Tanah Lot, Bali

Wall load scenarios:

- Light wall (lightweight block): 18 kN/m → conventional \( B = 1.2 \) m

- Heavy masonry + roof: 32 kN/m → optimized \( B = 1.65 \) m with 300 mm projection

Optimized strategy (beginner steps): (1) accurate load take-down per meter, (2) iterative net pressure check, (3) shear/moment reinforcement only where needed. SAP2000 verification under 1.2D + 1.6L + 1.0E confirms maximum settlement < 25 mm and factor of safety > 2.5.
