Abstract

Infrastructure construction projects are highly susceptible to disputes due to their scale, technical complexity, long durations, and significant public interest. This paper examines engineering audit methodologies as a systematic and effective approach for resolving disputes in infrastructure projects. Through independent technical audits, forensic investigations, compliance verification, delay analysis, and cost performance evaluation, engineering audits deliver objective evidence and actionable recommendations for mediation, arbitration, and negotiation. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to propose a comprehensive engineering audit framework tailored for infrastructure disputes. Special attention is given to the unique challenges in Bali, Indonesia, including seismic and volcanic risks, environmental sensitivities, regulatory compliance, and rapid tourism-related infrastructure development. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to strengthen audit accuracy, data integration, and long-term performance assessment. Practical guidelines are provided for government agencies, contractors, project owners, and legal practitioners to achieve efficient, transparent, and technically sound dispute resolutions.

### 1. Introduction

Infrastructure projects such as roads, bridges, tunnels, airports, and utilities involve massive investments and multiple stakeholders, making disputes over delays, quality, variations, and cost overruns almost inevitable. Engineering audits provide an independent, systematic evaluation of technical compliance, performance, and causation, serving as a powerful tool for fair dispute resolution.

In Bali, major infrastructure developments supporting tourism growth face unique technical and environmental challenges. This paper explores engineering audit approaches specifically designed for infrastructure dispute resolution.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research highlights the increasing application of engineering audits in construction dispute resolution. Studies in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Journal of Construction Engineering and Management*, *Transportation Research Record*, and *Buildings* demonstrate that structured audits improve transparency, strengthen evidence, and accelerate settlements. Recent literature emphasizes digital audit tools, forensic techniques, and performance benchmarking for large-scale infrastructure.

### 3. Methodology

This study is based on a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus sources (2015–2025), analysis of international and regional infrastructure dispute cases, and synthesis of engineering audit standards. A six-phase engineering audit framework is proposed for infrastructure dispute resolution.

### 4. Key Components of Engineering Audit in Infrastructure Disputes

4.1 Schedule and Delay Audit

Forensic analysis of project timelines using Critical Path Method and contemporaneous records.

4.2 Structural and Geotechnical Audit

Evaluation of design adequacy, construction quality, and ground conditions, especially important for bridges and roads on Bali’s volcanic terrain.

4.3 Cost and Variation Audit

Verification of quantities, rates, and changed conditions with detailed cost modeling.

4.4 Compliance and Quality Audit

Assessment against SNI standards, environmental permits, and international specifications.
