Abstract— Construction disputes are increasingly common due to technical failures, structural deviations, and material non-compliance. This paper proposes a systematic framework for dispute resolution leveraging forensic engineering. By employing non-destructive testing (NDT), structural modeling, and empirical verification against Indonesian National Standards (SNI), technical experts can provide objective evidence for arbitration. The study focuses on tropical seismic zones where environmental degradation and seismic loads complicate liability assessments.

I. Introduction

In the construction industry, disputes often arise from subjective interpretations of "quality" and "safety". In rapidly developing regions like Bali, the intersection of high tourism demand and seismic risks necessitates a more rigorous approach to conflict resolution. Forensic engineering provides a scientific basis to translate physical damage into contractual liability.

II. Forensic Engineering Framework

The forensic approach involves four primary stages: data collection, structural analysis, root cause determination, and remediation planning. In disputes involving reinforced concrete, the investigation often centers on whether the as-built capacity meets the design requirements defined by SNI 2847:2019.

A. Structural Reliability Recalculation

To settle claims regarding structural instability, the expert must determine the actual Safety Factor ($SF$). The fundamental relationship is:

$$SF = \text{Capacity} / \text{Demand} \ge 1.4$$

For a disputed structural beam, the ultimate moment capacity ($M_n$) is calculated as follows:

$$M_n = A_s \cdot f_y \cdot (d - a/2)$$

Where the equivalent stress block depth ($a$) is:

$$a = (A_s \cdot f_y) / (0.85 \cdot f'_c \cdot b)$$
