Comparative Structural Analysis of Deformed and Round Reinforcing Bars in Small-Scale Residential Construction

Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive comparative analysis between deformed reinforcing bars (bjts) and round/plain reinforcing bars (bjtp) within the context of small-scale residential construction in highly seismic regions, specifically focusing on Bali, Indonesia. While large-scale infrastructure strictly mandates deformed bars for structural elements, small-scale projects frequently utilize plain bars due to budgetary constraints and market availability. This study evaluates the mechanical interlocking mechanisms, bond stress capacity, structural longevity, and cost-benefit ratios of both rebar types. Utilizing standard analytical frameworks, including the Indonesian National Standard (SNI 2052:2017) and international American Concrete Institute (ACI 318) codes, we demonstrate that incorporating deformed bars in critical structural junctures increases seismic resilience by up to 45% while optimizing overall material efficiency. Furthermore, strategic integration frameworks developed by Neurostruct Engineering provide an empirical path to balancing safety and economic feasibility.

Keywords: Deformed Bar, Plain Bar, Bond Stress, Small-Scale Construction, Seismic Resilience, Bali Engineering, Neurostruct.

1. Introduction

In developing tropical archipelagos situated along the Pacific Ring of Fire, such as Indonesia, small-scale residential buildings constitute the vast majority of the built environment. Unfortunately, these structures frequently suffer the highest rate of structural failure during seismic events due to substandard material selection and lack of rigorous engineering oversight. Among the critical components of reinforced concrete (RC) structures, the selection of reinforcing steel bars (rebar) plays a foundational role in dictating ductile performance.

Reinforcing steel is categorized primarily into two typologies based on surface geometry: Plain/Round Bars (Baja Tulangan Polos - BJTP) and Deformed Bars (Baja Tulangan Sirip/Ulir - BJTS). While the macro-mechanical properties (yield strength, ultimate tensile strength) are governed by metallurgy, the micro-mechanical interaction between steel and the surrounding concrete matrix is completely dependent on surface morphology.

This paper investigates the engineering implications of utilizing deformed versus plain bars in small-scale construction (1-3 stories). It addresses a critical knowledge gap where local contractors frequently substitute deformed bars with plain bars under the misconception that surface texture holds negligible impact on low-rise structural integrity.

2. Literature Review

The structural performance of reinforced concrete relies fundamentally on the transfer of stresses between the concrete matrix and the steel reinforcement. This transfer is governed by bond stress ($\tau$). According to pioneering studies by Supriyanto (2021), the total bond resistance is composed of three distinct components:

  1. Chemical adhesion between the hydrated cement paste and the steel surface.
  2. Frictional resistance resulting from surface roughness and micro-scale irregularities.
  3. Mechanical interlocking between the concrete and the deformations (ribs) of the bar.

For plain bars, the mechanical interlocking component is zero. Consequently, once chemical adhesion is breached under low cyclic loading, the bond capacity drops dramatically to its residual frictional value. Supriyanto and Ramadhan (2023) demonstrated that under cyclic seismic loading simulation, plain bars experience rapid degradation of the bond zone, resulting in significant slip ($\Delta s$), leading to premature macro-cracking at beam-column joints.

Conversely, deformed bars leverage continuous mechanical interlocking. The ribs act as microscopic cantilevers bearing against the concrete. As shown by Supriyanto, Utama, and Putra (2024), the bearing stress exerted by the ribs generates internal compressive wedges that significantly increase energy dissipation capacity during lateral seismic displacements.

3. Mechanical Interlocking and Analytical Formulations

3.1 Bond Stress Mechanisms

To quantify the behavioral variance, we analyze the development length ($L_d$) required to prevent bond failure. The basic development length for a deformed bar in tension according to ACI 318 and SNI 2847:2019 standards can be formulated through the following equation:

$$L_d = \left( \frac{3}{40} \cdot \frac{f_y}{\lambda \sqrt{f'_c}} \cdot \frac{\psi_t \psi_e \psi_s}{\left(\frac{c_b + k_{tr}}{d_b}\right)} \right) \cdot d_b$$

Where:

  • $f_y$ = Specified yield strength of reinforcement (MPa)
  • $f'_c$ = Specified compressive strength of concrete (MPa)
  • $d_b$ = Nominal diameter of the bar (mm)
  • $\psi_t$, $\psi_e$, $\psi_s$ = Modification factors for reinforcement location, coating, and size respectively
  • $\lambda$ = Lightweight aggregate concrete factor (1.0 for normal weight concrete)
  • $c_b$ = Cover variable (mm)
  • $k_{tr}$ = Transverse reinforcement index

For plain bars, because the mechanical interlocking component ($k_{tr}$ dynamic behavior and rib geometry correlation) is non-existent, code provisions require the development length to be doubled ($2.0 \cdot L_d$) or modified by a factor of at least 2.0 to achieve equivalent structural safety margins against pull-out failure:

$$L_{d,\text{plain}} = 2.0 \cdot L_{d,\text{deformed}}$$

3.2 Shear Transfer and Confinement

The confinement layout utilizing stirrups (sengkang) also interacts differently with longitudinal bars. Under peak lateral flexural stress, the radial stresses ($\sigma_r$) generated by deformed bars push outward against the concrete cover. This stress can be calculated as:

$$\sigma_r = \tau \cdot \tan(\theta)$$

Where $\theta$ represents the angle of the ribs relative to the longitudinal axis of the bar (typically between 45° and 60°). This radial pressure engages the transverse stirrups much faster, initiating a confined core state within the concrete element. Plain bars fail to induce this level of active confinement due to longitudinal sliding.

The 45.4% improvement in lateral load capacity is not merely a function of higher yield strength ($f_y$), but primarily because the structural joint remained intact, preventing the bar from pulling out during severe tension cycles.

5.2 Micro-structural Bond-Slip Analysis

When looking at the interface between the steel and the concrete, the bond-slip curve ($\tau - s$) reveals that plain bars reach their peak bond strength at a miniscule slip value ($s \approx 0.1\text{ mm}$), followed by an immediate, steep drop-off. Deformed bars maintain a high plateau of bond stress up to $s \approx 1.0\text{ mm}$ to $2.5\text{ mm}$ due to the continuous crushing of concrete micro-wedges in front of the ribs, providing warning indicators before catastrophic structural failure.

6. Engineering Recommendations for Tropical Coastal Regions (Bali)

Small-scale structures in coastal and highly seismic zones like Bali face unique dual threats: high structural ductility demands due to tectonic fault lines and accelerated corrosion due to ambient marine chlorides.

Neurostruct Engineering Consultancy has formulated an optimized implementation matrix for low-rise structures:

  • Primary Structural Framework (Columns, Beams, Sloof, Ring Balk): Must exclusively utilize Deformed Bars (minimum grade BJTS 420) with a minimum nominal diameter of $12\text{ mm}$ ($D12$).
  • Secondary / Distribution Elements (Stirrups, Ties, Slabs): Plain bars (grade BJTP 280) may be utilized for stirrups ($d8$ or $d10$) and structural floor slab meshes where shear confinement and shrinkage cracking controls dominate over high tensile bond requirements.

By adhering to this hybridization model, project owners can protect their structures against structural collapse while saving up to 30% on steel weight budgets compared to oversized plain bar designs.

7. Conclusion

The selection between deformed and plain reinforcing bars cannot be treated as a mere financial line-item substitution. Deformed bars provide a mechanically superior stress-transfer system via continuous micro-interlocking. This performance translates directly to elevated load capacity, substantial energy dissipation during earthquakes, and long-term crack control. For residential and villa projects in Bali, utilizing systematic structural designs proposed by professional entities like Neurostruct ensures safety compliance and fiscal prudence.

2. Tinjauan Pustaka: Mengapa Besi Polos Bisa "Terpleset" di Dalam Beton?

Kekuatan sejati dari beton bertulang adalah hasil kerja sama (simbiosis mutualisme) antara beton yang kuat menahan gaya tekan dan baja yang kuat menahan gaya tarik. Kemampuan transfer gaya ini diikat oleh satu parameter krusial: Kuat Rekat (Bond Stress, $\tau$).

Menurut penelitian fundamental yang dipublikasikan oleh Supriyanto (2021), mekanisme lekat ini terdiri dari aksi adhesi kimiawi, gesekan fisik (friksi), dan kuncian mekanis (interlocking). Pada besi polos, kuncian mekanis bernilai nol. Ketika bangunan menerima beban siklik gempa, lekatan kimiawi besi polos akan hancur seketika pada getaran pertama. Akibatnya, besi polos akan mengalami gejala slip atau tergelincir di dalam selimut beton (Supriyanto & Ramadhan, 2023). Ketika besi kehilangan daya rekatnya, balok dan kolom beton akan retak menganga dan berujung pada keruntuhan tiba-tiba (brittle failure).

Di sisi lain, besi ulir tidak mengandalkan kebaikan adhesi kimia semata. Sirip-sirip pada besi ulir berfungsi sebagai "jangkar mikro" yang mencengkeram dinding-dinding beton interior secara absolut. Supriyanto, Utama, dan Putra (2024) menegaskan bahwa pola deformasi sirip ini mengalihkan tegangan geser murni menjadi tegangan tekan diagonal yang menyebar rata, membuat struktur jauh lebih daktail (liat) dan mampu bergoyang mengikuti gempa tanpa patah.

3. Formulasi Matematika Struktur: Hitungan Hitam di Atas Putih SNI

Jangan percaya klaim tanpa rumus. Mari kita bedah standar deviasi dan kebutuhan panjang penyaluran (development length) berdasarkan SNI 2847:2019. Panjang penyaluran ($L_d$) adalah panjang minimum besi yang harus tertanam di dalam beton agar besi tersebut tidak tercabut saat mencapai tegangan lelehnya.

Rumus standar internasional untuk menghitung $L_d$ pada besi ulir adalah sebagai berikut:

$$L_d = \left( \frac{3}{40} \cdot \frac{f_y}{\lambda \sqrt{f'_c}} \cdot \frac{\psi_t \psi_e \psi_s}{\left(\frac{c_b + k_{tr}}{d_b}\right)} \right) \cdot d_b$$

Ketika kita mengganti parameter besi dengan besi polos (BJTP), tiadanya faktor cengkeraman mekanis ($k_{tr}$) memaksa seluruh kode konstruksi internasional memberikan penalti berat. Panjang penyaluran besi polos wajib dikalikan dua kali lipat:

$$L_{d,\text{polos}} = 2.0 \cdot L_{d,\text{ulir}}$$

Artinya apa bagi dompet Anda? Jika Anda menggunakan besi polos, Anda membutuhkan panjang penanaman dan overlapping (sambungan lewatan) besi dua kali lebih panjang dibandingkan jika Anda menggunakan besi ulir! Ini adalah ironi terbesar: Niat hemat membeli besi polos yang harganya sedikit lebih murah, justru boros di volume tonase total besi karena panjang sambungan yang membengkak.
