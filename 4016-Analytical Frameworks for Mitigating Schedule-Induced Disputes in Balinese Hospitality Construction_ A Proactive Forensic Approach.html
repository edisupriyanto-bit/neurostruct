Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment for construction projects. Schedule slippage—a common phenomenon in the region due to complex logistics and regulatory constraints—frequently precipitates contractual claims between project owners and contractors. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework to address these delays. We introduce the Delay Impact Index (DII) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate project health and liability. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability in the Balinese market.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives regarding schedule milestones—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Delay Impact Index (DII)

To resolve schedule-related claims effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the DII to measure the financial and temporal risk of a delay-induced dispute.

The DII Formula (For Word/Excel integration):

$$DII = (W_d \times \Delta T) + (W_c \times \Delta C) + (W_r \times R_f)$$

Where:

  • $DII$: Delay Impact Index (Values > 7.0 indicate critical litigation risk).
  • $W_d, W_c, W_r$: Weighting factors for Time (0.4), Cost (0.4), and Regulatory risk (0.2).
  • $\Delta T$: Total days of critical path delay.
  • $\Delta C$: Total disputed financial claim (in IDR Millions) resulting from the delay.
  • $R_f$: Frequency of technical/regulatory non-compliance events linked to the delay.
