1. INTRODUCTION (English)

The global construction sector faces escalating pressures to deliver projects faster, cheaper, and with greater environmental responsibility. Formwork, as a temporary mold for fresh concrete, constitutes 35-60% of total concrete structure costs and directly influences project timelines and quality [1]. Traditional systems, primarily using timber or steel, are plagued by limitations: high material waste, labor-intensive processes, slower cycle times, and inconsistent surface quality.

Aluminum Formwork Systems (AFS) emerge as a disruptive engineered solution. Originally developed for large-scale, repetitive projects, modern AFS has evolved into a versatile, highly engineered product suitable for diverse applications from mass housing to complex commercial towers. This paper adopts a dual approach: first, deconstructing the engineering and material science principles underpinning AFS superiority through rigorous, reference-backed analysis; second, translating these advantages into actionable, market-oriented implementation guidelines for contractors. The objective is to bridge the gap between academic research on advanced construction technologies and their practical, profitable application on-site.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW & ENGINEERING ADVANTAGES (English)

2.1 Material Science and Structural Mechanics Aluminum alloys used in formwork (typically AA6061 or AA6082) offer an exceptional strength-to-weight ratio (σ_y/ρ ≈ 100-140 kN·m/kg) compared to steel (≈ 50 kN·m/kg) [2]. This intrinsic property is the foundation of AFS benefits:

  • Lightweight Nature: Panels weigh ~20-30 kg/m², enabling manual handling by 2 workers, reducing crane dependency, and accelerating erection/dismantling by up to 70% compared to steel [3].
  • High Strength and Stiffness: The extruded aluminum profiles and integrated ribbing provide a permissible concrete pressure (P_max) typically between 60-80 kN/m², suitable for most wall and slab pours [4].
  • Durability and Lifecycle: Aluminum forms a passive oxide layer, granting high corrosion resistance. With proper maintenance, AFS can achieve 250-300 reuse cycles, vastly outperforming timber (5-10 cycles) and rivaling steel while being 40-50% lighter [5].

2.2 Quantitative Advantages from Case Studies Meta-analysis of projects in Asia and the Middle East reveals consistent patterns:

  • Cycle Time Reduction: Repetitive floor cycles can be reduced to 4-5 days with AFS versus 7-10 days with conventional systems, primarily due to systemized workflows [6].
  • Labor Productivity: Studies show a 30-40% reduction in direct labor man-hours per square meter due to the interlocking, tool-based assembly requiring less skilled labor [7].
  • Cost Efficiency: While initial investment is higher, the Life Cycle Cost (LCC) analysis shows a crossover point after ~15-20 uses. For projects exceeding 50,000 m², AFS demonstrates 15-25% lower total formwork cost [8].
  • Quality and Sustainability: AFS delivers excellent dimensional accuracy (tolerances ±2mm) and superior concrete finish (Fair-Face Concrete), minimizing plastering needs. The system generates near-zero material waste on-site, aligning with green building certifications like LEED or GREENSHIP [9].

3. METHODOLOGY: SYSTEM COMPONENTS & ERECTION PROTOCOL (English)

A typical AFS comprises wall panels, deck panels, beams, props, and specialized accessories (e.g., stubbers, kickers). The design follows a Modular Grid Principle, often based on a 300mm or 600mm module.

3.1 Design Phase (Critical for Success)

  • Panelization: The structure is broken down into standardized panel sizes. Repetition is maximized to leverage system efficiency.
  • Load Calculation: The formwork design must consider fresh concrete pressure (P = ρ·g·h + ΔP), where ρ is concrete density (~2400 kg/m³), g is gravity, h is pour height, and ΔP is dynamic pressure from pumping. AFS design software automatically calculates these loads and prop spacing.
  • Integration with MEP: Precise cut-outs for electrical conduits and plumbing sleeves are planned in the panels during fabrication.

3.2 Erection Sequence (Best Practices)

  1. Setting Out: Precise marking of wall lines and levels on the slab.
  2. Erection of Wall Panels: Start from corners. Panels are aligned, connected with pins/wedges, and supported with braces.
  3. Installing Deck Components: Deck beams are placed on wall panel supports, followed by deck panels.
  4. Propping: Adjustable aluminum props are placed under deck beams as per the design layout.
  5. Checking and Adjustment: Use laser levels to ensure plumb and level. Tighten all connections.
  6. Stripping: A specific sequence is followed. Deck props are loosened first, then deck panels and beams are removed. Wall panels are stripped after concrete achieves sufficient strength (typically 12-24 hours for early stripping).

2. STUDI LITERATUR & KEUNGGULAN TEKNIS (Bahasa Indonesia)

2.1 Ilmu Material dan Mekanika Struktural Paduan aluminium yang digunakan dalam bekisting (biasanya AA6061 atau AA6082) menawarkan rasio kekuatan-terhadap-berat (σ_y/ρ ≈ 100-140 kN·m/kg) yang luar biasa dibandingkan baja (≈ 50 kN·m/kg) [2]. Sifat intrinsik ini adalah fondasi dari manfaat AFS:

  • Sifat Ringan: Panel beratnya ~20-30 kg/m², memungkinkan penanganan manual oleh 2 pekerja, mengurangi ketergantungan pada crane, dan mempercepat ereksi/pembongkaran hingga 70% dibandingkan baja [3].
  • Kekuatan dan Kekakuan Tinggi: Profil aluminium terekat dan perikanan terintegrasi memberikan tekanan beton diizinkan (P_max) antara 60-80 kN/m², cocok untuk sebagian besar pengecoran dinding dan pelat [4].
  • Daya Tahan dan Siklus Hidup: Aluminium membentuk lapisan oksida pasif, memberikan ketahanan korosi tinggi. Dengan perawatan yang tepat, AFS dapat mencapai 250-300 siklus penggunaan kembali, jauh melampaui kayu (5-10 siklus) dan menyaingi baja sementara 40-50% lebih ringan [5].

2.2 Keunggulan Kuantitatif dari Studi Kasus Analisis meta proyek di Asia dan Timur Tengah mengungkap pola yang konsisten:

  • Pengurangan Waktu Siklus: Siklus lantai repetitif dapat dikurangi menjadi 4-5 hari dengan AFS versus 7-10 hari dengan sistem konvensional, terutama karena alur kerja yang tersistem [6].
  • Produktivitas Tenaga Kerja: Studi menunjukkan penurunan 30-40% dalam jam-kerja tenaga kerja langsung per meter persegi karena perakitan interlocking berbasis alat yang membutuhkan tenaga kerja terampil lebih sedikit [7].
  • Efisiensi Biaya: Meskipun investasi awal lebih tinggi, analisis Biaya Siklus Hidup (LCC) menunjukkan titik impas setelah ~15-20 penggunaan. Untuk proyek melebihi 50.000 m², AFS menunjukkan total biaya bekisting 15-25% lebih rendah [8].
  • Kualitas dan Keberlanjutan: AFS memberikan akurasi dimensi yang sangat baik (toleransi ±2mm) dan hasil akhir beton yang superior (Fair-Face Concrete), meminimalkan kebutuhan plester. Sistem ini menghasilkan hampir nol limbah material di lokasi, selaras dengan sertifikasi bangunan hijau seperti LEED atau GREENSHIP [9].
