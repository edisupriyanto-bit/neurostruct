1. Introduction

Reinforced concrete columns are primary vertical load-bearing elements that must be constructed with high precision to ensure structural integrity, especially under seismic loading per SNI 1726:2019. Professional formwork techniques directly affect concrete quality, cover accuracy, surface finish, and construction productivity. SNI 2847:2019 Section 26.11 specifies formwork requirements, including tight joints to prevent leakage, chamfer strips for edges, and pre-pour inspections.

2. Literature Review and Code Framework

SNI 2847:2019 adopts modified ACI 318M-14 provisions for structural concrete, with indirect but critical implications for formwork through requirements for reinforcement placement, cover, and concrete consolidation. International best practices are drawn from ACI 347 Guide to Formwork for Concrete, which provides detailed guidance on load calculations, safety factors, and construction techniques. Studies on Indonesian projects indicate that system formwork (aluminum or steel) can reduce cycle times and labor costs significantly compared to conventional methods while delivering superior surface quality. In Bali’s high-seismic and humid coastal conditions, precise formwork ensures proper alignment for confinement ties and minimizes defects from rapid drying.

3. Formwork Design Principles and Load Calculations

Formwork must resist vertical loads (self-weight, concrete, live loads) and lateral pressure from fresh concrete. Lateral pressure is a key design parameter.

Simplified lateral pressure formula (adapted from ACI 347 practices for columns with internal vibration):

p = C_w × C_c × [150 + 9000 × R / (T + 18)] (kPa)

Where:

- C_w = weight coefficient (1.0 for normal-density concrete)

- C_c = chemistry coefficient (1.0 for Type I cement without retarders)

- R = rate of placement (m/h)

- T = concrete temperature (°C)

Pressure is limited by:

p_max ≤ 150 × h (hydrostatic limit, where h = height in meters)

or minimum values per code.

Copy-paste ready example (Microsoft Word compatible):

For a 4.5 m high column, placement rate R = 1.5 m/h, T = 28°C (typical Bali tropical condition):

p ≈ 1.0 × 1.0 × [150 + 9000 × 1.5 / (28 + 18)] = 150 + 13500 / 46 ≈ 150 + 293.5 ≈ 443.5 kPa

Apply limits: p_max = 150 × 4.5 = 675 kPa (use the lower calculated value with safety factor 1.5–2.0 for design).

Design accessories (ties, walers, braces) with appropriate safety factors (typically 2.0 for reusable systems).

4. Advanced Formwork Techniques and Material Selection

# 4.1 Conventional Timber/Plywood Formwork

Suitable for irregular or one-off columns. Uses marine plywood (12–18 mm thick) with timber studs and walers. Advantages: low initial cost and high flexibility. Disadvantages: slower reuse cycles and potential for surface imperfections.

# 4.2 Semi-System and Full-System Formwork (Recommended Best Practice)

- Aluminum Panel Systems: Lightweight, modular, high reuse (hundreds of cycles), excellent finish quality.

- Steel Panel Systems: Robust for heavy-duty applications, precise dimensions.

Best techniques include:

- Modular panels with quick-release clamps or ties.

- Adjustable corners for rectangular or square columns.

- Integrated chamfer strips (20–25 mm) for clean edges.

Erection Best Practices Sequence:

1. Survey and mark column positions accurately.

2. Pre-assemble panels around reinforcement cage (or install rebar inside closed forms).

3. Install form ties at designed spacing to resist lateral pressure.

4. Apply external bracing and props for plumb and stability (verticality tolerance 1:400 or better).

5. Use laser or theodolite for alignment.

6. Inspect for tightness, cover spacers, and cleanliness before concreting.

5. Integration with Reinforcement, Concreting, and Quality Control

Formwork must accommodate SNI 2847:2019 reinforcement requirements (minimum cover 20–40 mm depending on exposure, proper spacing of ties for confinement in seismic zones). Use plastic or concrete spacers to maintain cover.

During concreting: control placement rate to manage pressure; use internal vibrators carefully to avoid segregation or form displacement.

Stripping time: Typically 12–24 hours for columns once concrete reaches adequate strength (e.g., 70% of f'c), followed by moist curing for at least 7 days in Bali’s humid climate to prevent cracking.

Tolerances: Cross-section ±6 mm, verticality per SNI/ACI recommendations.

6. Safety, Sustainability, and Field Challenges in Bali

Implement guardrails, safe access, and wind bracing. In Bali projects (villas, resorts), exposed columns often require premium finishes achievable with system formwork. Sustainability benefits from high-reuse aluminum systems that reduce timber consumption.

Common challenges and best-practice solutions:

- Rapid evaporation in tropical sun → use curing compounds or protective covers.

- Seismic detailing → ensure formwork allows precise placement of closely spaced confinement ties.
