Abstract

The nexus between legal land ownership and physical construction accuracy is often a primary point of failure in civil engineering projects. This research investigates the function of land surveying as a dual-purpose tool for establishing legal boundaries (cadastral) and ensuring geometric precision in infrastructure. By utilizing High-Precision Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) and rigorous coordinate transformation protocols, surveyors bridge the gap between National Land Agency (BPN) certificates and physical architectural layouts. The study concludes that early-stage surveyor involvement mitigates litigation risks and prevents structural deviations that could lead to significant financial loss.

1. Introduction

In the global construction landscape, legal certainty is the bedrock of project viability. Land surveying acts as the "legal-technical" bridge. Especially in rapidly developing regions like Bali, where land tenure systems are multifaceted, the surveyor ensures that the building resides strictly within its legal envelope. A deviation of just a few centimeters can result in "Encroachment," leading to court-ordered demolition or permanent legal entanglements.

2. Technical Framework: Harmonizing Legal and Physical Realities

The surveyor’s primary technical task is the verification of the Certificate of Title against the physical site. This involves the establishment of a localized "Control Network." The relationship between the geodetic coordinates $(\phi, \lambda)$ and the local project grid $(N, E)$ is managed via the following transformation model:

$$E = X_0 + s \cdot (x \cdot \cos \theta - y \cdot \sin \theta)$$

$$N = Y_0 + s \cdot (x \cdot \sin \theta + y \cdot \cos \theta)$$

Where $(X_0, Y_0)$ are translation parameters, $s$ is the scale factor, and $\theta$ is the rotation angle. This mathematical rigor ensures that the physical construction aligns perfectly with the legal certificate coordinates.

3. Accuracy in Volumetric and Structural Placement

Land surveying also functions as the basis for calculating volumetric excavation. The accuracy of the Digital Terrain Model (DTM) is verified using the Standard Error formula:

$$\sigma = \pm \sqrt{\frac{\sum (v^2)}{n-1}}$$

Where $v$ is the residual of measurements and $n$ is the number of observations. High $\sigma$ values indicate poor survey quality, which poses a direct risk to structural placement and budget adherence.

2. Analisis Teknis: Presisi Koordinat untuk Keamanan Hukum

Surveyor menggunakan koordinat global untuk memastikan posisi lahan tidak bergeser. Dalam menentukan ketepatan posisi titik bangunan terhadap batas lahan, digunakan perhitungan deviasi standar posisi:

$$\text{Akurasi Posisi} = \sqrt{MS E_x^2 + MS E_y^2}$$

Nilai akurasi ini harus berada di bawah ambang batas toleransi nasional (SNI) agar data survey dapat diterima sebagai bukti legal dan teknis yang sah. Dengan data ini, arsitek dapat merancang bangunan dengan tenang, karena posisi lahan sudah terkunci secara matematis.
