Abstract

Construction disputes frequently require robust technical evidence obtained through systematic field investigations and detailed engineering assessments to reach fair and efficient resolutions. This paper explores technical and field investigation-based approaches as a foundational methodology for resolving disputes in building and infrastructure projects. By combining on-site inspections, non-destructive testing, material sampling, geotechnical evaluations, and forensic documentation, these investigations deliver objective, verifiable data essential for mediation, arbitration, and litigation. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to propose an integrated investigation framework. Particular emphasis is placed on the unique environmental and technical challenges in Bali, Indonesia, including volcanic soils, seismic activity, high rainfall, and rapid tourism-driven development. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to enhance data accuracy, analysis depth, and overall dispute resolution effectiveness. Practical guidelines are provided for project stakeholders, consultants, and legal practitioners seeking transparent, evidence-based outcomes.

### 1. Introduction

Effective resolution of construction disputes relies heavily on accurate, site-specific technical data obtained through rigorous field investigations. Technical and field investigation approaches bridge the gap between theoretical claims and real-world conditions, providing impartial evidence that supports fair settlements.

In Bali, the combination of challenging site conditions and accelerated construction timelines makes field-based technical investigations particularly valuable for resolving disputes in villas, hotels, resorts, and infrastructure projects. This paper details methodologies, frameworks, and best practices in this domain.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research underscores the importance of field investigations in forensic engineering and dispute resolution. Studies published in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Forensic Engineering* (ICE), *Engineering Failure Analysis*, and *Buildings* highlight the effectiveness of integrated on-site testing, documentation protocols, and multi-source data triangulation. Recent works emphasize the role of digital field tools and systematic investigation frameworks in improving evidentiary quality.

### 3. Methodology

This study employs a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus sources (2015–2025), analysis of international and regional case studies, and synthesis of established forensic field investigation protocols. A six-phase technical and field investigation framework is proposed.

### 4. Core Components of Technical and Field Investigations

4.1 On-Site Inspection and Documentation

Systematic visual surveys, crack mapping, and photographic/video recording.

4.2 Non-Destructive Testing (NDT)

Ultrasonic testing, rebound hammer, ground-penetrating radar (GPR), and half-cell potential surveys.

4.3 Geotechnical Field Investigation

Soil boring, standard penetration tests (SPT), and plate load testing, critical for Bali’s volcanic terrain.

Bali’s construction environment presents distinctive difficulties including variable volcanic soils, seismic hazards, high groundwater tables, heavy seasonal rainfall, and cultural site constraints. Field investigations must align with Indonesian Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services and SNI standards while meeting international forensic benchmarks.
