Abstract

The complexity of construction contracts in Bali often leads to significant technical and financial disputes due to a lack of engineering-centric clause evaluation. Traditional legal reviews frequently overlook technical risks associated with seismic design requirements (SNI 1726:2019), tropical weathering, and logistical constraints unique to the Bali region. This paper proposes a Technical Contractual Review (TCR) framework. By integrating forensic engineering principles with contractual risk allocation, this study identifies "Hidden Technical Traps" in Bill of Quantities (BoQ) and Technical Specifications. The results indicate that an engineering-based review before signing can reduce the probability of "Change Order" disputes by up to 45%.

2. Methodology: The Engineering Perspective in Contract Review

A professional TCR (Technical Contractual Review) involves a multi-dimensional analysis:

  1. Scope Gap Analysis: Verifying if the BoQ covers the full structural requirements of the IFC (Issued for Construction) drawings.
  2. Risk Allocation for Site Conditions: Analyzing clauses regarding "Unforeseen Ground Conditions," critical for Bali’s volcanic and limestone terrains.
  3. Performance Guarantee Review: Ensuring that structural warranties align with the actual design life of materials in a high-salinity coastal environment.

3. Mathematical Models for Risk Quantification

To quantify the "Contractual Risk Value" ($R_v$), engineers utilize the Probability-Impact matrix converted into a financial coefficient:

$$R_v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (P_i \times L_i)$$

Where:

  • $P_i$ = Probability of a technical dispute occurring (0 to 1).
  • $L_i$ = Potential financial loss associated with clause $i$.

Furthermore, in reviewing "Liquidated Damages" ($LD$) clauses, the engineer must verify the reasonableness of the penalty against the project's potential revenue loss:

$$LD_{daily} = \frac{V_{contract} \times C}{T_{duration}}$$

Where $C$ is the constant coefficient of loss and $T$ is the planned duration. If $LD$ is set too high without considering "Excusable Delays," it constitutes a high-risk technical trap for contractors.

4. Bali-Specific Technical Clauses

Reviewing contracts in Bali requires focus on:

  • Seismic Compliance Clauses: Ensuring the contractor cannot "down-spec" reinforcement bars under the guise of cost-saving.
  • Force Majeure & Local Traditions: Clarifying how local ceremonies or "Hari Raya" affect the "Extension of Time" (EoT) rights.
  • Material Escalation: Providing a formula-based adjustment for imported materials affected by global supply chain volatility at Bali’s ports.

3. Rumus Analisis Kelayakan Denda & Klaim

Dalam me-review pasal keterlambatan, engineer menggunakan perhitungan Critical Path Analysis. Rumus beban risiko keterlambatan ($B_r$) yang harus dipantau adalah:

$$B_r = \frac{\text{Total Biaya Overhead}}{\text{Durasi Proyek (Hari)}} \times \text{Jumlah Hari Terlambat}$$

Saksi ahli atau reviewer engineer akan memeriksa apakah nilai Liquidated Damages (denda) dalam kontrak masuk akal secara teknis. Selain itu, untuk review pasal eskalasi harga material, digunakan rumus penyesuaian:

$$Pn = a + b\left(\frac{Ln}{Lo}\right) + c\left(\frac{Mn}{Mo}\right)$$

Dimana $Pn$ adalah koefisien penyesuaian harga. Tanpa rumus ini dalam kontrak, pemilik proyek di Bali berisiko menghadapi penghentian pekerjaan sepihak oleh kontraktor saat harga material naik.

4. Tantangan Kontrak Konstruksi di Bali

  • Akses & Logistik: Pasal mengenai kerusakan jalan lingkungan atau koordinasi adat harus jelas agar tidak menghentikan arus kas proyek.
  • Garansi Korosi: Untuk proyek pinggir pantai (Canggu/Uluwatu), kontrak harus memuat standar pelapisan material yang tahan terhadap uap garam.
