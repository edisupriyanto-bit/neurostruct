Abstract

Construction disputes and claims frequently escalate due to technical complexities, differing interpretations of contract requirements, and insufficient objective evidence. This paper investigates the pivotal role of expert technical services in mediation processes for construction disputes and project claims. Through independent forensic analysis, structural and geotechnical assessments, delay quantification, and impartial expert testimony, technical experts facilitate informed negotiations and higher settlement rates in mediation. The study draws upon Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to propose a structured expert-mediated framework. Special emphasis is placed on the challenges in Bali, Indonesia, where rapid tourism infrastructure and building development create unique seismic, geotechnical, and regulatory complexities. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to enhance the quality, credibility, and efficiency of expert input during mediation. Practical recommendations are provided for project stakeholders, mediators, and legal practitioners seeking faster, cost-effective, and technically sound resolutions.

### 1. Introduction

Mediation has become the preferred alternative dispute resolution (ADR) method in the construction industry due to its flexibility, confidentiality, and potential to preserve business relationships. Expert technical professionals play a central role by providing clear, evidence-based explanations of complex engineering issues that enable parties to reach informed settlements.

In Bali’s booming construction sector, disputes over delays, defects, variations, and cost claims in villas, hotels, resorts, and infrastructure projects increasingly require expert technical mediation support. This paper presents methodologies and frameworks for effective expert involvement in mediation.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed studies consistently demonstrate the positive impact of expert technical input in construction mediation. Research by Cheung and colleagues on mediator tactics, combined with works in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *International Journal of Project Management*, and *Buildings*, shows that neutral technical experts significantly increase settlement rates and reduce resolution time. Recent literature highlights the integration of forensic engineering and digital tools in mediation processes.

### 3. Methodology

This study employs a systematic literature review of high-impact Scopus journals (2015–2025), analysis of international mediation case studies, and synthesis of best-practice frameworks for expert technical services. A five-phase expert mediation support model is proposed.
