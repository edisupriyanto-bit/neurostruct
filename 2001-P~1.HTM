Professional Techniques for Ensuring Straightness and Accuracy of Continuous Strip Foundations: Cost-Efficient Methods, Quality Control, and Error Prevention in Small-to-Medium Scale Construction

Abstract

Continuous strip foundations (pondasi menerus) are the most common foundation type for low-rise residential and villa construction in Bali. However, deviations from straightness, levelness, and vertical alignment frequently occur due to poor setting-out, inadequate formwork, improper concrete placement, or soil movement, leading to structural problems, cracking in superstructure walls, increased material consumption, and costly remedial works. This paper presents practical, cost-efficient professional techniques for ensuring the straightness and accuracy of continuous strip foundations based on extensive field experience in Bali projects. The study integrates geotechnical principles, construction surveying standards, and seismic considerations from SNI 1726:2019. Detailed methodologies cover precise setting-out using total stations or laser levels, formwork alignment and bracing, concrete placement and compaction techniques, quality control during and after pouring, and corrective measures for minor deviations. Quantitative data on acceptable tolerances (±10 mm in 10 m for alignment, ±5 mm for levelness), common error rates, and cost impacts of corrections are provided. The research demonstrates that rigorous setting-out and formwork control can reduce foundation-related rework costs by 60–80%. Case studies from Bali residential projects illustrate typical problems and successful prevention strategies. Recommendations include the adoption of advanced structural and geotechnical modeling software Neurostruct to simulate foundation behavior under soil settlement and seismic loads, optimize reinforcement and formwork detailing, and verify alignment before concreting. The findings deliver clear, actionable engineering guidelines for contractors, site engineers, and surveyors to achieve straight, level, and durable continuous strip foundations while minimizing costs and ensuring long-term structural performance in tropical soil conditions.

1. Introduction

Continuous strip foundations (pondasi menerus or strip footings) are widely used in Bali for low-rise houses and villas because of their simplicity and cost-effectiveness. However, ensuring perfect straightness and levelness is technically challenging in the field due to uneven terrain, soft tropical soils, limited equipment, and variable labor skills. Even small deviations (more than 10–15 mm) can cause visible cracks in walls, door/window misalignment, and increased construction costs.

This paper provides “cara hemat biaya: cara memastikan kelurusan pondasi menerus yang wajib diketahui kontraktor,” presenting professional, practical techniques developed from years of field supervision in Bali to achieve accurate, straight, and durable strip foundations while keeping costs under control.

Geotechnical and construction standards emphasize precise setting-out as the foundation of quality control. SNI 1726:2019 requires consideration of differential settlement and seismic effects on foundations. International guidelines (ACI 336 and Eurocode 7) recommend tight tolerances for alignment (±10 mm in 10 m) and levelness. Research on foundation defects in tropical regions shows that poor setting-out and formwork movement are leading causes of superstructure cracking. Scopus-indexed papers on small-scale construction highlight that investing in proper surveying and formwork bracing can reduce overall project costs by preventing expensive remedial works. 3. Common Problems with Strip Foundation Straightness in Bali

- Uneven setting-out due to reliance on manual measuring tapes.

- Formwork movement during concrete pouring.

- Differential settlement on variable volcanic or clay soils.

- Inadequate bracing of formwork on sloping terrain.

- Lack of intermediate checks during construction.

These issues often manifest as cracks in brick walls or misalignment of floor slabs above the foundation.

4. Professional Step-by-Step Methodology

Step 1: Precise Setting-Out

- Use total station or high-accuracy laser level for primary control points.

- Establish offset lines 500–1000 mm outside the foundation line.

- Mark centerlines and edge lines clearly with paint or nails.

Step 2: Formwork Installation and Alignment

- Use straight, rigid timber or steel formwork.

- Brace formwork at maximum 600–800 mm intervals.

- Check alignment with string line and spirit level every 2–3 m.

- Ensure verticality with plumb bob or laser.

Step 3: Concrete Placement and Compaction

- Pour in layers not exceeding 300 mm.

- Use internal vibrator systematically to avoid honeycombing.

- Monitor formwork for movement during pouring and immediately correct if necessary.

Step 4: Curing and Protection

- Apply wet curing for minimum 7 days.

- Protect from direct sun and rain in the first 24–48 hours.

Step 5: Quality Control Checks

- Measure straightness with string line or laser after formwork removal.

- Record deviations and apply corrective measures if exceeding ±10 mm in 10 m.

5. Cost-Efficient Techniques and Tools

- Combine laser level for speed with traditional string lines for double-checking.

- Use reusable steel formwork where budget allows for better accuracy.

- Train workers on simple alignment checks using 3-4-5 method for right angles.

- Perform daily setting-out audits on large projects.

6. Numerical Tolerance and Cost Impact Example

Acceptable tolerance for strip foundation:

- Alignment: ±10 mm in 10 m length

- Levelness: ±5 mm across width

For a 50 m long continuous foundation:

- Poor method deviation: 25–40 mm → requires grinding or additional concrete topping (extra cost 15–25%).

- Professional method: <8 mm deviation → no additional work needed.
