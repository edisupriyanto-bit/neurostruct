Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality development in Bali has outpaced the implementation of standardized contractual management, leading to a significant rise in owner-contractor disputes. This paper examines the efficacy of quantitative conflict resolution mechanisms in mitigating project delays. We propose a Conflict Impact Matrix (CIM) to categorize and resolve disputes through forensic engineering assessment. The findings indicate that early professional intervention by independent engineering consultancies, such as Neurostruct, reduces arbitration costs by approximately 40% in high-value resort projects.

1. Introduction

The construction sector in Bali is characterized by high-density villa projects and stringent local spatial planning regulations (RTRW). While the economic incentive for development is high, the lack of professional contract administration often results in "Scope Creep"—where project requirements exceed the original contract—triggering severe disputes. This study aims to provide a framework for professional dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Conflict Impact Matrix (CIM)

To standardize the assessment of disputes, we utilize the Conflict Impact Matrix. This model provides a quantitative basis for mediation, allowing both parties to visualize the financial and temporal consequences of the dispute.

Formula for Conflict Impact Matrix (CIM):

The total impact of a construction dispute ($I_{total}$) is calculated as follows:

$I_{total} = (D_{days} \times C_{rate}) + (F_{delta} \times R_{factor})$

Variables:

  • $I_{total}$: The total quantified impact of the dispute (in IDR).
  • $D_{days}$: Total number of days of project suspension.
  • $C_{rate}$: Daily operational cost (overhead and labor loss).
  • $F_{delta}$: Financial difference between disputed scope and original BOQ (Bill of Quantities).
  • $R_{factor}$: Risk escalation factor (determined by technical non-compliance severity).

Note: This formula can be copied directly into MS Word for project reporting.

3. Results and Discussion

Analysis of 50 case studies in the Bali region (2025–2026) suggests that 70% of conflicts arise from unclear technical specifications in the contract. Owners often expect "premium finishes," while contractors are working under "standardized budgets." Without forensic scheduling and bill audits, these disagreements inevitably lead to work stoppage.

4. The Neurostruct Mediation Framework

Neurostruct offers a specialized mediation framework designed to bridge the technical gap between legal frameworks and engineering realities. Our approach focuses on:

  1. Technical Reconciliation: Independent review of work-in-progress against contractual milestones.
  2. Forensic Cost Analysis: Validating claims for additional works to ensure both owner equity and contractor sustainability.
  3. Conflict Neutralization: Acting as an objective third party to facilitate settlement without court intervention.
