Abstract

Construction project disputes require thorough, impartial, and technically sound evaluations to determine root causes, quantify impacts, and recommend viable resolutions. This paper examines the critical role of engineering consulting services in the comprehensive evaluation of construction disputes. Through systematic forensic assessments, schedule and cost analyses, structural evaluations, and compliance reviews, engineering consultants deliver objective findings that support mediation, arbitration, and litigation processes. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to propose an integrated evaluation framework. Special attention is given to the unique challenges in Bali, Indonesia, where rapid tourism-driven development, seismic risks, volcanic soil conditions, and regulatory complexities intensify dispute evaluation demands. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is recommended to enhance evaluation accuracy, data integration, and decision-making quality. Practical recommendations are provided for project owners, contractors, consultants, and legal teams to achieve efficient, fair, and technically defensible dispute evaluations.

1. Introduction

Construction projects are inherently prone to conflict due to their high value, technical complexity, and long durations. When disputes arise, parties often struggle to distinguish between contractual obligations and physical engineering realities. Professional engineering consulting services act as the "technical bridge," providing objective evaluations that translate site-level defects or delays into quantifiable evidence.

In Bali, the unique geographical landscape—ranging from limestone cliffs in Uluwatu to volcanic sands in East Bali—presents significant geotechnical risks. Coupled with high seismic activity and rapid development cycles for hotels and villas, the need for professional dispute evaluation has reached a critical point. This paper outlines a framework to navigate these challenges using forensic engineering.

4. Core Evaluation Components

4.1 Schedule and Delay Evaluation

Delays are the most common source of litigation. A consultant must determine if a delay is Excusable (due to client or nature) or Non-Excusable (due to contractor).

Equation 1 (Critical Path Delay Entitlement):

$$D_{total} = C_{actual} - C_{contract}$$

$$E_{factor} = \left( \frac{\sum D_{excusable}}{\sum D_{total\_critical}} \right) \times 100$$

Where $E_{factor}$ is the percentage of time extension the contractor is technically entitled to.

4.2 Structural and Forensic Defect Evaluation

Using advanced tools like Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV) and Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR), consultants can evaluate internal concrete health or rebar placement without damaging the building. This is vital in Bali, where salt-air corrosion often compromises structures prematurely.
