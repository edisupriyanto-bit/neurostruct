Best Practices for Stone Rubble Foundation Construction: Achieving Superior

Strength and Durability

Introduction

Stone rubble foundation, commonly known as pondasi batu belah or pondasi batu kali in

Indonesia, remains one of the most reliable and cost-effective shallow foundation systems

for residential, low-rise buildings, and rural structures. This traditional technique uses locally

sourced split stones bonded with high-quality cement-sand mortar to create a robust base

that evenly distributes structural loads to the underlying soil. When executed with the best

techniques, stone rubble foundations offer exceptional longevity, seismic resistance, and

thermal stability—qualities that are especially valuable in Indonesia’s diverse geological and

climatic conditions.

Best practices emphasize meticulous material selection, precise execution, proper

compaction, and full mortar coverage to eliminate voids. These methods not only enhance

load-bearing capacity but also minimize long-term settlement and cracking. Compliance with

relevant Indonesian National Standards (SNI), particularly SNI 2836:2008 for unit price

calculations and material indices, ensures professionalism and safety. By adopting advanced

techniques such as layered placement, thorough soaking of stones, and systematic curing,

contractors can achieve foundations that outperform standard installations in strength and

durability.

Optimal Materials and Mix Design

Selecting premium materials is the foundation of superior workmanship. Use hard,

non-porous split stones (batu belah) sized 15–25 cm, free from cracks, organic matter, or

weak layers. Ideal stones come from river or quarry sources with high compressive strength.

Mortar mixes follow proven volume ratios for optimal performance:

●​ 1 Portland Cement (PC) : 3 Sand (PP) – for high-load applications

●​ 1 PC : 4 PP – balanced strength and economy

●​ 1 PC : 5 PP – most common for residential foundations, providing excellent

workability and durability

Cement must comply with SNI 15-2049-2014. Sand should be clean, well-graded river sand

with less than 5% silt or clay. Pre-soaking stones prevents premature absorption of mixing

water, ensuring strong bonding. In seismic zones, incorporating a reinforced concrete plinth

beam (sloof) on top of the rubble foundation significantly improves structural integrity and

ties the entire building together.

Best Execution Techniques Step by Step

Implementing best practices requires disciplined sequencing:

1.​ Site Investigation and Excavation: Conduct soil testing to determine bearing

capacity. Excavate trenches to the designed depth and width (typically 1.5–2 times

the wall thickness). Slope sides at 10° outward for stability and remove all soft or

organic soil.

2.​ Base Preparation: Compact the trench bottom to at least 95% Proctor density using

mechanical tampers or plate compactors. Lay a 10–15 cm layer of clean sand,

moisten it thoroughly, and compact again to create a level, stable bedding.

3.​ Formwork and Alignment: Install temporary formwork or use guide profiles

(bouwplank) for precise alignment. Ensure verticality and horizontality using spirit

levels and plumb bobs.

4.​ Stone Placement with Superior Technique: Soak stones in water for at least 30

minutes before placement. Begin with the largest stones at the base for maximum

stability. Arrange stones in interlocking patterns, staggering joints like brickwork to

maximize contact and shear resistance. Fill all voids and gaps completely with mortar

of creamy consistency. Build in layers of 30–40 cm, compacting each layer firmly with

tamping rods to eliminate air pockets. Pay special attention to the core—ensure it is

as dense as the faces.

5.​ Integration with Reinforcement: For enhanced performance, install starter bars or

anchors that will connect to the column or ring beam above. In earthquake-prone

areas, integrate a reinforced concrete plinth band at the top of the foundation.

6.​ Curing and Protection: Maintain continuous moist curing for a minimum of 7–14

days using wet burlap or curing compounds. Protect the fresh foundation from direct

sunlight, rain, or drying winds.

7.​ Backfilling: Backfill in thin layers (15–20 cm) with selected non-expansive soil,

compacting each layer to prevent differential settlement.

Quality Control and Inspection

Rigorous quality assurance includes visual checks for alignment, full mortar coverage, and

stone interlocking. Conduct compressive strength tests on mortar samples (target 5–10 MPa

at 28 days depending on design). Measure foundation dimensions against shop drawings

and verify compaction density. Any voids or honeycombing must be repaired immediately

with non-shrink grout.

Advantages, Challenges, and Sustainability

When built with best techniques, stone rubble foundations are 30–50% more economical

than full concrete footings, utilize local resources, reduce carbon footprint, and provide

natural damping against seismic activity. They offer excellent thermal mass for comfortable

indoor temperatures.

Challenges include the need for skilled masons and careful supervision to avoid poor

compaction. In soft soils, combine with wider footings or soil stabilization. Proper technique

mitigates these risks effectively.

Pekerjaan Pondasi Batu Belah dengan Teknik Terbaik

Pendahuluan

Pondasi batu belah atau pondasi batu kali merupakan sistem pondasi dangkal yang paling

andal dan ekonomis untuk rumah tinggal, bangunan rendah, serta proyek pedesaan di

Indonesia. Teknik tradisional ini menggunakan batu belah lokal yang diikat dengan adukan

semen-pasir berkualitas tinggi untuk menciptakan dasar yang kokoh dan mendistribusikan

beban secara merata ke tanah. Dengan menerapkan teknik terbaik, pondasi batu belah

menghasilkan kekuatan superior, ketahanan gempa, serta stabilitas termal yang

tinggi—sangat cocok dengan kondisi geologi dan iklim Indonesia yang beragam.

Teknik terbaik menekankan pemilihan bahan premium, pelaksanaan presisi, pemadatan

sempurna, serta pengisian adukan penuh tanpa rongga. Metode ini tidak hanya

meningkatkan daya dukung, tetapi juga mencegah penurunan dan retak jangka panjang.

Bahan dan Desain Campuran Optimal

Pilih batu belah keras, tidak porous, ukuran 15–25 cm, bebas retak dan bahan organik.

Adukan standar meliputi rasio 1 Semen Portland : 3 Pasir (untuk beban berat), 1:4, atau 1:5

(paling umum untuk rumah). Semen sesuai SNI 15-2049-2014 dan pasir bersih dengan

kadar lumpur rendah. Rendam batu sebelum pasang agar ikatan optimal. Untuk zona

gempa, tambahkan sloof beton bertulang di atas pondasi.

Teknik Pelaksanaan Terbaik Langkah demi Langkah

1.​ Investigasi tanah dan galian sesuai gambar, buang tanah lunak.

2.​ Padatkan dasar galian hingga 95% dan tambah lapisan pasir 10–15 cm.

3.​ Pasang profil panduan untuk keselarasan.

4.​ Rendam batu, susun secara saling mengunci berlapis-lapis (30–40 cm per lapis), isi

semua rongga dengan adukan kental, dan padatkan rapat.

5.​ Integrasikan dengan tulangan atau sloof untuk ketahanan gempa.

6.​ Lakukan curing basah minimal 7–14 hari.

7.​ Backfill bertahap dengan tanah pilihan dan padatkan.

Pengendalian Kualitas

Periksa keselarasan, kepadatan, dan tidak adanya rongga. Uji kuat tekan adukan serta ukur

dimensi sesuai gambar.

Keunggulan dan Tantangan

Keunggulan: Biaya lebih rendah 30–50%, ramah lingkungan, tahan lama, dan cocok untuk

daerah rawan gempa. Tantangan: Memerlukan tukang terampil dan pengawasan ketat, yang

dapat diatasi dengan teknik terbaik.
