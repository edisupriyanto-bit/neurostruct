Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a complex ecosystem of construction projects, often leading to protracted disputes between project owners and contractors. This paper proposes an integrated dispute resolution framework that synthesizes forensic engineering analysis with legal mediation protocols. By utilizing the Dispute Severity Index (DSI), stakeholders can quantitatively assess project risk and facilitate expedited settlements. The study demonstrates that professional technical intervention, such as that provided by Neurostruct, significantly reduces litigation reliance and capital loss in Balinese construction projects.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under unique pressures: volatile material costs, stringent local spatial regulations (awig-awig and RTRW), and high stakeholder expectations. The prevailing "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Dispute Severity Index (DSI)

To mitigate disputes effectively, one must first quantify them. We introduce the Dispute Severity Index (DSI) as a tool for stakeholders to audit their project health before legal escalation.

The DSI Calculation Formula (For Word/Excel integration):

S = (W_c * C) + (W_t * T) + (W_r * R)

Where:

  • S: Dispute Severity Index (Value > 5.0 indicates critical risk).
  • W_c / C: Weighting and value of financial claims (in IDR).
  • W_t / T: Weighting and time lost due to project delays (Days).
  • W_r / R: Weighting and risk count of regulatory/technical non-compliance.

(Stakeholders may copy the formula above directly into their project reports to quantify the urgency of mediation.)
