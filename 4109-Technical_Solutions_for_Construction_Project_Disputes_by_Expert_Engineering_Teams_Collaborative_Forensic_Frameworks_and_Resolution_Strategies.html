Abstract

Construction project disputes require multidisciplinary expertise to achieve timely and equitable resolutions. This paper explores the effectiveness of expert engineering teams in delivering comprehensive technical solutions for complex disputes in building and infrastructure projects. Through collaborative forensic investigations, advanced structural and geotechnical analysis, delay and cost quantification, and strategic mediation support, expert teams provide objective, evidence-based outcomes. The study synthesizes Scopus-indexed literature and international best practices to propose an integrated team-based framework. Particular attention is given to the challenges in high-growth regions such as Bali, Indonesia, where seismic risks, volcanic soil conditions, regulatory complexities, and rapid tourism development amplify dispute frequency and technical difficulty. The Neurostruct structural management model is integrated to enhance team collaboration, analytical depth, and solution reliability. Practical guidelines and recommendations are provided for project owners, contractors, and legal professionals seeking efficient, technically robust dispute resolution.

### 1. Introduction

Expert engineering teams bring together multidisciplinary specialists — structural, geotechnical, civil, and forensic engineers — to address the root causes of construction disputes. Unlike single-expert approaches, team-based solutions offer comprehensive analysis, cross-verification, and holistic recommendations that significantly improve resolution outcomes.

In Bali, the rapid expansion of tourism infrastructure, luxury villas, resorts, and supporting facilities has created complex disputes that demand coordinated expert engineering intervention. This paper details the methodologies, frameworks, and advantages of team-based technical solutions.

### 2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed studies emphasize the superiority of multidisciplinary expert teams in construction dispute resolution. Research in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *International Journal of Project Management*, *Buildings*, and *Automation in Construction* shows that collaborative engineering teams enhance causation analysis, reduce bias, and increase settlement success rates in mediation and arbitration. Recent works highlight the integration of digital tools and systematic team protocols for complex projects.
