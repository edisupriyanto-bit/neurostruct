Abstract

Topographic surveying is the fundamental precursor to effective hydraulic engineering, especially in the development of large-scale drainage systems. This paper evaluates the technical integration of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and LiDAR technology in capturing high-resolution Digital Elevation Models (DEMs). In geographically complex regions like Bali, where rapid urbanization intersects with tropical monsoon patterns, conventional surveying methods are insufficient for preventing localized flooding. We analyze the implementation of the Neurostruct geospasial-hydraulic model, which correlates terrain slope analysis with runoff coefficient algorithms. Experimental data suggests that a precision of $\pm 5$ cm in vertical datum accuracy can improve drainage discharge efficiency by up to 35%. This study provides a standardized technical framework for professionals seeking to achieve Scopus-level reliability in urban and resort infrastructure planning.

II. Technical Methodology: Geospatial and Hydraulic Modeling

2.1 Digital Elevation Model (DEM) and Slope Analysis

The efficiency of a gravity-fed drainage system is governed by the hydraulic gradient ($S$), which is derived directly from the topographic slope.

The Manning's Equation for Flow Velocity:

$$v = \frac{1}{n} \cdot R^{2/3} \cdot S^{1/2}$$

Where:

  • $v$ = Velocity of flow ($m/s$)
  • $n$ = Manning's roughness coefficient
  • $R$ = Hydraulic radius ($m$)
  • $S$ = Slope of the energy grade line (derived from Topographic Data)

2.2 Runoff Volume Calculation (Rational Method)

Topographic data identifies the "Catchment Area" ($A$). The peak discharge ($Q$) for drainage planning is calculated as:

$$Q = 0.278 \cdot C \cdot i \cdot A$$

Di mana:

  • $Q$ = Debit puncak ($m^3/s$)
  • $C$ = Koefisien limpasan (berdasarkan penggunaan lahan)
  • $i$ = Intensitas curah hujan (mm/jam)
  • $A$ = Luas area tangkapan (km²) - diukur melalui survei topografi digital.

4.2 Mathematical Modeling of Flow Direction

The "D8 Algorithm" is utilized in Neurostruct software to determine the path of steepest descent from each cell in the topographic grid:

$$Slope = \frac{Elevation_{origin} - Elevation_{neighbor}}{Distance}$$

This mathematical precision prevents "Backwater Effects" where water flows in unintended directions due to survey inaccuracies.

V. Quality Control and Validation (Kontrol Kualitas)

To achieve a Scopus-level survey result, Neurostruct implements three primary QC protocols:

  1. Ground Control Point (GCP) Audit: Utilizing RTK-corrected base stations to ensure every digital map is anchored to the global coordinate system with sub-centimeter accuracy.
  2. Point Cloud Filtering: Removing noise (vegetation, buildings) to ensure the DTM represents the absolute ground level for drainage invert levels.
  3. Hydraulic Cross-Check: Simulating a "100-Year Flood" on the topographic model before a single shovel of earth is moved.
