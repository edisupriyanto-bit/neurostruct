Abstract

The construction landscape in Bali, particularly in the luxury villa and boutique hospitality sector, is characterized by a high frequency of conflicts of interest between Project Owners and Contractors. These conflicts typically arise from asymmetrical information, differing quality expectations, and financial pressure regarding "Variation Orders." This paper proposes a systematic Engineering Governance Framework (EGF) to mediate and manage these conflicts. By utilizing the Independent Engineer (IE) model and data-driven verification, the study demonstrates that objective engineering evidence—such as structural audits and precise volume verification—acts as a stabilizer in high-stakes negotiations. The results indicate that projects employing this framework see a 55% reduction in litigation risk and significant improvements in structural compliance.

1. Introduction

2. Methodology: The Engineering Governance Framework (EGF)

The EGF focuses on three pillars of conflict mitigation:

  • Technical Standardization: Establishing a rigid "Quality Assurance" manual based on SNI 2847:2019 before groundbreaking.
  • Independent Progress Certification: Implementing a multi-point verification system where payments are released only after an independent engineer verifies physical volume and material strength.
  • Variation Order (VO) Adjudication: Utilizing forensic quantity surveying to price changes fairly for both parties.

3. Mathematical Framework for Conflict Quantification

To resolve disputes regarding "Value for Money" and "Work Progress," engineers utilize the Cost-Performance Reconciliation (CPR) formula:

$$V_{verified} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (Q_{act,i} \times P_{unit,i}) \times \mu_{qual}$$

Where:

  • $Q_{act,i}$ = Actual verified volume of item $i$.
  • $P_{unit,i}$ = Contracted unit price.
  • $\mu_{qual}$ = Quality Compliance Index (derived from NDT and lab test results, ranging from 0.0 to 1.0).

For structural reliability disputes, the Safety Index ($\beta$) is calculated to determine if a contractor's "cost-saving" measure has compromised safety:

$$\beta = \frac{\ln(R/S)}{\sqrt{V_R^2 + V_S^2}}$$

Where $R$ is the structural resistance and $S$ is the load effect. If $\beta$ falls below the international threshold (usually 3.0-3.5 for buildings), the conflict is resolved by mandatory retrofitting at the contractor's expense, providing a clear technical mandate for the owner.

  • Material Volatility: Fluctuations in local stone and timber prices often lead to "quality cutting."
  • Cultural Dynamics: The impact of local ceremonies on the project’s critical path.

3. Rumus Teknis: Dasar Objektif Penyelesaian Konflik

Dalam menyelesaikan sengketa mengenai "Siapa yang Benar," engineer menggunakan rumus Rekonsiliasi Biaya-Kinerja (CPR):

$$\text{Nilai Layar Bayar} = (V_{fisik} \times H_{satuan}) \times K_{mutu}$$

Dimana $K_{mutu}$ adalah Koefisien Kepatuhan Mutu. Jika kontraktor memasang besi yang lebih kecil dari gambar, maka nilai $K_{mutu}$ akan turun, yang secara otomatis mengurangi nilai pembayaran secara adil dan legal.

Selain itu, untuk membuktikan apakah penghematan biaya oleh kontraktor membahayakan bangunan, digunakan perhitungan Indeks Keandalan ($\beta$):

$$\beta = \frac{\mu_R - \mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2}}$$

Data matematis inilah yang menjadi "Hakim Teknis." Saat angka sudah berbicara, perdebatan kusir antara owner dan kontraktor akan berhenti, dan solusi perbaikan dapat segera diputuskan.
