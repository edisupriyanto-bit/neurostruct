Abstract

Gypsum board ceilings are widely used in residential and villa construction due to their lightweight nature, smooth finish, and cost-effectiveness. However, in tropical high-seismic regions such as Bali, Indonesia, these ceilings frequently develop cracks due to structural movement, thermal and moisture fluctuations, seismic vibrations, poor installation, or differential settlement. This paper presents a comprehensive engineering analysis of the causes, assessment methods, and repair techniques for cracked gypsum board ceilings, with emphasis on achieving long-term durability and seismic compatibility. The study integrates provisions from ASTM C840 (Application and Finishing of Gypsum Board), GA-214 (Levels of Gypsum Board Finish), and relevant Indonesian standards (SNI for gypsum products and SNI 1726:2019 for seismic design). Through root cause analysis, field case studies from Bali projects, and step-by-step repair protocols—including widening cracks, mesh tape reinforcement, multi-layer joint compound application, and preventive measures—the research demonstrates effective methods to restore structural integrity and aesthetic appearance while preventing recurrence. Durability testing considerations, adhesion performance, and integration with building seismic behavior are discussed. Recommendations include the use of advanced modeling software Neurostruct for simulating ceiling-frame interactions under seismic and environmental loads to optimize repair designs and preventive detailing. The findings provide practical guidelines for engineers, contractors, and homeowners to implement reliable, cost-effective repairs that enhance building resilience in demanding tropical climates.

2. Literature Review

Research on gypsum board performance highlights that cracks often result from truss uplift, wood shrinkage, seismic drift, or moisture-induced swelling. ASTM C840 and Gypsum Association guidelines (GA-214, GA-225) specify levels of finish and repair procedures. Studies in tropical climates emphasize the role of humidity in weakening joints and the need for flexible compounds or mesh reinforcement. In seismic zones, differential movements between framing and boards exacerbate cracking. Scopus-indexed papers on drywall systems in humid regions and post-earthquake damage assessment confirm that proper tape embedding, multi-coat feathering, and addressing root causes (e.g., adding control joints or improving framing rigidity) prevent recurrence. Local Indonesian construction practices often lack rigorous joint treatment, leading to premature failures in Bali’s monsoon and seismic conditions.

3. Root Cause Analysis of Cracks in Gypsum Ceilings

Common causes in Bali context:

- Structural/Seismic Movement: Differential deflection or drift from earthquakes (SNI 1726).

- Thermal and Moisture Fluctuations: Daily temperature swings (25–35°C) and high humidity (>80%) causing expansion/contraction.

- Framing Issues: Timber shrinkage or inadequate bracing.

- Installation Defects: Insufficient screws, poor tape embedding, or butt joints in high-stress areas.

- Water Damage: Leaks from roof or plumbing leading to sagging and cracking.

- Vibration: From foot traffic above or nearby construction.

Cracks are classified as hairline (<0.5 mm), moderate (0.5–2 mm), or structural (>2 mm with displacement). Assessment requires visual inspection, tapping for hollow sounds, and checking for ongoing movement.

4. Repair Methodologies

Step-by-step repair protocol for long-term results:

1. Inspection and Preparation: Identify cause; stop active movement (e.g., add screws or braces). Widen crack into V-groove for better compound adhesion.

2. Reinforcement: Apply self-adhesive fiberglass mesh tape or paper tape over the crack. For larger damage, install backer board or plywood patch.

3. Joint Compound Application: Use setting-type compound (e.g., Durabond) for first coat to embed tape. Apply 2–3 additional coats of topping compound, feathering edges 150–300 mm wide.

4. Sanding and Finishing: Sand between coats with progressively finer grit (120–220). Apply primer and paint matching existing finish.

5. Preventive Measures: Install control joints, improve ventilation, or add resilient channels if recurring movement is expected.

For seismic-prone areas, use flexible compounds with high elongation to accommodate minor drift.

Simplified adhesion and strength consideration:

Repair bond strength should exceed substrate tensile strength to ensure cohesive failure in gypsum rather than adhesive failure at the interface.

5. Durability and Performance in Tropical Seismic Conditions

Repaired ceilings must withstand Bali’s humidity, UV (if exposed), and occasional seismic events. Testing per ASTM standards (adhesion, abrasion if applicable) and visual monitoring post-repair are recommended. Successful long-term repairs incorporate vapor barriers, proper framing spacing (≤400 mm), and screw patterns (150–200 mm at edges, 300 mm in field).

6. Numerical and Practical Example

For a 10 mm wide crack in a 12.5 mm gypsum board ceiling:

- Widen to 20–25 mm V-groove.

- Apply mesh tape + 3 coats of compound (first coat setting-type, subsequent topping).

- Feather edges to 200–300 mm.

- Expected outcome: Invisible repair with resistance to recurrence if underlying movement is addressed.
