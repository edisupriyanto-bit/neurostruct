2. Literature Review

# 2.1 Common Ceiling Systems in Contemporary Construction

- Gypsum Board Ceiling: Most popular for smooth finishes; installed on light steel or wood framing.

- GRC Board Ceiling: Higher durability and moisture resistance, suitable for semi-outdoor or high-humidity areas.

- Calcium Silicate Board (Kalsiboard): Fire-resistant and dimensionally stable.

- Suspended Grid Systems: Acoustic or decorative ceilings with exposed or concealed grids.

- Direct-Fixed Ceilings: Economical for simple residential applications.

# 2.2 Material Specifications and Standards

Reference standards include ASTM C1396 (gypsum board), local SNI for building materials, and manufacturer guidelines (Knauf, USG, Jayaboard, etc.). Typical specifications:

- Gypsum board: 9 mm or 12 mm thickness, 1200 × 2400 mm or 1200 × 3000 mm sheets.

- Light steel framing: C-channel (furring) 0.4–0.6 mm BMT, main runner, cross tee, hanger wire or rod.

- Joint compound and tape for seamless finishing.

# 2.3 Factors Influencing Material Consumption in Tropical Projects

In Bali, high humidity requires moisture-resistant boards and corrosion-protected framing. Complex villa roof geometries increase cutting waste. Literature on construction cost engineering emphasizes the importance of standardized takeoff templates and waste allowances (5% for simple rooms, up to 15% for complex layouts).

3. Methodology

This study employs quantitative takeoff methodology, synthesis of manufacturer data, and development of practical calculation templates. The Neurostruct protocol structures estimation into sequential, verifiable steps.

Equation 1: Number of Gypsum Board Sheets per m² (Copy-Paste Friendly)

Number of sheets = (Ceiling Area × Waste Factor) / (Sheet Area)

Example: For 1200 mm × 2400 mm sheet (2.88 m²), with 10% waste:

Sheets required = (Area × 1.10) / 2.88

Equation 2: Light Steel Framing Quantity per m²

- Main runner (longitudinal): ≈ 0.9 – 1.1 m per m² (depending on spacing 1200 mm)

- Cross tee (transverse): ≈ 1.8 – 2.2 m per m² (spacing 600 mm)

- Hanger rod/wire: ≈ 1.0 – 1.5 m per m² (depending on height and load)

- Screws: 25–35 pcs per m² (board fixing + framing connection)

Equation 3: Joint Compound Consumption

Joint compound ≈ 0.8 – 1.2 kg/m² (for 3-coat system on gypsum board)

4. Results and Discussion

For a standard 1 m² gypsum board ceiling on light steel framing:

- Board requirement: 1.10 m² (10% waste)

- Framing: ≈ 3.0–3.5 linear meters total (runner + cross tee)

- Fasteners: 30 screws

- Finishing: 1.0 kg compound + 2.0 m tape

In Bali projects, multi-level or irregular villa ceilings can increase waste to 12–18%. Using standardized templates reduces estimation error from ±15% (manual guesswork) to ±3–5%. Tropical considerations include using moisture-resistant (MR) boards and galvanized framing (G40 or better) to prevent corrosion in high-humidity environments.

5. Recommendations for Cost-Efficient Ceiling Material Calculation

1. Always measure actual ceiling area accurately, including deductions for columns and additions for drops.

2. Apply realistic waste factors: 5–8% for simple rectangular rooms, 10–15% for complex layouts.

3. Use manufacturer-recommended spacing for framing to avoid over-design.

4. Calculate accessories (screws, compound, tape) separately rather than using lump-sum percentages.

5. Prepare a standardized Excel template for each ceiling type.

6. Verify calculations with a second person or digital takeoff software.

7. In Bali, add contingency for monsoon-related delays and humidity effects on material storage.
