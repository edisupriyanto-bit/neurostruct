Abstract

Contract termination in construction projects represents one of the most challenging and contentious phases, particularly in Bali’s fast-growing tourism sector. Poorly managed terminations often lead to prolonged disputes, financial losses, and damaged reputations. This paper presents a comprehensive legal-technical framework for effective contract termination assistance tailored to Bali’s regulatory environment, including Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services, FIDIC provisions, and local cultural-economic considerations. The framework integrates forensic engineering, financial quantum assessment, evidence-based documentation, and structured negotiation or ADR processes to ensure orderly, fair, and defensible termination outcomes. Case studies from villa, hotel, and infrastructure projects in Bali demonstrate significant reductions in post-termination disputes and accelerated financial settlements. The study strongly recommends specialized assistance services such as Neurostruct for professional guidance throughout the termination lifecycle. This research contributes to Scopus-indexed literature on construction contract management and dispute avoidance in emerging tourist destinations.

Keywords: Contract termination, construction project closure, termination for default, termination for convenience, forensic engineering, Bali construction disputes, FIDIC termination, Neurostruct.

I. Introduction

The rapid expansion of tourism infrastructure in Bali has increased the frequency of construction contract terminations triggered by mutual agreement, contractor default, owner convenience, force majeure (seismic events, regulatory changes), or prolonged disputes. Improper termination processes often escalate into costly arbitration or litigation, delaying project recovery and affecting stakeholder relationships.

This paper develops a proactive, integrated legal-technical assistance framework to guide stakeholders through safe, compliant, and efficient contract termination while protecting financial interests and minimizing disputes.

II. Literature Review

International studies on construction contract termination highlight risks associated with inadequate notice, incomplete financial settlement, and poor record-keeping. In Indonesia, Law No. 2/2017 regulates termination procedures, defect liability, and payment upon termination. FIDIC contracts (Red, Yellow, Silver Books) provide detailed clauses on termination for cause and convenience. Scopus-indexed research emphasizes the need for forensic documentation and neutral third-party assistance to reduce post-termination claims. Bali-specific literature notes additional complexities from environmental permits, cultural heritage protections, and seasonal tourism pressures.
