Abstract (English Version)

Leakage of cement paste through formwork joints remains one of the most common defects in reinforced concrete column construction, leading to honeycombing, poor surface finish, reduced durability, and costly repairs. This comprehensive paper investigates leak-proof formwork techniques for reinforced concrete columns in strict compliance with Indonesian National Standard SNI 2847:2019 (Persyaratan Beton Struktural untuk Bangunan Gedung), which modifies ACI 318M-14 provisions. Particular emphasis is placed on tropical coastal environments such as Bali, where high humidity and saline conditions accelerate surface defects if leakage occurs. Advanced techniques using semi-system and aluminum modular formwork are compared with conventional timber/plywood methods.

1. Introduction

High-quality concrete surfaces free from honeycombing and grout leakage are essential for durability, aesthetics, and long-term performance of reinforced concrete columns. SNI 2847:2019 Section 26.11 explicitly requires formwork to be tight enough to prevent loss of mortar, with provisions for chamfer strips and pre-pour inspections. Leakage not only affects appearance but can also reduce cover thickness and compromise corrosion protection in coastal Bali environments.

This paper provides a rigorous technical analysis in the English segment for international academic reference and detailed practical guidance in the Indonesian segment for site engineers and contractors.

2. Literature Review and Code Framework

SNI 2847:2019 adopts modified ACI 318M-14 provisions and emphasizes formwork tightness in Section 26.11. ACI 347 Guide to Formwork for Concrete recommends tight joints, use of sealants, and proper alignment to prevent leakage. Indonesian field studies show that conventional timber formwork frequently experiences grout loss due to panel warping and gaps, while modular aluminum and semi-system formwork significantly reduce leakage when properly assembled. In seismic zones, leak-proof formwork also ensures accurate placement of confinement reinforcement without voids.

3. Causes of Leakage and Prevention Principles

Common causes:

- Gaps between panels or at joints

- Warping of timber/plywood

- Insufficient ties or bracing allowing movement during pouring

- Poor sealing at corners and tie holes

- High lateral pressure from fast placement

Prevention strategies focus on:

- Use of high-quality, dimensionally stable materials

- Effective joint sealing methods

- Adequate tie spacing and bracing

- Pre-pour inspection protocols

4. Lateral Pressure Calculations for Leak-Proof Design

Lateral pressure must be accurately calculated to determine tie spacing and prevent form movement that opens joints.

Simplified lateral pressure formula (adapted from ACI 347 for columns with internal vibration):

p = C_w × C_c × [150 + 9000 × R / (T + 18)] (kPa)

Where:

- C_w = 1.0 (normal weight concrete)

- C_c = 1.0 (Type I cement)

- R = rate of placement (m/h)

- T = concrete temperature (°C)

Limits: p ≤ 150 × h (hydrostatic limit, h in meters).

Copy-paste ready example (Microsoft Word compatible):

For a 4.5 m high column, R = 2 m/h, T = 30°C (typical Bali tropical condition):

p ≈ 150 + 9000 × 2 / (30 + 18) = 150 + 18000 / 48 ≈ 150 + 375 = 525 kPa

p_max = 150 × 4.5 = 675 kPa → design using 525 kPa with safety factor 1.5–2.0 for ties and bracing to maintain joint tightness.

This calculation ensures formwork rigidity, minimizing joint opening during concreting.

5. Advanced Leak-Proof Formwork Techniques

# 5.1 Material and System Selection

- Conventional Timber/Plywood: Requires additional sealing (tape, sealant, or plastic liners). Prone to warping in humid Bali climate.

- Semi-System and Aluminum Modular Formwork (Recommended): Factory-precision panels with tight tolerances, integrated gaskets or overlapping joints, and quick-clamp systems that maintain pressure without gaps.

# 5.2 Joint Sealing Best Practices

- Use foam tape, rubber gaskets, or silicone sealants at panel edges.

- Seal tie holes with proprietary plugs or cones.

- Apply chamfer strips (20–25 mm) at corners for clean edges and reduced leakage risk.

- Ensure panels are clean and free of concrete residue from previous uses.

6. Erection, Concreting, and Quality Assurance Procedures

1. Assemble panels with proper alignment using laser or theodolite (verticality 1:400).

2. Install ties at calculated spacing based on pressure.

3. Apply external bracing to resist wind and concrete pressure.

4. Perform thorough inspection: check all joints, cover spacers, cleanliness, and reinforcement position.

5. Pour concrete with controlled rate and proper vibration, avoiding over-vibration that increases pressure.

6. After stripping, inspect surface for any minor defects and apply curing immediately (minimum 7 days moist curing in Bali climate).

7. Integration with Seismic Detailing and Durability

Leak-proof formwork ensures full concrete cover and eliminates voids around confinement ties required by SNI 2847:2019 Chapter 18. In Bali’s saline environment, this directly improves long-term durability against corrosion.
