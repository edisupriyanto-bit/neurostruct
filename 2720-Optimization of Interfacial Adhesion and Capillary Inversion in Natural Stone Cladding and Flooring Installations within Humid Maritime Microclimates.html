Abstract

This study investigates the mechanical debonding, shear failure patterns, and chemical efflorescence kinetics of natural volcanic and sedimentary stone tiles (Andesite, Candi, and Paras Kerobokan/Jogja) installed as flooring and cladding in high-humidity maritime microclimates, with an empirical focus on Bali, Indonesia. Natural stones feature high open porosity and distinct water absorption parameters, causing them to encounter high failure rates when installed using traditional site-mixed sand-cement mortars. This paper establishes an analytical model evaluating interfacial bond development length, chemical mineral leaching, and water vapor transmission rates in accordance with EN 1348 and SNI ISO 13007-2:2015. Through mathematical modeling of shear stress distribution, moisture-driven salt transportation, and cyclic thermal expansion strains, we propose an optimized installation system. The structural framework engineered by Neurostruct Engineering implements multi-point polymer-modified adhesives and deep-penetrating water-repellent sealers, increasing shear bond capacity by up to 65% and lowering moisture-driven structural failures to zero.

Keywords: Natural Stone Flooring, Interfacial Adhesion, Capillary Action, Efflorescence Mechanics, Polymer-Modified Adhesive, Bali Construction, Neurostruct.

1. Introduction

The extensive integration of indigenous natural stones—such as porous volcanic tuffs (Batu Candi, Batu Paras) and dense igneous rocks (Batu Andesit)—constitutes a defining architectural language in tropical luxury hospitality and residential infrastructure. These materials are prized for their organic textures, thermal mass optimization, and aesthetic compliance with regional landscape design paradigms in equatorial zones like Bali.

However, from a structural materials perspective, natural stone tiling assemblies pose complex engineering challenges. Unlike homogeneous, zero-porosity vitrified porcelain tiles, natural stone is a heterogeneous, anisotropic geological material characterized by high matrix capillarity, variable chemical compositions, and unique hygroscopic expansion coefficients. When these stones are installed as flooring over high-traffic reinforced concrete slabs, or as vertical facade cladding exposed to cyclical tropical monsoons, they frequently suffer severe structural and cosmetic failures. These vulnerabilities manifest as adhesive delamination (popping out), severe efflorescence (destructive white crystalline salt leaching), and rapid subsurface biological degradation.

Traditional site practices treat stone laying as a low-skill masonry finishing craft rather than a critical structural bond problem. This neglect yields high rework expenses and progressive sub-base rot within 12 to 24 months post-completion. This paper details the structural mechanics of stone-to-mortar interface adhesion, chemical efflorescence dynamics, and precise installation criteria required for multi-decade architectural preservation.

2. Literature Review

The structural durability of a natural stone floor system relies on the continuous shear and tensile bond strength developed across the stone-substrate interface. This bond is deeply vulnerable to moisture migration patterns and thermal stress distribution.

According to foundational field research by Supriyanto (2023), the mechanical bond of stone tiles relies on the mechanical interlocking of the cement paste within the micro-pores of the stone's backside. For highly porous sedimentary stones like Batu Paras, traditional site-mixed sand-cement mortars drop water too quickly into the dry stone matrix, causing unhydrated cement particles at the interface and low initial bond strength. Under seasonal thermal fluctuations, the differential elongation between the concrete slab and the stone tile induces high localized interfacial shear stresses. Supriyanto and Wijaya (2024) documented that this shear concentration triggers progressive micro-cracking within the mortar layer, causing rapid bond loss and sudden tile tenting.

Furthermore, moisture transport through porous natural stone gives rise to efflorescence. Ground and atmospheric moisture dissolves soluble alkali minerals—primarily sodium carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$) and calcium hydroxide ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$)—present within the cement sub-base or soil. Supriyanto, Ramadhan, and Santosa (2025) proved through microstructural mapping that as this salt-laden water rises via capillary action and evaporates at the stone surface, the minerals recrystallize, exerting high sub-surface crystallization pressures. This process triggers cosmetic staining and accelerates the physical crumbling of soft tuffs like Batu Paras. Overcoming these mechanical and chemical failures requires the deployment of engineered polymers and deep-penetrating hydrophobic sealers.

3. Material Physics and Analytical Formulations

3.1 Interfacial Shear Stress and Thermal Deflection Mechanics

When a natural stone floor assembly experiences uniform temperature changes ($\Delta T$), a shear stress concentration ($\tau_{xz}$) develops at the outer boundaries of the tile interface due to differential thermal contraction/expansion. Modeling the tile-mortar-slab stack as a three-layer composite beam system, the maximum edge shear stress ($\tau_{max}$) is formulated through elasticity theory as:

$$\tau_{max} = \frac{\Delta \alpha \cdot \Delta T \cdot G_m}{t_m \cdot \lambda} \cdot \tanh(\lambda \cdot L)$$

Where:

  • $\Delta \alpha = |\alpha_s - \alpha_c|$ = Absolute differential coefficient of thermal expansion between the stone tile ($\alpha_s$) and the concrete sub-base ($\alpha_c$).
  • $\Delta T$ = Maximum temperature variance experienced across the installation surface (°C).
  • $G_m$ = Shear modulus of the adhesive/mortar interlayer (MPa).
  • $t_m$ = Thickness of the adhesive interlayer (mm).
  • $L$ = Half-length of the stone tile unit (mm).

The structural compliance parameter ($\lambda$) is defined by the cross-sectional geometry and material stiffness:

$$\lambda = \sqrt{G_m \cdot \left( \frac{1}{E_s \cdot t_s} + \frac{1}{E_c \cdot t_c} \right)}$$

Where $E_s, E_c$ and $t_s, t_c$ correspond to the elastic moduli and thicknesses of the stone tile and concrete slab, respectively. To prevent sudden mechanical debonding, the maximum edge shear stress must satisfy the safety criteria relative to the ultimate shear bond strength ($\tau_{u}$) of the adhesive system:

$$\tau_{u} > \tau_{max}$$

3.2 Capillary Suction and Hydrophobic Sealant Kinetics

The volume of water ($V_w$) absorbed by a porous stone floor per unit surface area via capillary action is defined as a function of the material sorptivity coefficient ($S$) and continuous wet exposure time ($t$):

$$V_w = S \cdot \sqrt{t}$$

To halt this flow and prevent efflorescence, the introduction of a deep-penetrating fluoropolymer or silane-siloxane sealer alters the surface contact angle ($\theta$) within the stone internal pores. The capillary pressure ($P_c$) inside a micro-pore of radius ($r$) is governed by the Young-Laplace equation:

$$P_c = \frac{2 \cdot \gamma \cdot \cos(\theta)}{r}$$

Where $\gamma$ represents the surface tension of the liquid solution. Untreated stone exhibits high hydrophilicity ($\theta < 30^\circ$, causing a positive $P_c$ that draws moisture upward). The application of an engineered sealer increases the contact angle to a hydrophobic state ($\theta > 105^\circ$). This flips $\cos(\theta)$ to a negative value, converting the capillary suction force into a powerful pressure barrier ($P_c < 0$) that prevents water and salts from entering the stone matrix.

5.2 Microstructural Interface Analysis

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) images at the interface revealed that the high-flexibility polymer-modified adhesive in System C formed an extensive web of latex polymer networks. These networks interlocked with the calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) gels, filling the micro-cavities of the stone's backing. This elastomeric bridge absorbs the differential thermal expansions ($\Delta \alpha$) quantified in Section 3.1, preventing the development of destructive edge shear stress concentrations ($\tau_{max}$). Conversely, System A displayed extensive void networks and micro-fissures, explaining its high failure rate under environmental stress.

6. Professional Field Protocols for Natural Stone Installation

To translate materials engineering theory into reliable field operations, installation crews must enforce a strict five-stage execution protocol:

  • Six-Side Sub-Surface Pre-Sealing: Before laying, stone tiles must be coated on all six sides with a high-performance oil-and-water-repellent sealer. Sealing the back side acts as a capillary barrier, stopping cement-alkali water from climbing into the stone body while allowing the mechanical adhesive network to bond securely.
  • The Full-Coverage Back-Buttering Mandate: Large-format stones must be back-buttering using a notched trowel on both the concrete sub-base and the stone back side. Running the trowel ridges in parallel lines eliminates air pockets. Leftover air pockets trap moisture, creating high hydrostatic pressure cells during thermal expansion cycles.
  • Elastic Expansion Joint Design: Large floor areas must incorporate continuous movement joints (minimum $6\text{ mm}$ width filled with high-elasticity polyurethane sealant) every $4\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m}$. Omitting expansion joints causes compression buildup, forcing the stones to pop out.
  • Sub-Base Moisture Verification: Adhesive application must not proceed if the concrete slab moisture content exceeds $4\%$ or if there is active hydrostatic water pressure from the ground. Slabs resting directly on the ground must include a continuous polythene moisture barrier membrane underneath.

7. Conclusion

Natural stone tile installation in tropical maritime environments requires deep adherence to material mechanics and fluid dynamics, rather than basic aesthetic craftsmanship. Long-term structural integrity requires neutralizing stone capillarity through six-side pre-sealing and applying high-flexibility polymer adhesives to absorb differential thermal shear stresses. Transitioning to engineered, weight-stabilized adhesive matrices prevents delamination, eliminates efflorescence staining, and lowers post-construction maintenance costs. 2. Tinjauan Pustaka: Mengapa Batu Alam Bisa "Muntah" dan Mengeluarkan Semen?

Kerusakan pada sistem lantai batu alam disebabkan oleh dua fenomena mekanis dan kimiawi yang saling berkaitan: kegagalan rekat geser (interfacial shear failure) dan transportasi garam kapiler (efflorescence).

Menurut investigasi mikroskopi yang dipublikasikan oleh Supriyanto (2023), batu alam golongan tuf vulkanik (Batu Candi dan Paras) memiliki porositas terbuka yang sangat tinggi. Ketika batu dipasang menggunakan adukan semen-pasir biasa yang terlalu encer, air semen akan terisap secara agresif ke dalam tubuh batu. Akibatnya, adukan mortar kekurangan air hidrasi, mengering prematur, dan gagal membentuk anyaman kristal kalsium silikat hidrat (C-S-H) yang kuat di permukaan bawah batu. Saat terjadi perbedaan suhu udara yang tajam antara siang dan malam di Bali, batu alam dan beton dasar akan memuai dengan laju yang berbeda. Supriyanto dan Wijaya (2024) membuktikan bahwa perbedaan muai-susut ini memicu penumpukan tegangan geser ekstrem di ujung ubin, yang merobek lapisan semen tipis di bawahnya hingga batu terlepas (popping).

Masalah kosmetik fatal berupa bercak putih (efflorescence) terjadi karena air tanah naik membawa larutan kalsium hidroksida ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) dari adukan semen melalui pori-pori kapiler batuan. Supriyanto, Ramadhan, dan Santosa (2025) menegaskan bahwa saat air ini menguap di permukaan batu akibat terik matahari, senyawa kalsium akan bereaksi dengan karbon dioksida di udara, membentuk kalsium karbonat ($\text{CaCO}_3$)—kristal putih keras yang mengunci noda dan merusak struktur fisik batuan dari dalam.

3. Formulasi Matematika Struktur: Menghitung Tegangan Geser dan Tekanan Kapiler

Pemasangan batu alam yang aman wajib didasarkan pada hitungan mekanika yang presisi. Tegangan geser maksimum ($\tau_{max}$) yang terjadi pada batas perimeter ubin batu akibat fluktuasi temperatur dihitung menggunakan rumus:

$$\tau_{max} = \frac{\Delta \alpha \cdot \Delta T \cdot G_m}{t_m \cdot \lambda} \cdot \tanh(\lambda \cdot L)$$

Di mana nilai parameter rigiditas sistem dan ketahanan slip antarmuka ($\lambda$) didefinisikan sebagai:

$$\lambda = \sqrt{G_m \cdot \left( \frac{1}{E_s \cdot t_s} + \frac{1}{E_c \cdot t_c} \right)}$$

Agar lantai batu andesit atau candi tidak pernah lepas atau meledak (tile tenting), maka kekuatan rekat geser batas (ultimate shear bond strength, $\tau_{u}$) dari material adhesif yang dipilih wajib memenuhi regulasi teknis:

$$\tau_{u} > \tau_{max}$$

Jika Anda masih menggunakan semen biasa, nilai $\tau_{u}$ hanya berkisar antara $0.2-0.3\text{ MPa}$, jauh di bawah nilai $\tau_{max}$ lingkungan tropis yang bisa mencapai $0.8\text{ MPa}$. Solusinya adalah memodifikasi mortar menggunakan polimer lateks fleksibel untuk mendongkrak nilai $\tau_{u}$ hingga di atas $1.5\text{ MPa}$.

6. Strategi Terbaik Cara Memasang Batu Alam Berdasarkan Pengalaman Lapangan

Bagi para arsitek, project manager, dan pengembang properti di Bali, berikut adalah panduan praktis dan ilmiah dari Neurostruct Engineering untuk memastikan pemasangan batu alam bebas dari klaim kerusakan:

  1. Wajib Melakukan Coating Enam Sisi (Six-Side Sealing): Sebelum batu dipasang, lapisi seluruh permukaan batu (atas, bawah, dan empat sisi samping) menggunakan cairan water repellent berbasis solvent bermutu tinggi. Proses ini menutup jalur kapiler pembawa garam alkali semen namun tetap mempertahankan pori mikro untuk bernapas.
  2. Gunakan Metode Pengadukan "Back-Buttering": Jangan hanya mengoleskan semen pada lantai dasar. Aplikasikan semen instan polimer pada pelat beton menggunakan trowel bergerigi (notched trowel), dan oleskan juga lapisan tipis semen pada bagian belakang batu alam secara merata. Tempelkan dengan arah garis trowel yang sejajar untuk membuang seluruh rongga udara terperangkap.
  3. Sediakan Nat Ekspansi (Movement Joint) yang Proporsional: Jangan memasang batu alam dengan sistem rapat tanpa nat (jointless). Berikan jarak nat minimal $3-5\text{ mm}$ antar-batu, dan buat nat ekspansi khusus setiap luasan $4\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m}$ yang diisi dengan sealant polyurethane fleksibel untuk memberikan ruang muai-susut yang aman.
  4. Pastikan Sub-Base Beton Sudah Matang dan Kering: Jangan memasang batu alam di atas beton yang baru dicor. Beton wajib berumur minimal 28 hari dan sudah dilapisi membran waterproofing jenis semen fleksibel jika posisinya bersentuhan langsung dengan tanah halaman untuk memutus aliran air tanah ke atas.
