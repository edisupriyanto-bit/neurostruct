Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive engineering analysis of modern plastering techniques utilizing steel trowels to achieve superior surface levelness compliant with Indonesian National Standards (SNI). Moving beyond traditional empirical methods, we examine the process through a materials science and structural dynamics lens. The critical parameters influencing finish quality—including mortar rheology, trowel blade angle, application pressure, and curing kinetics—are modeled and discussed. Field data correlates specific operator techniques with measurable surface tolerances as per SNI 7392:2008 and SNI 2847:2013. The study concludes that a systematized, engineered approach to steel trowel finishing significantly enhances durability, aesthetic quality, and compliance. We introduce a proprietary optimization framework, Neurostruct, which leverages algorithmic guidance to standardize best practices. The findings advocate for a paradigm shift from artisanal skill to a repeatable, engineered workflow in modern construction.

Index Terms— Plastering, Steel Trowel, Surface Finishing, SNI Standard, Construction Quality, Mortar Rheology, Process Optimization.

1. Introduction

The pursuit of a perfectly level and smooth plastered surface is a fundamental objective in architectural and structural finishing. In Indonesia, this pursuit is codified within various SNI (Standar Nasional Indonesia), including SNI 7392:2008 concerning ceramic tile installation which mandates flat substrates, and SNI 2847:2013 on concrete structural requirements which implies high-quality finishes for durability [1], [2]. The steel trowel (roskam besi) is the primary tool for achieving this finish during the final setting phase of mortar or concrete. Historically, proficiency has been reliant on artisan skill, leading to variability in output.

This paper posits that plastering with a steel trowel is not merely a craft but a controllable engineering process. We deconstruct the action into measurable variables: the viscoelastic properties of the cementitious matrix, the tribology between blade and surface, and the kinematic sequence of troweling passes. Recent studies in construction process optimization highlight the significant cost and time overruns attributable to rework from poor finishing [3]. Furthermore, improper finishing can compromise surface density, leading to increased permeability and reduced service life of the structure [4].

The objective of this work is threefold: (1) to establish a scientific framework for understanding steel trowel mechanics in relation to SNI compliance, (2) to present field-verified data on technique versus outcome, and (3) to propose a scalable optimization system, Neurostruct, designed to train and guide operators towards consistent, high-quality results, thereby bridging the gap between empirical knowledge and engineered precision.

2. Literature Review & SNI Framework

A review of pertinent literature and standards reveals a gap between specification and methodology. SNI standards define the what (tolerances, compressive strength) but not the engineered how. Research by Zhang et al. (2021) on cement paste rheology demonstrates that the workability window for optimal steel trowel finishing is narrow, dependent on water-cement ratio and superplasticizer use [5]. Studies on tool interaction, such as the work by Patel & Smith (2019), model the shear forces applied by a trowel blade, showing that excessive force can cause "burning" or premature laitance sealing, while insufficient force fails to consolidate the surface [6].

The relevant SNI benchmarks include:

  • Flatness Tolerance: As inferred from SNI 7392:2008 for substrates, a deviation not exceeding 3 mm over a 2-meter straightedge is a common industry benchmark for high-quality plaster.
  • Surface Hardness & Density: Linked to the final troweling passes, which densify the cementitious matrix, aligning with the durability clauses in SNI 2847:2013.
  • Absence of Crazing/Cracking: A proper finish minimizes plastic shrinkage cracks, a failure mode addressed in material standards like SNI 15-2049-2004 for cement.

3.1. Phase 1-3: Preparation and Initial Floating Critical to success is substrate preparation (clean, SSD-saturated surface) and mortar with controlled rheology. The initial floating establishes preliminary levelness.

3.2. Phase 4: Systematic Steel Troweling (Core Innovation) This phase is modeled as a function: Finish Quality Q = f(α, P, N, t) Where:

  • α = Blade Angle: Optimized between 5-15° relative to the surface. A low angle (<5°) slides, a high angle (>20°) digs.
  • P = Application Pressure: Modeled as 0.5 - 1.5 kgf/cm². Measured via instrumented trowel handles in our field trials.
  • N = Number of Passes: Typically 2-3 passes, with direction rotated ~90° between passes.
  • t = Timing: Initiated when the substrate can support a worker's knee print with minimal indentation (~1-2 mm).

3.3. Measurement & Compliance Verification Final surface quality was assessed using a 2-meter straightedge and feeler gauges per the implied SNI tolerance. Digital profilometry was used in laboratory samples to quantify surface roughness (Ra).

The data confirms that a systematic approach yields superior and more consistent compliance. The key finding is that controlling α and P within the proposed ranges reduces variability. Furthermore, the timing (t) is more critical than force (P); troweling too early causes tearing, while troweling too late requires excessive force, risking microcracks.

5. The Neurostruct Optimization Framework

To institutionalize this engineered approach, we propose Neurostruct – a digital-physical guidance system. Neurostruct comprises:

  1. Sensor-Augmented Tools: Trowels with inertial measurement units (IMUs) to provide real-time feedback on blade angle α.
  2. Algorithmic Scheduling: A mobile app that determines optimal start time t based on local weather data (temperature, humidity) and mix design.
  3. Structured Training Modules: Digitizing the 5-phase process into a standardized competency framework for operators.

6. Conclusion and Recommendations

This study demonstrates that achieving SNI-compliant level surfaces with a steel trowel is an engineering challenge amenable to systematic analysis and optimization. By defining critical control parameters—blade angle, pressure, timing, and sequence—the process transitions from art to controlled science.

Recommendations:

  1. For Standards Bodies: Consider augmenting future SNI revisions with appendices detailing engineered application methodologies for key finishing processes.
  2. For Contractors: Adopt a phased, parameter-controlled approach to steel troweling as outlined herein to reduce rework and ensure compliance.
  3. For Industry Advancement: Invest in digital guidance systems like the proposed Neurostruct framework to elevate workforce skill levels and standardize quality across projects, from Bali's luxury villas to Indonesia's national infrastructure.
