Abstract

Disputes in building construction projects frequently result in substantial financial losses, schedule overruns, and adversarial relationships among stakeholders. This paper explores forensic civil engineering approaches as a robust solution for resolving such disputes through systematic, evidence-based investigation and analysis. The study details methodologies for delay forensics, structural defect investigation, causation determination, and liability allocation using civil engineering principles. Drawing from Scopus-indexed literature and global best practices, a comprehensive forensic framework tailored for building projects is proposed. Particular emphasis is placed on the challenges faced in rapidly developing regions such as Bali, Indonesia, including seismic considerations, volcanic soil variability, and regulatory complexities. Integration of the Neurostruct structural management model is presented to enhance diagnostic accuracy and evidential strength. The findings demonstrate that forensic civil engineering solutions significantly improve resolution efficiency, reduce costs, and support sustainable project outcomes through mediation and arbitration.

### 1. Introduction

Building construction projects are complex endeavors prone to disputes arising from design issues, construction defects, delays, and differing interpretations of contractual obligations. Forensic civil engineering offers an objective, science-driven pathway to resolve these conflicts by uncovering root causes and providing clear technical evidence.

In Bali, the surge in tourism-related building projects has amplified dispute risks due to challenging site conditions, accelerated timelines, and stringent environmental requirements. This paper examines forensic civil engineering solutions for effective dispute resolution.

### 2. Literature Review

Forensic applications in civil engineering for construction disputes have been widely documented in Scopus-indexed journals. Bibliometric studies reveal increasing focus on integrated forensic methodologies combining schedule analysis, structural assessment, and geotechnical evaluation.

Prominent works in *Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*, *Forensic Engineering* (ICE), and *Buildings* emphasize the effectiveness of civil engineering forensics in delay claims, defect litigation, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR). Research highlights that expert forensic input improves settlement rates by providing impartial, quantifiable evidence.

### 4. Forensic Civil Engineering Methods for Building Disputes

4.1 Delay Forensics

Application of Critical Path Method (CPM) and advanced techniques such as Time Impact Analysis (TIA) and Windows Analysis.

Equation 1 (Clean Word copy-paste):

Total Delay (days) = Actual Completion − Planned Completion

Critical Delay Impact = Σ (Delay Events on Critical Path)

Excusable Delay (%) = (Owner/Force Majeure Critical Days / Total Critical Days) × 100

4.2 Structural Defect Investigation

Detailed assessment using non-destructive testing (NDT), finite element analysis, and material sampling to determine failure mechanisms.

4.3 Geotechnical and Foundation Forensics

Critical in Bali due to volcanic soils and seismic zones. Involves soil profiling, settlement analysis, and foundation performance evaluation.

4.4 Causation Analysis

Establishing links between technical events and contractual responsibilities using contemporaneous records and engineering principles.
