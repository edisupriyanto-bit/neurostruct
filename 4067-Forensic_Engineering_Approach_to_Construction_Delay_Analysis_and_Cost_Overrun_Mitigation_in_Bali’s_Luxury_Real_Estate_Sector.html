Abstract

Construction projects in Bali, particularly in the luxury hospitality and villa sectors, are frequently plagued by significant delays and budget overruns. This paper examines the application of Forensic Delay Analysis (FDA) and cost quantification methods within the unique regulatory and environmental landscape of Bali. By utilizing the Critical Path Method (CPM) and Time Impact Analysis (TIA), this study provides a framework for identifying excusable vs. non-excusable delays. The results demonstrate that rigorous data-driven engineering analysis is essential for successful claim substantiation in international arbitration and local litigation.

The construction industry in Bali is characterized by high complexity, driven by rapid tourism infrastructure growth and challenging site conditions (e.g., volcanic soil, high humidity, and logistical constraints). 2. Theoretical Framework: Delay Analysis Methodologies

In forensic engineering, the selection of a delay analysis methodology is crucial. This paper focuses on:

  • Impacted As-Planned (IAP): Inserting delay events into the original schedule.
  • As-Built vs. As-Planned (ABvAP): Comparing actual progress against the baseline.
  • Collapsed As-Built (CAB): Removing delay events from the actual schedule to see what "would have happened."
  • Time Impact Analysis (TIA): The most favored method for contemporary claims, focusing on incremental impacts.

3. Mathematical Models for Cost Quantification

To quantify cost overruns (Prolongation Costs), we utilize the Hudson or Emden formulas for overhead calculation.

The Emden Formula:

$$\text{Overhead Claim} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Overhead}}{\text{Total Turnover}} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Contract Value}}{\text{Contract Period}} \right) \times \text{Delay Days}$$

For labor productivity loss, the Measured Mile analysis is applied:

$$\text{Efficiency Loss} = \frac{\text{Productivity in Unimpacted Period} - \text{Productivity in Impacted Period}}{\text{Productivity in Unimpacted Period}}$$

2. Metode Analisis Keterlambatan (Delay Analysis)

Untuk memenangkan klaim konstruksi, seorang engineer harus menguasai metode berikut:

  • Metode Jalur Kritis (Critical Path Method - CPM): Mengidentifikasi aktivitas yang jika terlambat satu hari saja, akan memundurkan seluruh tanggal penyelesaian proyek.
  • Analisis Dampak Waktu (Time Impact Analysis): Metode terbaik untuk menghitung perpanjangan waktu (EOT - Extension of Time) secara kronologis saat peristiwa terjadi.

3. Rumus Perhitungan Kerugian Biaya (Prolongation Costs)

Klaim biaya tidak boleh hanya sekadar perkiraan. Salah satu rumus yang diakui secara internasional adalah Rumus Hudson:

$$\text{Klaim Overhead} = \frac{\text{Persentase Overhead}}{100} \times \frac{\text{Nilai Kontrak}}{\text{Durasi Kontrak (Minggu)}} \times \text{Jumlah Minggu Terlambat}$$

Rumus ini memastikan kontraktor mendapatkan kompensasi atas biaya operasional kantor pusat dan alat yang tetap standby di lapangan akibat kesalahan pemilik proyek (misalnya keterlambatan pembayaran atau perubahan desain).

4. Masalah Khas Konstruksi di Bali

  • Akses Logistik: Keterlambatan pengiriman material dari Jawa melalui pelabuhan.
  • Faktor Upacara Adat: Manajemen sumber daya manusia yang harus sinkron dengan kalender lokal Bali agar tidak terjadi downtime yang tidak terencana.
  • Perubahan Regulasi: Transisi dari IMB ke PBG yang seringkali memakan waktu lebih lama dari perkiraan jadwal awal.
