PART I: ENGLISH VERSION (International Journal Standard)

Abstract

Construction disputes are inherently multi-dimensional, often involving complex technical failures that legal professionals alone cannot adequately resolve. In the burgeoning construction landscape of Bali, disputes frequently stem from structural non-compliance, material substitution, and intricate delay claims. This paper explores the critical role of the Professional Engineer as a mediator and technical adjudicator. By shifting the focus from purely legalistic interpretations to forensic engineering evidence, mediation becomes more efficient, equitable, and technically sound. The study proposes a framework where engineering data—derived from structural analysis and quantitative schedule auditing—acts as the primary catalyst for settlement, proving that technical clarity is the ultimate antidote to legal deadlock.

1. Introduction

Construction projects in Bali, ranging from luxury coastal villas to large-scale hospitality infrastructure, are subject to high seismic risks and aggressive environmental conditions. When disputes arise, the standard legal response often fails to address the "root cause" of the conflict, which is typically an engineering failure or a technical misunderstanding. This paper argues that the inclusion of an experienced engineer in the mediation process is essential to translate complex structural and contractual data into actionable settlement terms.

2. Methodology: The Engineering-Centric Mediation Framework

The proposed framework for technical mediation involves:

  • Technical Fact-Finding: Utilizing Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) to establish the current physical state of the project.
  • Root Cause Analysis (RCA): Differentiating between design flaws, contractor negligence, and Force Majeure.
  • Quantum Calculation: Providing precise financial valuations of defects and delays based on engineering principles rather than estimates.

3. Mathematical Framework for Technical Adjudication

To resolve disputes regarding structural integrity and safety, the mediator-engineer utilizes the Reliability Index ($\beta$). This provides a mathematical basis for whether a structure is "Acceptable" or "Defective."

$$\beta = \frac{\mu_R - \mu_S}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2}}$$

Where:

  • $\mu_R, \sigma_R$ = Mean and standard deviation of structural resistance (Capacity).
  • $\mu_S, \sigma_S$ = Mean and standard deviation of load effects (Demand).

In delay disputes, the Window Analysis method is employed to calculate the compensable delay ($D_{comp}$):

$$D_{comp} = T_{actual} - T_{baseline} - D_{exc}$$

Where $D_{exc}$ represents excusable delays identified through the forensic logbook audit. These formulas provide a "Hard Data" foundation for mediation, making it difficult for either party to present baseless claims.

4. Beyond the Law: The Engineer as a Bridge

In Bali, many disputes involve international investors and local contractors. The engineer acts as a cultural and technical bridge. While lawyers focus on the "breach," the engineer focuses on the "fix." By providing a technical "Remediation Roadmap," the engineer allows mediation to move from finger-pointing to problem-solving, which is vital for the continuity of Bali's property development.

3. Rumus Teknis: Dasar Objektif dalam Mediasi

Untuk menyelesaikan sengketa mengenai kelayakan struktur di daerah gempa seperti Bali, mediator-engineer menggunakan perhitungan Rasio Kapasitas ($R_c$):

$$R_c = \frac{\text{Kapasitas Aktual Terpasang}}{\text{Beban Rencana SNI}}$$

Jika $R_c < 1.0$, maka secara teknis bangunan tersebut cacat mutu dan kontraktor wajib bertanggung jawab. Selain itu, untuk menyelesaikan selisih hitung volume (RAB) yang sering menjadi pemicu sengketa, digunakan rumus deviasi:

$$\text{Selisih Biaya} = (V_{aktual} - V_{kontrak}) \times \text{Harga Satuan}$$

Data matematis inilah yang dibawa ke meja mediasi. Saat angka sudah berbicara, ego masing-masing pihak biasanya akan turun, dan kesepakatan damai jauh lebih mudah tercapai.
