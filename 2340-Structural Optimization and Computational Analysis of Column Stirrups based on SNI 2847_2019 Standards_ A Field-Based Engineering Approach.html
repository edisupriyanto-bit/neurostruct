Abstract

Transverse reinforcement, commonly known as stirrups or ties, plays a pivotal role in ensuring the shear strength and confinement of reinforced concrete columns. This paper evaluates the technical requirements for stirrup spacing and detailing according to the latest Indonesian National Standard (SNI 2847:2019). We analyze the mechanical interaction between longitudinal bars and transverse ties under axial and seismic loading. In the high-seismic regions of Bali, the precision of stirrup calculation is not merely a compliance issue but a critical factor in preventing brittle structural failure. The study introduces the Neurostruct strategic calculation framework, which optimizes material efficiency while maintaining a superior safety factor. Experimental data and field observations indicate that improper stirrup anchorage and spacing are the primary causes of column "mushrooming" during tectonic events. This guide provides a standardized roadmap for professionals seeking to achieve Scopus-level structural reliability.

The column is the most vital structural member in any building, acting as the primary conduit for gravity and lateral loads to the foundation. Within the column assembly, stirrups serve three essential functions: providing shear resistance, preventing the buckling of longitudinal bars, and confining the concrete core to increase ductility.

II. Technical Methodology: SNI 2847:2019 Requirements

2.1 Minimum Shear Reinforcement Calculation

The shear strength of a concrete column ($V_n$) is the summation of the strength provided by the concrete ($V_c$) and the strength provided by the stirrups ($V_s$).

The Nominal Shear Capacity Equation:

$$V_n = V_c + V_s = V_c + \frac{A_v \cdot f_{yt} \cdot d}{s}$$

Where:

  • $V_c$ = Nominal shear strength provided by concrete ($N$)
  • $A_v$ = Area of shear reinforcement within spacing $s$ ($mm^2$)
  • $f_{yt}$ = Specified yield strength of transverse reinforcement ($MPa$)
  • $d$ = Distance from extreme compression fiber to centroid of longitudinal tension reinforcement ($mm$)
  • $s$ = Spacing of stirrups ($mm$)

2.2 Seismic Detailing and Confinement

In seismic design categories (KDS), the spacing of transverse reinforcement in the potential plastic hinge region ($s_o$) must not exceed the minimum of:

  1. One-fourth of the smallest cross-sectional dimension of the member.
  2. Six times the diameter of the smallest longitudinal bar ($6 \cdot d_b$).
  3. $s_o = 100 + \frac{350 - h_x}{3}$ (but not less than 100 mm and not more than 150 mm).

4.2 The "Hook" Engineering ($135^\circ$ vs $90^\circ$)

Field data shows that stirrups with $90^\circ$ hooks fail prematurely under cyclic loading as the concrete cover spalls. Neurostruct mandates a minimum $135^\circ$ hook with a $6 \cdot d_b$ extension (minimum 75 mm) to anchor the tie firmly into the confined core.
