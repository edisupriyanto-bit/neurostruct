1. Introduction

Bored piles are preferred deep foundation elements in areas with poor surface soils or high loading requirements. However, construction defects such as necking, voids, soil inclusions, or segregation during tremie concreting can compromise axial and lateral capacity, leading to costly repairs or structural failure. Crosshole Sonic Logging (CSL) is the most accurate and reliable nondestructive method for assessing concrete integrity within the reinforcement cage of bored piles.

CSL involves transmitting ultrasonic pulses between water-filled access tubes (typically steel or PVC, minimum 38–50 mm ID) attached to the rebar cage before concreting. First Arrival Time (FAT) and signal energy are recorded to identify anomalies where velocity drops or energy attenuates significantly. In Bali’s geology—characterized by loose volcanic deposits, soft clays, high groundwater, and occasional karst features—CSL provides critical quality control for large-diameter piles supporting villas, hotels, and multi-story buildings.

2. Literature Review

CSL was standardized in ASTM D6760 and is widely adopted globally. Research in *Journal of Geotechnical and Geoenvironmental Engineering* (e.g., Zhang et al., 2005) evaluates reliability, showing high detection probability for defects inside the cage but limited sensitivity outside it. Studies confirm that velocity reductions >10–20% or significant energy loss indicate anomalies.

DFI (Deep Foundations Institute) guidelines classify results as Class A (acceptable), Class B (conditionally acceptable), and Class C (highly anomalous). Tomography enhances 2D/3D imaging of defect location and extent. In tropical regions, papers address challenges like high humidity affecting tube bonding (steel preferred over PVC) and variable concrete quality due to long tremie pours.

Indonesian practice increasingly specifies CSL for critical piles per SNI requirements for quality assurance in deep foundations. Field case studies from Southeast Asia highlight successful defect detection and remedial grouting or replacement.

3. Theoretical Background and Testing Principles

# 3.1 Wave Propagation in Concrete

CSL measures compressional (P-wave) velocity through concrete. Typical good-quality concrete velocity ranges from 3,500–4,500 m/s. Defects cause delays in First Arrival Time (FAT) or attenuation of signal energy.

The ultrasonic pulse velocity \( V_p \) is calculated as:

\[ V_p = \frac{D}{t} \]

where \( D \) is the distance between transmitter and receiver tubes (m), and \( t \) is the First Arrival Time (s).

Relative energy is obtained by integrating the received signal amplitude over time.

# 3.2 Anomaly Interpretation Criteria

Common criteria (per ASTM D6760 and DFI guidelines):

- Velocity reduction <10% from average: Normal concrete.

- 10–20% reduction with moderate energy loss: Minor anomaly (Class B).

- >20% reduction or significant energy drop: Major defect (Class C).

Tomographic inversion provides velocity tomograms for precise defect mapping.

# 3.3 Limitations and Complementary Methods

CSL excels inside the rebar cage but has limited detection outside. It is often combined with Thermal Integrity Profiling (TIP) or core drilling for comprehensive evaluation.

4. Field Implementation and Best Practices

# 4.1 Access Tube Installation

Install minimum 3–4 steel tubes (preferred for better bonding) equally spaced around the rebar cage perimeter. Tubes must extend full pile length plus 0.3–0.5 m above concrete level and be water-filled before testing.

# 4.2 Testing Procedure

- Use calibrated equipment (e.g., CHAMP or equivalent) meeting ASTM D6760.

- Lower transmitter and receiver probes simultaneously in paired tubes.

- Record data at 50 mm depth intervals while pulling probes upward at constant speed.

- Test all tube combinations (e.g., 6 profiles for 4 tubes).

# 4.3 Data Processing and Reporting

Generate waterfall plots (variable density logs), FAT vs. depth, relative energy plots, and tomographic images. Classify anomalies and recommend actions (further investigation, grouting, or replacement).

# 4.4 Quality Control in Tropical Conditions

Perform CSL soon after concrete reaches sufficient strength (typically 7–14 days). Protect tubes from damage during concreting. In high-rainfall Bali, schedule testing to avoid delays from weather.

3. Latar Belakang Teori dan Prinsip Pengujian

# 3.1 Propagasi Gelombang di Beton

CSL mengukur kecepatan gelombang tekan (P-wave). Kecepatan beton berkualitas baik biasanya 3.500–4.500 m/s.

Kecepatan pulsa ultrasonik:

\[ V_p = \frac{D}{t} \]

# 3.2 Kriteria Interpretasi Anomali

- Pengurangan kecepatan <10%: Normal.

- 10–20% dengan kehilangan energi sedang: Anomali minor (Class B).

- >20% atau penurunan energi signifikan: Cacat mayor (Class C).

# 3.3 Keterbatasan dan Metode Pelengkap

CSL unggul di dalam kandang tulangan tetapi sensitivitas terbatas di luar. Sering dikombinasikan dengan Thermal Integrity Profiling atau pengeboran inti.

4. Implementasi Lapangan dan Praktik Terbaik

# 4.1 Pemasangan Tabung Akses

Pasang minimal 3–4 tabung baja secara merata di sekitar kandang tulangan.

# 4.2 Prosedur Pengujian

Gunakan peralatan terkalibrasi, rekam data setiap 50 mm sambil menarik probe ke atas.

# 4.3 Pengolahan Data dan Pelaporan

Buat plot waterfall, grafik FAT vs. kedalaman, dan gambar tomografi. Klasifikasikan anomali dan rekomendasikan tindakan.

# 4.4 Pengendalian Kualitas di Kondisi Tropis

Lakukan CSL setelah beton mencapai kekuatan yang cukup (7–14 hari). Lindungi tabung dari kerusakan selama pengecoran.
