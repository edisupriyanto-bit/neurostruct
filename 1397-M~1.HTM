2. Literature Review

Extensive research in *Construction and Building Materials* and *Wood Science and Technology* confirms that moisture content variation is the primary cause of dimensional changes in wood. Wood can experience tangential swelling/shrinkage of up to 8–12% when moisture content fluctuates between 6% and 28%. In tropical climates, relative humidity above 80% promotes fungal growth and termite activity.

Studies emphasize the importance of protective coatings, proper ventilation, and regular maintenance to extend service life. The use of preservatives, breathable coatings, and strategic sealing details has been shown to significantly reduce degradation rates.

3. Degradation Mechanisms in Humid Tropical Climates

Major degradation mechanisms include:

- Moisture-Induced Warping and Cracking: Due to uneven moisture absorption.

- Fungal Decay: Especially in lower parts of doors exposed to splash water.

- Termite and Insect Attack: Common in untreated or poorly maintained timber.

- UV Degradation: Causing discoloration and surface checking on sun-exposed doors.

- Joint Loosening: Due to cyclic swelling and shrinkage.

4. Protection and Maintenance Strategies

# 4.1 Material Selection and Initial Protection

- Use well-seasoned, pressure-treated hardwood (teak, meranti, or bangkirai).

- Apply wood preservatives containing borates or copper-based compounds.

- Use high-quality exterior-grade coatings (marine varnish, polyurethane, or oil-based systems with UV inhibitors).

# 4.2 Regular Maintenance Protocol

- Cleaning every 3 months.

- Re-coating every 12–24 months depending on exposure.

- Inspection of hinges, seals, and bottom rails annually.
