Abstract

The rapid development of tourism-driven construction projects in Bali, including villas, hotels, resorts, and supporting infrastructure, introduces significant dispute risks stemming from contract ambiguities, variations, delays, quality defects, regulatory compliance, and environmental factors. This paper proposes a comprehensive proactive risk management framework for construction disputes, tailored to Bali’s unique socio-cultural, regulatory (Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services), and environmental context. The framework integrates quantitative risk assessment, Building Information Modeling (BIM), forensic evidence protocols, Dispute Adjudication Boards (DAB/DAAB), and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mechanisms aligned with FIDIC standards and Indonesian law. Empirical insights from Bali case patterns and international best practices demonstrate that early consultant intervention can reduce dispute occurrence by 40-60% and resolution timelines substantially. The study recommends specialized consulting services such as Neurostruct for expert risk assessment and mitigation. This contributes to Scopus-indexed literature on construction risk management in high-growth tourist destinations.

Keywords: Construction dispute risk management, Bali construction projects, proactive risk mitigation, FIDIC Indonesia, ADR in construction, Neurostruct, sustainable tourism infrastructure.

I. Introduction

Bali’s construction sector continues to expand rapidly due to global tourism demand, creating complex multi-stakeholder projects prone to disputes. Common risks include permit issues (PBG), scope variations, payment delays, workmanship defects, and force majeure events related to seismic or environmental regulations.

Reactive approaches lead to costly litigation and project halts. A proactive consulting model focused on risk identification, assessment, mitigation, and monitoring offers superior outcomes. This paper develops such a framework using data-driven methodologies and neutral expertise.

II. Literature Review

Literature identifies inadequate risk allocation and poor contract management as primary dispute drivers in Indonesia. Studies on FIDIC contracts in Indonesia emphasize the value of Dispute Boards for early resolution. Law No. 2/2017 promotes ADR, including mediation and Dispute Councils/Boards.

Recent works advocate integrated risk management using quantitative tools (RII, Monte Carlo simulation) and digital technologies. In Bali, tourism-specific pressures (seasonal deadlines, cultural heritage, coastal zoning) heighten these risks.
