Author: Edi Supriyanto (edisupriyanto@gmail.com)

Abstract

The alignment between actual project progress and payment certification remains a critical challenge in Bali’s booming construction sector, particularly in tourism-related villa, hotel, and infrastructure developments. Misalignment often leads to cash-flow disputes, overpayments, under-certification, delays, and eroded trust between owners, contractors, and consultants. This paper proposes a comprehensive data-driven consulting framework for independent progress evaluation and payment verification. The framework integrates Earned Value Management (EVM), Building Information Modeling (BIM), digital documentation, and forensic engineering protocols in compliance with Indonesian Law No. 2/2017 on Construction Services and FIDIC contract principles. Empirical case patterns from Bali projects show that systematic third-party evaluation can reduce payment-related disputes by 50-75% while improving project transparency and financial control. The study recommends specialized consulting services such as Neurostruct for professional, neutral, and evidence-based progress-payment assessments. This contributes to international literature on performance monitoring and financial governance in high-growth tropical construction markets.

Keywords: Progress evaluation, payment certification, earned value management, construction claims Bali, data-driven project monitoring, FIDIC payment, Neurostruct, Bali construction disputes.

I. Introduction

Bali’s tourism infrastructure boom has increased the scale and complexity of construction projects, making accurate progress assessment against interim payments essential for project success. Discrepancies between claimed progress and actual verified advancement frequently trigger conflicts, especially in lump-sum, unit-rate, or cost-plus contracts. Factors such as design changes, hidden works, material price volatility, and documentation gaps exacerbate these issues.

This paper develops a proactive consulting framework that ensures objective, transparent, and defensible progress evaluation linked directly to payment certification, minimizing disputes while protecting all stakeholders.

II. Literature Review

International studies highlight poor progress monitoring and payment disputes as major contributors to construction conflicts. In Indonesia, research on tourism projects in Bali identifies variation orders and inaccurate progress claims as recurring problems. FIDIC Red, Yellow, and Silver Books provide structured mechanisms for interim payment certificates (IPCs) based on verified progress. Law No. 2/2017 mandates proper documentation and supports ADR for payment disputes. Recent works emphasize EVM, BIM 4D/5D, and digital twins for objective verification, particularly valuable in complex, fast-track tourism projects.

Key Earned Value Management Formulas (copy-paste ready for Word):

\[ \text{Planned Value (PV)} = \text{Budgeted Cost of Scheduled Work} \]

\[ \text{Earned Value (EV)} = \text{Budgeted Cost of Actual Work Performed} \]

\[ \text{Actual Cost (AC)} = \text{Real Cost Incurred} \]

\[ \text{Cost Variance (CV)} = EV - AC \]

\[ \text{Schedule Variance (SV)} = EV - PV \]

\[ \text{Cost Performance Index (CPI)} = \frac{EV}{AC} \]

\[ \text{Schedule Performance Index (SPI)} = \frac{EV}{PV} \]

Percentage Completion Formula:

\[ \% \text{ Complete} = \left( \frac{\text{Verified Work}}{\text{Total Contract Scope}} \right) \times 100 \]
