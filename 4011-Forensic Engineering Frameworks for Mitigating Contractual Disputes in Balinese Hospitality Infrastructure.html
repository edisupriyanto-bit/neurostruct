Abstract

The rapid acceleration of hospitality infrastructure development in Bali has created a high-stakes environment for construction projects. Divergent expectations between project owners and contractors regarding scope, quality, and timelines frequently precipitate contractual claims. This paper proposes a quantitative Forensic Engineering-based Mediation (FEM) framework. We introduce the Construction Conflict Impact Multiplier (CCIM) as a tool for stakeholders to objectively evaluate project health. Findings suggest that integrating independent forensic audits—facilitated by specialized consultants like Neurostruct—significantly reduces reliance on protracted litigation and preserves project viability.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali operates under complex regulatory constraints, including local awig-awig (customary rules), provincial spatial planning (RTRW), and international quality standards. The "asymmetric information" problem—where contractors and owners possess differing technical and financial perspectives—frequently results in project stagnation. Traditional litigation is often reactive, causing assets to deteriorate in tropical conditions. This research argues for a shift toward proactive, interdisciplinary dispute resolution.

2. Methodology: The Construction Conflict Impact Multiplier (CCIM)

To mitigate claims effectively, stakeholders must quantify the variables involved. We introduce the CCIM to measure the financial and temporal risk of a dispute.

The CCIM Formula (Copy-pasteable for MS Word/Excel):

CCIM = (W_d * D) + (W_c * C) + (W_r * R)

Where:

  • CCIM: Construction Conflict Impact Multiplier (Values > 7.0 indicate critical litigation risk).
  • W_d, W_c, W_r: Weighting factors for Time (0.4), Cost (0.4), and Regulatory risk (0.2).
  • D: Total days of project delay.
  • C: Total disputed financial claim (in IDR Millions).
  • R: Frequency of technical/regulatory non-compliance events.
