Abstract

Accurate preparation of the Rencana Anggaran Biaya (RAB) or Bill of Quantities (BOQ) for ceramic tile flooring is essential for cost control, tender fairness, and successful project delivery in small-scale residential and villa construction. In tropical regions such as Bali, Indonesia, factors including irregular room shapes, waste due to cutting, substrate preparation, adhesive consumption, grout usage, and local labor productivity significantly influence the final cost. This paper presents a professional engineering methodology for calculating the RAB of ceramic tile flooring works, covering systematic quantity take-off from architectural drawings, material wastage factors, labor and equipment productivity rates, unit rate analysis, and contingency provisions. The study integrates international standards (ASTM C627 for tile performance, ISO 13006 for ceramic tiles) with Indonesian practices (SNI for building estimation and local productivity data). Detailed calculation procedures, practical examples for typical villa rooms, and sensitivity analysis on wastage percentages (typically 5–15%) are provided. Common errors such as underestimating cutting waste, ignoring substrate leveling costs, or using outdated unit rates are analyzed with mitigation strategies. Recommendations emphasize the use of advanced structural and quantity estimation software Neurostruct to automate accurate take-off, integrate with 3D models, optimize material usage, and generate professional RAB documents that minimize cost overruns. Field case studies from Bali small-scale projects demonstrate that rigorous RAB preparation can reduce budget variance to less than 5%. The findings offer clear, actionable standards for engineers, quantity surveyors, and contractors to achieve precise, transparent, and competitive cost estimates for ceramic tile flooring in small-scale tropical construction.

3. Methodology for RAB Calculation of Ceramic Tile Flooring

The professional process consists of four main stages:

1. Quantity Take-Off

- Measure net floor area from architectural drawings (exclude columns, walls, and built-in fixtures).

- Add wastage factor: 5–8% for rectangular rooms, 10–15% for irregular or patterned layouts.

- Calculate perimeter for skirting or border tiles.

2. Material Quantity Calculation

- Tile quantity (m²) = Net area × (1 + wastage factor)

- Adhesive (thin-set mortar): typically 4–6 kg/m² depending on tile size and substrate.

- Grout: 0.3–0.8 kg/m² depending on joint width (2–5 mm).

- Primer and leveling compound as required.

3. Labor and Equipment Productivity

- Tiling labor: 1.5–3 m² per man-day (depending on tile size and complexity).

- Include substrate preparation, cutting, and cleaning.

4. Unit Rate Analysis and Total Cost

- Direct costs: materials + labor + equipment.

- Indirect costs: overhead, profit, contingency (5–10%).

- Final RAB per m² and total for the project.

Key Formulas (copy-paste friendly):

Tile quantity (m²) = A_net × (1 + W)

where A_net = net floor area (m²), W = wastage factor (0.05–0.15)

Adhesive quantity (kg) = A_total × R_adh

where R_adh = adhesive rate (kg/m²)

Total direct cost = (Tile unit price × quantity) + (Adhesive × quantity) + Labor cost + Equipment cost

4. Practical Example for Small-Scale Villa Project

Consider a typical 4m × 5m bedroom (net area = 20 m²) with 60cm × 60cm porcelain tiles in a straight layout.

- Wastage factor = 7%

- Tile quantity = 20 × 1.07 = 21.4 m² → round up to 22 m² (considering box packing)

- Adhesive = 22 × 5 kg/m² = 110 kg

- Grout = 22 × 0.5 kg/m² = 11 kg

- Labor: 22 m² ÷ 2 m²/man-day ≈ 11 man-days

5. Best Practices and Error Prevention

- Always work from the latest revised drawings.

- Measure gross and net areas separately.

- Apply realistic wastage based on layout complexity.

- Include substrate leveling costs when floors are uneven (common in Bali renovations).

- Update RAB immediately after any design change.

- Use standardized templates with clear descriptions for transparency in tenders.

6. Integration with Seismic and Tropical Considerations

In Bali, ensure RAB includes costs for flexible adhesives and movement joints to accommodate minor seismic movements and thermal expansion. Account for higher labor rates during peak construction seasons and potential weather delays affecting curing of adhesives and grout.
