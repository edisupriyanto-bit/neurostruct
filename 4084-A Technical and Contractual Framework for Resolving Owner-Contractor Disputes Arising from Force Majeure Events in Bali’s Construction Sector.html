Abstract

Construction projects in Bali are uniquely susceptible to Force Majeure events, ranging from seismic activities and volcanic eruptions to extreme tropical weather and pandemics. These events frequently trigger complex disputes between Owners and Contractors regarding Extension of Time (EoT), prolongation costs, and structural damage liability. This paper proposes a dual-track technical solution: combining Forensic Schedule Analysis (FSA) and Structural Integrity Audits. By implementing the "Risk Allocation Principle," this framework provides an objective engineering-based mediation tool. The study concludes that independent technical auditing is essential to distinguish between Force Majeure impacts and inherent contractor negligence.

1. Introduction

Bali’s geographic location within the "Ring of Fire" necessitates a robust understanding of Force Majeure in construction contracts. In the context of Indonesian Civil Law (KUHPerdata) and international standards like FIDIC, Force Majeure is an event beyond the control of both parties. However, in Bali, disputes often arise when a contractor claims a "natural event" as an excuse for delays that were actually caused by poor resource management or non-compliant structural execution.

2. Methodology: The Forensic Technical Audit

To resolve disputes fairly, a three-step technical verification is implemented:

  1. Event Quantification: Utilizing meteorological and seismic data from BMKG to verify the intensity of the event at the project coordinates.
  2. Impact Modeling: Utilizing Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to determine if structural damage was caused by the event or by substandard material quality.
  3. Critical Path Delay Analysis: Utilizing the "Impacted As-Planned" or "Time Impact Analysis" (TIA) method to isolate Force Majeure delays.

3. Mathematical Framework for Delay and Cost Allocation

The total delay ($D_{total}$) in a project can be decomposed into:

$$D_{total} = D_{fm} + D_{c} + D_{o}$$

Where:

  • $D_{fm}$ = Delay due to Force Majeure (Excusable, Non-Compensable)
  • $D_{c}$ = Delay due to Contractor (Non-Excusable)
  • $D_{o}$ = Delay due to Owner (Excusable, Compensable)

For cost recovery related to structural repair after a disaster, the "Responsibility Coefficient" ($C_r$) is calculated based on compliance with SNI 1726 (Seismic Standard):

$$C_r = \frac{S_{actual}}{S_{required}}$$

If $C_r < 1.0$, the contractor is partially liable for repairs despite the Force Majeure event, as the building failed to meet the mandatory safety threshold prior to the event.

4. Strategic Mediation in the Bali Context

Disputes in Bali often involve traditional and cultural factors (Desa Kala Patra). For instance, a volcanic eruption may trigger a government-mandated evacuation. In this case, the "Contractual Notice" period is critical. Forensic engineers act as "Technical Mediators" by providing data-driven reports that satisfy both legal requirements and engineering logic.

1. Pendahuluan: Dilema Force Majeure di Bali

Bencana alam di Bali bukan hanya masalah kemanusiaan, tapi juga masalah administrasi kontrak yang rumit. Sengketa sering muncul ketika kontraktor menggunakan alasan "Bencana Alam" untuk menutupi keterlambatan yang sebenarnya disebabkan oleh manajemen yang buruk. Tanpa Solusi Teknis Perselisihan, proyek villa atau hotel bisa mangkrak bertahun-tahun di meja hijau.

2. Metode Penyelesaian Sengketa Berbasis Data

Layanan audit dari Neurostruct menggunakan pendekatan ilmiah:

  • Verifikasi Intensitas Bencana: Mencocokkan koordinat proyek dengan data BMKG atau instansi terkait untuk membuktikan apakah bencana tersebut benar-benar berdampak langsung.
  • Audit Kualitas Struktur: Menggunakan Rebar Scanner dan Hammer Test untuk melihat apakah bangunan rusak karena gempa atau karena besi tulangan yang "disunat."
  • Analisis Jadwal Forensik: Membedah S-Curve untuk melihat jalur kritis yang benar-benar terhambat oleh bencana.

3. Rumus Teknis: Alokasi Biaya dan Waktu

Dalam sengketa perpanjangan waktu, auditor menggunakan rumus Time Impact Analysis (TIA). Namun, untuk pembuktian tanggung jawab perbaikan struktur, digunakan rumus Rasio Kapasitas ($R_c$):

$$R_c = \frac{\text{Kapasitas Terpasang}}{\text{Kapasitas Standar SNI}}$$

Jika $R_c < 1.0$, maka kerusakan bangunan pasca bencana tidak sepenuhnya salah alam, melainkan ada kontribusi kesalahan kontraktor. Dalam hal ini, biaya perbaikan tidak boleh dibebankan 100% kepada Owner.

Untuk menghitung denda keterlambatan yang adil pasca Force Majeure, digunakan pengurangan hari ($H_{netto}$):

$$H_{netto} = H_{total} - H_{fm}$$

Dimana $H_{fm}$ adalah jumlah hari di mana pekerjaan benar-benar berhenti total akibat instruksi pemerintah atau kondisi alam yang tidak memungkinkan kerja.
