1. Introduction

Topographic survey is the process of determining the three-dimensional configuration of the earth’s surface, including natural features (hills, valleys, rivers) and man-made elements (buildings, roads, utilities). It produces detailed maps and models essential for every stage of construction—from feasibility studies and design to earthwork execution and as-built verification.

In modern construction, particularly for residential villas and buildings on undulating terrain in tropical regions like Bali, Indonesia, inaccurate topographic data leads to costly errors in cut and fill volumes, poor drainage, foundation settlement, and regulatory non-compliance. This paper explores the definition, methods, and often-overlooked applications of topographic surveys in construction, highlighting the transition from traditional total station surveys to advanced UAV photogrammetry and LiDAR technologies.

The review emphasizes practical benefits for contractors, including precise volume calculations for earthwork optimization and integration with BIM workflows. It aligns with international best practices and relevant Indonesian standards for land surveying and geotechnical investigations.

2. Literature Review

Conventional topographic surveys rely on total stations, GPS, and leveling instruments for ground-based data collection. Recent advancements integrate Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with photogrammetry and LiDAR for rapid, high-accuracy mapping over large or inaccessible areas. Studies demonstrate that UAV-LiDAR combinations can achieve vertical accuracy with RMSE as low as 0.075 m, outperforming traditional photogrammetry alone.

Research highlights the role of topographic surveys in infrastructure planning, earthwork quantity estimation, and sustainable land development. In tropical contexts, surveys must account for dense vegetation, rapid erosion, and seasonal flooding. Indonesian practice increasingly adopts digital methods to support SNI-compliant site investigations and detailed engineering designs for buildings and villas.

3. Theoretical Background and Survey Methods

# 3.1 Definition and Objectives

A topographic survey creates a detailed representation of terrain relief and features using contours, spot elevations, and break lines. Primary objectives include generating base maps for design, calculating earthwork volumes, planning drainage, and ensuring safe construction layouts.

# 3.2 Conventional Methods

- Total Station Survey: Combines electronic distance measurement and angle measurement for precise 3D coordinates.

- GPS/GNSS Survey: Provides absolute positioning with real-time kinematic (RTK) accuracy down to centimeters.

# 3.3 Modern Methods

- UAV Photogrammetry: Uses overlapping aerial images processed with Structure-from-Motion (SfM) algorithms to generate dense point clouds and orthomosaics.

- LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging): Emits laser pulses to measure distances, penetrating vegetation for bare-earth models.

# 3.4 Volume Calculation from Topographic Data

Common methods for cut and fill volumes:

Average End-Area Method (for linear features):

\[ V = \frac{A_1 + A_2}{2} \times L \]

Prismoidal Formula (higher accuracy):

\[ V = \frac{L}{6} (A_1 + 4A_m + A_2) \]

Grid Method (for areal sites):

Divide area into grids of side \( d \), compute average height difference \( h_{avg} \) at four corners, then cell volume = \( h_{avg} \times d^2 \). Sum all cells and apply swell/shrinkage factors.

5. Challenges and Best Practices in Tropical Environments

Tropical conditions in Bali present unique challenges: dense vegetation obscuring ground features, high rainfall causing erosion, and variable soil types affecting survey accuracy. Best practices include:

- Combining ground control points with UAV data for georeferencing

- Using LiDAR for vegetation penetration

- Conducting surveys during dry seasons when possible

- Verifying results with traditional methods for critical areas
