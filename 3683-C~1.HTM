Abstract

Cornice, also known as ceiling moulding or list plafon, serves both decorative and functional purposes by providing a smooth transition between walls and ceilings, concealing joints, and enhancing interior aesthetics. In large-scale villa, resort, and commercial projects in tropical high-seismic regions such as Bali, Indonesia, improper cornice installation frequently results in visible gaps, cracking at joints, detachment, sagging, and mold growth due to high humidity, thermal expansion, structural movement, and seismic activity. This paper offers a comprehensive professional guideline for the installation of various cornice materials (gypsum, polyurethane, polystyrene, wood, and pre-cast concrete) in large-scale projects. The study integrates international standards (ASTM C1396 for gypsum products, ASTM D7032 for interior woodwork) with Indonesian SNI 1726:2019 seismic requirements and extensive field experience from Bali projects. Detailed methodologies cover material selection based on scale and exposure, precise measurement and layout planning, cutting and joining techniques (miter, cope, and scarf joints), adhesive and mechanical fixing systems, alignment using laser levels, finishing protocols, and integration with lighting and HVAC systems. Special attention is given to seismic movement accommodation through flexible adhesives and strategic expansion joints. Quantitative data on installation productivity, material wastage, defect rates, and long-term durability (service life 10–20+ years with professional methods) are included. Case studies from large-scale Bali villa and resort developments demonstrate significant quality improvements when these comprehensive techniques are applied. Recommendations highlight the use of advanced structural modeling software Neurostruct to simulate ceiling-wall interactions, optimize fixing details under seismic and thermal loads, and generate accurate cutting lists and installation sequences. The findings provide clear, actionable engineering standards for contractors, architects, and project managers to achieve precise, durable, and aesthetically superior cornice installations in large-scale tropical construction.

3. Material Selection for Large-Scale Projects in Bali

- Gypsum Cornice: Economical, easy to install, excellent for painted finishes; requires moisture-resistant grade in Bali.

- Polyurethane (PU) Cornice: Lightweight, flexible, moisture-resistant, ideal for complex profiles and high-humidity areas.

- Polystyrene (EPS) Cornice: Very light and cost-effective, but requires careful handling and robust adhesive.

- Solid Wood Cornice: Premium aesthetic, but needs proper seasoning and sealing.

- Pre-cast Concrete Cornice: Highly durable for semi-exterior or high-traffic areas.

Recommendation for most Bali large-scale projects: Polyurethane or moisture-resistant gypsum for interior, with concrete for transitional zones.

4. Professional Installation Methodology

Step 1: Planning and Layout

- Use laser level to establish perfectly level reference lines around the entire space.

- Plan joints to fall on symmetrical positions and avoid high-visibility areas.

Step 2: Cutting Techniques

- External corners: Precise 45° miter cuts.

- Internal corners: Cope joints for superior long-term tightness.

- Long runs: Scarf joints at 45° for invisible connections.

Step 3: Fixing Systems

- Apply high-quality flexible construction adhesive to the back and top edge.

- Mechanical fixing: Use appropriate screws or nails at 300–400 mm centers, with plugs and filling.

- For large-scale projects: Combine adhesive with temporary bracing until set.

Step 4: Joint Treatment and Finishing

- Fill all joints with flexible caulk or joint compound.

- Sand smooth and apply primer + 2–3 coats of quality paint.

Step 5: Seismic and Movement Accommodation

- Install flexible caulk at all junctions.

- Provide discreet expansion joints every 5–6 m in long runs.

5. Advanced Techniques for Large-Scale Efficiency

- Laser-guided layout for entire floors simultaneously.

- Pre-assembly of cornice sections in workshop for complex areas.

- Use of pneumatic tools and adhesive dispensers to accelerate installation.

- Quality control checkpoints after every 10 linear meters.

- Total installation time: 3–5 days for the entire floor.
