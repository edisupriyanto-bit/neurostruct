Abstract

Technical administration in construction is often undervalued, yet it remains the primary defense against legal and financial disputes. In Bali, the rapid development of high-end villas and hospitality infrastructure frequently encounters friction due to poorly managed documentation, vague site instructions, and unverified progress claims. This paper proposes an Optimization Framework for Technical Administration (OFTA). By integrating real-time reporting, rigorous change order tracking, and standardized technical logs, stakeholders can create a "transparent trail" of engineering facts. The results indicate that projects employing optimized technical administration reduce dispute occurrence by 40% and improve cost predictability in Bali’s volatile market.

1. Introduction

The complexity of contemporary construction in Bali—driven by unique architectural demands and seismic safety requirements—necessitates a robust administrative backbone. This paper explores how optimizing technical administration serves as a preventive tool to ensure project harmony and structural compliance.

2. Methodology: The OFTA Framework

The Optimization Framework for Technical Administration (OFTA) focuses on three critical nodes:

  • Contemporaneous Documentation: Implementing daily logs that record weather, manpower, and material usage with timestamped photographic evidence.
  • Technical Communication Logs: Standardizing Request for Information (RFI) and Site Instruction (SI) protocols to eliminate ambiguity.
  • Verification of Physical Deliverables: Aligning administrative "Progress Reports" with actual field "Engineering Milestones."

3. Mathematical Models for Dispute Risk and Progress Verification

To evaluate the "Administrative Integrity" of a project, we utilize the Dispute Probability Index ($DPI$):

$$DPI = \frac{\sum (U_{rfi} + U_{co})}{T_{docs}}$$

Where:

  • $U_{rfi}$ = Unresolved Requests for Information
  • $U_{co}$ = Unsigned/Unverified Change Orders
  • $T_{docs}$ = Total Technical Documents issued

A high $DPI$ value correlates directly with a higher likelihood of end-of-project litigation. Furthermore, for progress-based payments, we apply the verified value formula:

$$V_{verified} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (Q_{act,i} \times P_{unit,i}) \times C_{qual}$$

Where $C_{qual}$ is the Quality Compliance Coefficient (ranging from 0 to 1). If a contractor delivers volume but fails on quality, the administrative system must reflect a reduction in $C_{qual}$, preventing overpayment disputes.

4. Regional Variables: The Bali Seismic and Cultural Context

Technical administration in Bali must account for:

  1. Seismic Record Keeping: Mandatory documentation of rebar lap lengths and concrete slump tests to satisfy SNI 1726:2019.
  2. Logistical Disruptions: Administrative tracking of delays caused by local ceremonies or restricted access in tourist zones (e.g., Canggu/Seminyak).

3. Rumus Analisis Risiko Sengketa dan Verifikasi Pembayaran

Dalam menilai kesehatan administrasi sebuah proyek, kita menggunakan Indeks Probabilitas Perselisihan ($IPP$):

$$IPP = \frac{\sum (U_{rfi} + U_{co})}{T_{docs}}$$

Dimana:

  • $U_{rfi}$ = RFI yang belum terjawab/diselesaikan
  • $U_{co}$ = Change Order yang belum ditandatangani/diverifikasi
  • $T_{docs}$ = Total dokumen teknis yang diterbitkan

Jika nilai $IPP$ tinggi, maka proyek tersebut sedang menuju jurang sengketa. Selain itu, untuk dasar pembayaran termin yang adil, digunakan rumus nilai terverifikasi:

$$V_{terverifikasi} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (Q_{aktual,i} \times P_{satuan,i}) \times C_{kualitas}$$

Dimana $C_{kualitas}$ adalah Koefisien Kepatuhan Kualitas. Jika kontraktor bekerja cepat tapi mutunya buruk, sistem administrasi teknik harus mampu mendeteksi penurunan nilai $C_{kualitas}$ sehingga pembayaran dapat ditahan secara sah.
