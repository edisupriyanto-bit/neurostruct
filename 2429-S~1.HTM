2. Literature Review

Numerous studies in *Construction and Building Materials* and *Journal of Building Engineering* confirm that more than 70% of waterproofing failures are directly attributable to poor surface preparation. Key factors include laitance, dust, oil contamination, moisture content, and insufficient surface roughness (profile).

International standards such as ASTM D4258 (Standard Practice for Surface Cleaning of Concrete) and ICRI guidelines emphasize the importance of achieving a Concrete Surface Profile (CSP) between CSP 3 and CSP 6 for most liquid-applied waterproofing systems. In tropical environments, high humidity and rapid drying make moisture control particularly challenging.

3. Common Surface Preparation Issues in Bali

- Presence of laitance and weak surface layer

- Residual form release agents and curing compounds

- Dust, dirt, and efflorescence

- High internal moisture content in concrete

- Cracks, honeycombing, and voids

- Inadequate surface roughness for mechanical bonding

These issues are exacerbated by Bali’s high humidity and frequent rainfall, which slow down drying and increase contamination risk.

4. Step-by-Step Surface Preparation Techniques

# 4.1 Initial Assessment

- Visual inspection and hammer sounding test

- Moisture content measurement (should be < 5% for most systems)

- Surface strength testing (pull-off test)

# 4.2 Surface Cleaning and Profiling

- Mechanical methods: shot blasting, grinding, scarifying, or high-pressure water jetting

- Chemical cleaning for oil and grease removal

- Achieving target Concrete Surface Profile (CSP 3–5)

# 4.3 Repair of Defects

- Injection or routing and sealing of cracks

- Patching of honeycombing with polymer-modified mortar
