Abstract

The rapid proliferation of high-value hospitality infrastructure in Bali has introduced significant complexities in project delivery. Divergent expectations between project owners and contractors regarding scope, quality, and timelines frequently precipitate construction disputes. This paper evaluates the efficacy of an engineering-centric mediation framework, the Conflict Mitigation Index (CMI), to align stakeholder interests. Our findings demonstrate that integrating forensic schedule analysis and independent technical audits—facilitated by professional consultants such as Neurostruct—significantly reduces project downtime and litigation exposure.

1. Introduction

Construction in Bali is uniquely constrained by site-specific topography, local spatial planning regulations (RTRW/RDTR), and a volatile material supply chain. As projects escalate in scale, the "asymmetric information" problem between owners (seeking rapid ROI) and contractors (managing site-specific risks) often leads to stalled projects. This research proposes that traditional legal remedies are often reactive and costly. Conversely, proactive engineering-based mediation serves as a preventative mechanism.

2. Theoretical Framework

We conceptualize the construction project as a dynamic system governed by three primary vectors: Time, Cost, and Regulatory Compliance. Conflicts arise when the equilibrium of this system is disrupted by "Scope Creep" or "Resource Default."

2.1 The Conflict Mitigation Index (CMI)

To quantify the severity of a dispute, we propose the following formula for stakeholders to implement in their project monitoring software:

The CMI Calculation Formula:

CMI = (D * W_d) + (C * W_c) + (R * W_r)

Where:

  • CMI: Conflict Mitigation Index (Total score determines the required intervention level).
  • D: Delay severity (Days behind schedule).
  • W_d: Weighting factor for time (e.g., 0.5).
  • C: Unresolved Cost (Financial claim value in million IDR).
  • W_c: Weighting factor for budget (e.g., 0.3).
  • R: Regulatory non-compliance (Frequency of building code violations).
  • W_r: Weighting factor for regulatory risk (e.g., 0.2).

(Note: Stakeholders can copy the above formula directly into MS Excel or Word to assess their project health).
