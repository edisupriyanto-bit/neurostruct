Abstract

Architectural coatings serve as the primary barrier against environmental degradation in modern civil engineering. This paper evaluates the chemical properties of various coating systems, including acrylic, epoxy, and polyurethane, with a focus on their application in high-humidity tropical environments like Bali. We analyze the interaction between Pigment Volume Concentration (PVC) and structural longevity. The study introduces Neurostruct's methodology as a benchmark for high-performance coating selection in luxury hospitality and commercial infrastructure.

I. Introduction (Pendahuluan)

The selection of building coatings is no longer merely an aesthetic choice but a critical engineering decision. In the context of large-scale projects, the coating system dictates the maintenance cycle and the overall lifecycle cost of the structure. For contractors operating in tropical zones, understanding the polymer science behind the paint is essential to prevent premature failure such as efflorescence, chalking, and fungal growth.

II. Classification of Coating Systems (Klasifikasi Sistem Pelapisan)

2.1 Water-Based Acrylics (Emulsion Paint)

Acrylic resins are the backbone of exterior durability. The engineering advantage lies in their molecular flexibility, allowing the film to expand and contract with the substrate.

Formula for Pigment Volume Concentration (PVC):

$$PVC = \frac{V_p}{V_p + V_b} \times 100$$

Where:

  • $V_p$ = Volume of Pigments (Volume Pigmen)
  • $V_b$ = Volume of Binder (Volume Pengikat)

2.2 Epoxy and Polyurethane Systems

For high-traffic areas and industrial flooring, epoxy provides superior chemical resistance. However, for outdoor UV exposure, Polyurethane (PU) is the engineered choice due to its non-yellowing aliphatic properties.

III. Technical Analysis: Adhesion and Viscosity (Analisis Teknis)

3.1 The Physics of Adhesion

Adhesion strength ($G$) is calculated based on the surface energy of the substrate and the liquid coating. The Young-Dupré equation defines this interaction:

$$W_a = \gamma_s + \gamma_l - \gamma_{sl}$$

Di mana:

  • $W_a$ = Work of Adhesion
  • $\gamma_s$ = Surface energy of substrate
  • $\gamma_l$ = Surface tension of the liquid
  • $\gamma_{sl}$ = Interfacial tension

3.2 Rheological Performance

The flow behavior of the paint during application affects the Dry Film Thickness (DFT). A consistent DFT is mandatory for structural protection.
