Abstract

Gypsum board ceilings are widely used in residential, villa, and commercial construction due to their smooth finish, lightweight properties, and rapid installation. However, in tropical climates such as Bali, Indonesia, gypsum ceilings frequently experience cracking due to high humidity, thermal expansion, structural movement, poor joint treatment, and improper installation. These defects not only compromise aesthetics but also reduce structural integrity and increase maintenance costs.

This paper presents a comprehensive engineering review and practical framework for the assessment, repair, and long-term rehabilitation of cracked gypsum board ceilings in accordance with relevant SNI standards. The study analyzes common failure mechanisms, including shrinkage cracking, joint failure, sagging, and moisture-induced damage. Various repair techniques — from simple patching to full reinforcement and replacement — are evaluated for effectiveness, durability, and cost-efficiency.

The Neurostruct framework is introduced as an integrated, performance-based methodology that combines systematic damage assessment, root cause analysis, appropriate repair techniques, moisture control measures, and preventive detailing tailored for tropical conditions. Numerical examples with copy-pasteable equations for deflection and stress analysis, along with conceptual repair diagrams, are provided. Recommendations align with SNI 03-6389-2000, SNI 7973:2013, and international best practices (ASTM C840, ASTM C1396). 2. Literature Review

Research in *Construction and Building Materials* and *Journal of Building Engineering* indicates that gypsum board cracking is primarily caused by differential movement between the board and supporting structure, improper joint treatment, and moisture fluctuations. In tropical regions, high relative humidity (often >80%) significantly reduces gypsum board strength and increases sag potential.

Indonesian National Standards (SNI 03-6389-2000 and SNI 7973:2013) provide guidelines for gypsum board installation and joint treatment. However, field application in Bali frequently deviates from these standards, resulting in premature failure.

3. Common Causes of Gypsum Ceiling Cracks in Bali

- Thermal and moisture-induced movement of roof structure

- Inadequate framing spacing or deflection of supporting members

- Poor joint tape and compound application

- Vibration from foot traffic or wind

- Water leakage from roof or AC condensation

- Improper screw spacing and edge support

4. Engineering Assessment and Repair Techniques

# 4.1 Damage Assessment

- Visual inspection and crack mapping

- Moisture content measurement

- Structural deflection check of ceiling frame

# 4.2 Repair Methods

1. Minor Hairline Cracks: Skim coating with flexible compound

2. Moderate Cracks: Remove damaged tape, re-tape with fiberglass mesh, and apply multiple coats

3. Severe Damage/Sagging: Partial or full replacement with reinforced gypsum board and improved framing
