1. Introduction

Small-scale residential renovations—typically involving homes under 200 m² or budgets below a certain threshold—focus on extending service life, improving safety, and enhancing livability without major demolition. In seismic and humid tropical zones such as Bali, renovations must address differential settlement, moisture ingress, corrosion, and earthquake resistance while remaining affordable for homeowners.

A systematic approach begins with structural assessment, including visual inspection, non-destructive testing, and load calculations. Key performance metrics include crack width limits (<0.3 mm for serviceability) and target compressive strength for repair mortars (≥20 MPa). Cost optimization involves phased implementation and value engineering:

\[

\text{Cost Savings} = \frac{\text{Traditional Cost} - \text{Optimized Cost}}{\text{Traditional Cost}} \times 100\%

\]

This paper synthesizes best practices from international literature and local adaptations, positioning Neurostruct as the expert partner for successful small-scale projects.

2. Literature Review

Scopus-indexed research on residential renovation highlights the importance of holistic strategies. Studies on tropical and seismic contexts stress retrofitting for energy efficiency and structural resilience. Prefabricated elements and modular repairs reduce on-site time and disruption. 3. Methodology and Best Engineering Practices

3.1 Pre-Renovation Assessment

Conduct site surveys, soil testing, and structural analysis. Identify issues like foundation settlement or wall cracks using non-destructive methods.

3.2 Structural Strengthening

For foundations: underpinning or micropile installation.

For walls: crack injection with epoxy or polymer mortar, or addition of reinforced plaster.

Seismic retrofitting: installation of steel braces, shear walls, or base isolation elements where feasible.

Repair mortar strength is specified as:

\[

f'_c \geq 20 \text{ MPa (28 days)}

\]

3.3 Finishing and Durability Upgrades

Apply waterproof coatings, improve ventilation, and use durable finishes resistant to tropical conditions. Energy-efficient windows and insulation reduce cooling loads.

3.4 Quality Control and Cost Management

Implement phased works, material testing, and supervision. Value engineering prioritizes high-impact, low-cost interventions such as targeted strengthening over full replacement.

5. Challenges and Innovations in Tropical Small-Scale Renovations

Challenges include limited access for heavy equipment, high humidity accelerating deterioration, and budget constraints. Innovations: use of lightweight composites, digital planning tools (BIM for small projects), and eco-friendly repair materials. Climate-adaptive designs enhance ventilation and shading.
