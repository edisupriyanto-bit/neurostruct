3. Theoretical Background and Design Principles

# 3.1 Formwork Pressure Calculation

Fresh concrete exerts lateral pressure on formwork. For columns or walls, the maximum pressure \( p_{max} \) can be estimated as:

\[ p_{max} = C_w C_c \left[ 150 + \frac{9000 R}{T} \right] \] (ACI 347 simplified, in kPa)

where \( C_w \) is unit weight coefficient, \( C_c \) is chemistry coefficient, \( R \) is rate of placement (m/h), and \( T \) is temperature (°C). For strip footings (shallow and slow placement), hydrostatic pressure \( p = \rho g h \) is often sufficient, with \( \rho \) ≈ 2400 kg/m³ for normal concrete.

# 3.2 Bracing and Stability

Formwork must resist lateral loads from concrete pressure, wind, and construction activities. Tie rods or braces are spaced based on calculated pressure and allowable deflection (typically L/360 or 3–6 mm).

# 3.3 Tolerances and Concrete Cover

Per SNI 2847:2019, formwork alignment tolerances are strict to ensure proper concrete cover (minimum 40–75 mm for foundations depending on exposure).

4. Cost-Effective Formwork Techniques for Continuous Strip Footings

# 4.1 Material Options and Comparison

- Traditional Timber: Low initial cost, easy to cut on site, but limited reuse (3–5 cycles), high waste, and labor-intensive.

- Steel Panel Systems: High initial investment but 50–100+ reuse cycles, excellent dimensional accuracy, and fast assembly.

- Modular Plastic or Composite Panels: Lightweight, corrosion-resistant, suitable for humid tropical conditions, moderate cost with good reusability.

- Hybrid Approach: Timber for irregular sections combined with reusable steel for straight runs—common cost-effective solution in Bali.

# 4.2 Design and Assembly Procedures

1. Site Preparation: Level and compact subgrade, install blinding concrete (50 mm) for even support.

2. Formwork Layout: Mark centerlines and widths according to structural drawings. Use adjustable width forms for flexibility.

3. Bracing System: Install strongbacks, walers, and ties at calculated spacing. For shallow footings (<1.2 m height), external stakes and diagonal braces suffice.

4. Waterproofing Integration: Apply release agent and install waterproof membrane or damp-proof course where required.

5. Concreting: Place concrete in layers with proper vibration to avoid honeycombing. Maintain pour rate to control pressure.

6. Stripping and Curing: Remove forms after 24–48 hours (sides) while protecting edges. Apply moist curing immediately.

# 4.3 Cost Optimization Strategies

- Maximize reuse of panels across multiple footings.

- Standardize footing widths to reduce custom cutting.

- Use prefabricated formwork for repetitive villa units.

- Implement just-in-time material delivery to minimize storage damage in humid conditions.

Typical cost breakdown shows that reusable systems can reduce formwork cost per m³ of concrete by 25–40% after 10–15 reuse cycles.
