1. Introduction

Bale Bengong is a lightweight, open-sided pavilion that serves as a relaxing space in Balinese house compounds, temple grounds, and modern villa gardens. Its elegant form—raised platform, slender columns, and steeply pitched roof—defines much of Bali’s architectural identity.

Contemporary construction demands that these structures meet modern safety standards, particularly seismic resistance, while retaining their cultural authenticity and aesthetic appeal. Contractors in Bali frequently encounter challenges when scaling traditional Bale Bengong for larger villas or tourism projects, including compliance with SNI seismic codes, durability in high-humidity conditions, and cost efficiency.

This paper reviews traditional construction methods and proposes practical modern engineering adaptations, including foundation design, structural framing, roofing systems, and detailing. The goal is to equip contractors with technically sound, cost-effective, and culturally respectful approaches for building Bale Bengong in today’s context.

2. Literature Review

Research on Balinese vernacular architecture highlights the lightweight nature and flexible joinery of traditional structures, which provide inherent ductility during moderate earthquakes. However, larger or modified pavilions require engineered reinforcement to satisfy current code requirements.

Scopus-indexed studies on hybrid construction systems discuss the integration of traditional timber framing with modern steel connectors, base isolation concepts, and improved moisture barriers. Papers on tropical building performance emphasize the importance of adequate roof overhangs, ventilation, and material treatments to combat humidity, termites, and UV degradation.

Indonesian standards (SNI 1726:2019 and SNI 2847:2019) mandate specific seismic detailing for non-engineered and hybrid structures. Local case studies from Bali villa developments show that well-executed hybrid systems successfully combine visual lightness with enhanced structural safety and longevity.

3. Theoretical Background and Design Principles

# 3.1 Structural Behavior

Traditional Bale Bengong relies on low mass and pinned-type connections for flexibility. Modern designs enhance this with capacity design principles, ensuring ductile behavior and controlled energy dissipation.

# 3.2 Seismic Detailing

Columns and beams should follow strong-column weak-beam hierarchy where possible. Connections must accommodate movement using ductile steel plates or brackets while preserving traditional appearance.

Base shear is calculated according to SNI 1726 response spectrum, with appropriate importance and soil factors for pavilion structures.

# 3.3 Durability in Tropical Climate

High humidity and rainfall require breathable construction, effective roof overhangs (minimum 1.0–1.5 m), and protective treatments for organic materials. Termite barriers and elevated platforms remain essential.

4. Modern Construction Techniques for Bale Bengong

# 4.1 Foundation System

Reinforced concrete strip or pad footings designed per SNI 2847:2019 and SNI 8460:2017. Raised platform (panggung) on concrete or stone pedestals with damp-proof course and termite shielding.

# 4.2 Structural Framing

Hybrid system combining traditional teak or coconut wood columns and beams with hidden steel connectors or engineered bamboo composites for improved strength and ductility. Use bolted or pinned connections with seismic washers for flexibility.

# 4.3 Roofing System

Traditional thatched (alang-alang or ijuk) or wooden shingle roofs over lightweight purlins and rafters. Modern alternatives include composite thatch panels or metal roofing with traditional aesthetic overlays. Maintain steep pitch (45°–60°) for rapid drainage and natural ventilation.

# 4.4 Joinery and Aesthetic Detailing

Preserve traditional mortise-and-tenon or lashed appearances while reinforcing critical joints with concealed steel plates. Carvings and decorative elements remain non-structural.

# 4.5 Construction Sequence

1. Site survey and layout respecting traditional proportions.

2. Foundation casting with proper curing.

3. Erection of main frame with temporary bracing.

4. Installation of roof structure and covering.

5. Application of protective finishes and final detailing.

6. Quality inspection for alignment, stability, and weather-tightness.

5. Applications and Case Considerations in Bali

In Bali’s villa and tourism projects, Bale Bengong functions as garden pavilions, poolside gazebos, or outdoor seating areas. Hybrid techniques allow larger spans while preserving the characteristic lightweight and open appearance. Practical experience shows that SNI-compliant foundations and connections markedly improve seismic performance compared with purely traditional construction, especially on sloping or variable soil sites.

Common challenges include material sourcing and artisan training, which can be addressed through collaborative workshops between engineers and local craftsmen.
