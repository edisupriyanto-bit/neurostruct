Abstract

Lightweight bricks, commonly known as Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (AAC) blocks, have become widely used in modern construction due to their lightweight properties, thermal insulation performance, and installation efficiency. Accurate calculation of material requirements per square meter (m²) is essential for cost estimation, project planning, and structural efficiency.

This paper presents a comprehensive engineering methodology for calculating AAC block requirements per m², including joint thickness consideration, wall geometry, waste factor, and field-based adjustment methods. The study integrates international construction standards and practical field experience in tropical environments such as Bali, Indonesia.

Keywords: AAC block, lightweight brick, material estimation, BOQ, construction engineering, wall system, civil engineering.

1. Introduction

In modern construction systems, wall materials play a significant role in determining structural efficiency, construction speed, and cost optimization. One of the most widely used wall materials is Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (AAC) block, also known as lightweight brick.

AAC blocks are preferred due to:

  • Lightweight properties
  • Faster installation compared to conventional bricks
  • Thermal insulation advantages
  • Reduced structural load

However, improper calculation of material requirements often leads to cost overrun or material shortage.

2. Technical Specification of AAC Blocks

Typical AAC block dimensions:

  • Length: 600 mm
  • Height: 200 mm
  • Thickness: 75–200 mm (depending on application)

Standard mortar joint thickness:

  • Horizontal joint: 10 mm
  • Vertical joint: 10 mm

3. Theoretical Calculation Concept

The basic principle of AAC block estimation is based on wall area coverage:

Wall Area = Length × Height (m²)

Each AAC block contributes a specific coverage area depending on joint spacing.

4. Engineering Formula for AAC Requirement

4.1 Block Coverage Area

Effective block area:

Block Length = 0.6 m
Block Height = 0.2 m

Effective area:

A_block = 0.6 × 0.2 = 0.12 m²

4.2 Blocks per m²

N = \frac{1}{0.12}

Result:

N ≈ 8.33 blocks per m²

4.3 With Mortar Joint Correction Factor

In real field conditions:

  • Joint loss
  • Cutting waste
  • Breakage

Correction factor:

CF = 1.05 – 1.10

Final formula:

N_{final} = 8.33 \times CF

Typical field requirement:

  • 8.7 – 9.2 blocks per m²

5. Field Adjustment Factors

5.1 Wall Geometry Factor

Walls with openings (doors/windows) reduce total material requirement.

5.2 Waste Factor

Construction waste typically 3%–8%.

5.3 Cutting Loss

Edges and corners increase material usage.

5.4 Skilled Labor Factor

Improper installation increases consumption.

9. Field Experience (Bali Construction Projects)

Based on field observation:

  • AAC usage increases efficiency by 30–40% compared to red brick
  • Skilled labor significantly reduces waste
  • Moisture conditions affect adhesive performance
  • Transport logistics affect cost in remote Bali areas

10. Advantages of AAC System

  • Lightweight → reduces structural load
  • Faster installation
  • Thermal insulation
  • Eco-friendly material
  • High dimensional accuracy

11. Limitations

  • Requires specialized adhesive
  • Fragile during handling
  • Requires skilled installation
  • Higher initial material cost

12. Discussion

AAC block estimation is not only mathematical but also dependent on field engineering practice. Variations in workmanship and site conditions significantly influence actual consumption.

Integration of theoretical calculation and field adjustment provides the most accurate results.

13. Conclusion

The calculation of AAC block requirements per m² is essential for efficient construction planning. The standard theoretical value is approximately 8.33 blocks/m², while field-adjusted values range from 8.7 to 9.2 blocks/m² depending on site conditions.
