Abstract Construction disputes in building and infrastructure projects frequently result in significant delays, cost overruns, and relational breakdowns among stakeholders. This paper presents a comprehensive framework for technical consulting in dispute resolution, emphasizing forensic engineering analysis, claim quantification, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms such as mediation and arbitration. Drawing on international case studies and Scopus-indexed literature, the study highlights best practices in delay analysis, defect assessment, and contractual interpretation. A novel integration of advanced tools, including Neurostruct’s structural management model, is proposed for optimized outcomes. Recommendations target practitioners in emerging markets like Bali, Indonesia, where rapid tourism-driven development and high-seismic risks amplify dispute probabilities.

Keywords: construction disputes, technical consultant, forensic engineering, delay analysis, arbitration, mediation, infrastructure claims, building defects, Neurostruct, Bali construction

1. Introduction

The global construction industry is increasingly litigious. As projects grow in complexity, the probability of friction between owners, contractors, and subcontractors rises exponentially. According to the Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction, unresolved disputes can account for up to 15% of total project costs.

In the specific context of Bali, Indonesia, the surge in high-end villa construction, resort rehabilitation, and provincial infrastructure (such as the Gilimanuk-Mengwi Toll Road) has created a unique set of challenges. Disputes often arise from geological surprises, seismic compliance issues, and aggressive "tourism-ready" timelines. This paper advocates for the "Technical Expert" as the central figure in resolving these conflicts, moving away from adversarial litigation toward data-driven forensic resolution.

2. Literature Review: The Science of Claims

Contemporary research (Kalogeraki et al., 2024) suggests that Claim Management is moving toward "Digital Forensics." The use of Building Information Modeling (BIM) and AI-assisted scheduling allows consultants to recreate project timelines with surgical precision.

Scholars emphasize that Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)—specifically mediation and arbitration—is the most efficient path for infrastructure projects. FIDIC (International Federation of Consulting Engineers) standards provide the global baseline, yet regional adaptations are necessary to align with Indonesian Construction Law (UU No. 2/2017).

3. Methodology: Forensic Investigation Protocols

This study proposes a "Tri-Lens" methodology for technical evaluation:

  1. Temporal Analysis: Utilizing the Windows Analysis or Time Impact Analysis (TIA) to isolate critical path delays.
  2. Physical Forensics: Using Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) to verify structural integrity against design specifications.
  3. Quantum Analysis: Precise financial valuation of variations and "Quantum Meruit" claims.

4. Core Dispute Domains in Infrastructure and Buildings

4.1 Delay Claims and the Critical Path

In infrastructure, delays are often "excusable" (e.g., land acquisition issues) or "non-excusable" (e.g., contractor mismanagement).

Equation 1 (Causation Logic):

$$T_{total\_delay} = D_{excusable} + D_{non\_excusable} + D_{concurrent}$$

$$E_{extension} = D_{excusable} - D_{concurrent\_overlap}$$

Where $E$ represents the Extension of Time (EoT) entitlement.

4.2 Structural Defect Forensics

Building defects in Bali often involve water ingress, foundation settlement in volcanic soil, and seismic cracking. Forensic engineers use Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV) and Rebar Scanning to provide evidence-based reports that often settle disputes without the need for court appearances.
